\begin{abstract}
Vehicles may supply calibration data from narrow speed regions while the
fleet collectively covers a wider interval. Pooling can recover missing
scheduling information but mix incompatible dynamics. We study federated
linear-parameter-varying (LPV) identification under these joint limitations.
Clients exchange additive regression statistics and assignment scores to learn
shared group maps. Across ten nonlinear vehicle fleets with family labels
withheld, grouped fitting reduces mean paired full-envelope matrix error by
\SmoothLocalFullGain\% relative to development-tuned Smooth Local and
\IdentGlobalGain\% relative to Global. Mean error at locally unobserved speeds
is \PrimaryFedUnseenError\%, versus \SmoothLocalUnseenError\% and
\PrimaryGlobalUnseenError\%, respectively. The distributed fit reproduces
centralized grouping. Benefits persist under alternative entry scaling but
are not uniform across entries or clients. The benchmark uses controlled
equilibrium-centered calibration with available states, exact current
regressors, and noisy next-state targets. Fresh stress tests expose fleet-wide
coverage failure and loss of grouping gains at lower calibration signal-to-noise.
Speed-scheduled linear-quadratic-integral (LQI) tests show modest gains over
Global; the tested Smooth Local prior yields infeasible responses for some
clients under the frozen controller protocol.
\end{abstract}

federated identification, LPV systems, incomplete operating coverage,

\section{Introduction}
Vehicles with speed-dependent and heterogeneous dynamics may observe only
narrow speed regions. LPV identification then requires complementary
scheduling information while avoiding bias from pooling incompatible dynamics.

Basis-dependent LPV identification requires scheduling excitation
\cite{toth2010lpv,bamieh2002}. Local approaches identify fixed-operating-point
models before constructing a scheduling-dependent map \cite{ferranti2017}.
Federated learning retains records locally \cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}.
For heterogeneous systems, FedSysID quantifies the tradeoff between
collaboration and model bias \cite{wang2023fedsysid}. IFCA alternates client
assignment and cluster-model updates \cite{ghosh2020ifca}.
Clustered SysID applies alternating assignment and estimation to linear
time-invariant (LTI) systems
with identical dynamics within each true cluster, providing conditional
recovery and sample-complexity guarantees \cite{toso2023clusteredsysid}.
IC-SYSID adds incremental cluster formation and stability promotion,
including a vehicle-fleet study \cite{kececi2025clustercraft}.
The vehicle study in \cite{weber2026ifac} uses uncertainty-aware grouping for controller
design. Here the central question is whether sharing
can identify an LPV map when each client lacks scheduling directions, rather
than how uncertainty in locally estimated dynamics informs grouping for
controller design.

Here each client's fixed speed support leaves its LPV scheduling design
deficient. Repeating transitions at those speeds reduces noise but cannot
identify the missing coefficient directions. Compatible pooling can supply
them, conditional on group coverage, while within-group variation remains.
The unresolved issue is whether assignment from partial-speed targets can
produce groups that are both sufficiently compatible and collectively
informative across the speed interval. With common physical-state coordinates,
we specialize the established alternating assignment and estimation structure
to LPV matrix regression. The cited recovery guarantees do not establish
recovery for our fixed deficient scheduling supports and approximate shared
maps. Our contribution is the LPV formulation and controlled evidence for
this joint limitation; Proposition~1 states the conditional rank requirement,
while the experiments assess its satisfaction and the remaining pooling bias.
Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the procedure.

Blocked-speed validation separates representation error from missing
information. Additive statistics and local scores implement grouped regression
without full local coefficient maps or family labels; centralized agreement
checks implementation. Paired fleet comparisons and group-rank audits assess
coverage and compatibility, while fresh stress tests bound their benefit.
Speed-scheduled linear-quadratic-integral (LQI) control tests downstream
usability on nonlinear vehicles.

\section{Identification Problem}
\subsection{Available states and incomplete speed coverage}
Client $i$ has sideslip angle $\beta$, yaw rate $r$, state $x=[\beta,r]^\top$,
steering input $u$, and
longitudinal speed $v$. The benchmark uses radians for $\beta,u$ and rad/s
for $r$. At a fixed cornering equilibrium, local perturbations satisfy
approximately
\begin{equation}
 \Delta x_{i,k+1}=A_i(v)\Delta x_{i,k}+B_i(v)\Delta u_{i,k}+e_{i,k},
 \label{eq:transition}
\end{equation}
where $A_i,B_i$ are discrete-time matrices and $e_{i,k}$ includes nonlinear
approximation error and next-state observation noise. Here
$\Delta x=x-x_i^\star(v)$ and $\Delta u=u-u_i^\star(v)$ are deviations
from the prescribed cornering equilibrium; the calibration workflow is
specified in Section~\ref{sec:calibration}. Define
$m_i(v)=[A_{11},A_{12},A_{21},A_{22},B_1,B_2]$.
The learned group assignment $g_i$ defines the approximation
\begin{equation}
 m_i(v)\approx \phi(v)^\top\Theta_{g_i},\qquad
 \Theta_g\in\R^{L\times6}.
 \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
Shared maps retain within-group approximation error. Each client provides matrix estimates at
speeds in $\mathcal V_i\subset\mathcal V=[10,30]$ m/s. ``Full envelope''
means this speed interval along the prescribed cornering curve, rather
than an arbitrary nonlinear state/input domain.

\subsection{Fixed representation and local estimation}
The scheduling span contains $L=7$ functions:
\begin{equation}
 b(v)=[1,v^{-1},v^{-2},z,z^2,z^3,z^4]^\top,\quad z=(v-20)/10.
 \label{eq:basis}
\end{equation}
Let the rows of $B_{\rm ref}$ be $b(v)^\top$ on a fixed 401-point uniform
10--30 m/s grid, with thin QR factorization $B_{\rm ref}=QR$.
The implemented row basis is $\phi(v)^\top=b(v)^\top R^{-1}$.
This invertible transform changes conditioning, not the represented span.
Development-only blocked-speed validation selects $L=7$ for cornering,
while the straight-line negative control retains $[1,v^{-1},v^{-2}]$.
No control comparison selects the representation.

At each observed speed, a client stacks 36 independent transitions.
With rows $[\Delta x_k^\top,\Delta u_k]$ in $Z$ and next-state rows in
$X_+$, ordinary least squares (OLS) gives
\begin{equation}
 \widehat C=\arg\min_C\|X_+-ZC\|_F^2,\qquad
 [\widehat A,\widehat B]=\widehat C^\top.
 \label{eq:local}
\end{equation}
In the primary study, current state/input regressors are exact, and only
next-state targets receive noise. The resulting matrix estimates and their
speeds are the group-regression targets. Vehicle parameters, exact evaluation
matrices, and family labels
do not enter that fitting or assignment interface.

The benchmark supplies centering equilibria from simulator parameters.
Section~\ref{sec:robustness-protocol} separately perturbs current measurements
and assumed centers. Estimating these centers from ordinary driving remains
unvalidated.
The clients contributing calibration records also receive the fitted maps.
Evaluation at additional speeds tests generalization across operating
conditions. Each fresh fleet repeats identification and grouping; transfer
of a fixed model library to new recipients is not evaluated.

\section{Federated Compatible Regression}
\subsection{Additive fitting and conditional identifiability}
Let $n_i=|\mathcal V_i|$ denote the number of observed speeds, not the number
of transitions. Client $i$ forms $\Phi_i\in\R^{n_i\times L}$ and matrix-entry targets
$Y_i\in\R^{n_i\times6}$. It contributes
$G_i=\Phi_i^\top\Phi_i$ and $H_i=\Phi_i^\top Y_i$.
For a fixed partition, the group fit is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Theta_g=\left(\sum_{i:g_i=g}G_i+
 (\lambda+\epsilon)I\right)^{-1}\sum_{i:g_i=g}H_i,
 \label{eq:fed}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda=10^{-8}$ and numerical diagonal $\epsilon=10^{-12}$.
The same unweighted ridge objective and basis are used in centralized
and federated grouped fitting. The matrix estimates have equal weight;
local estimation covariance is not propagated. The sensitivity study retains
this unweighted rule.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:rank}
For a fixed partition and exact shared-map targets
$Y_i=\Phi_i\Theta_g$, the unregularized coefficients are unique if and
only if the vertically stacked group design has rank $L$, equivalently
$\rank(\sum_{i:g_i=g}G_i)=L$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Justification:} The aggregate Gram matrix is positive definite
exactly when the stacked design has no nonzero null direction. Otherwise,
adding a null-direction coefficient matrix leaves all observed predictions
unchanged. Additivity also makes the centralized and distributed solutions
identical under the same numerical conventions.

This statement is conditional on a partition and model class. It neither
establishes correct group discovery nor removes pooling bias. Ridge gives
a numerical answer in deficient groups but does not restore information.

\subsection{Assignment and model selection}
The server derives a common entry scale from additive counts, sums, and
squared sums of $Y_i$. For entry $j$, let $s_j$ be its pooled standard
deviation, floored at $0.05$ times the median standard deviation, and set
$D=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_6)$. Clients return scores
\begin{equation}
 q_{ig}=\|(Y_i-\Phi_i\widehat\Theta_g)D^{-1}\|_F^2,
 \qquad g_i\leftarrow\arg\min_g q_{ig}.
 \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
Assignment ties choose the lowest group index. The common scaling balances
entries during assignment; fitting uses entry-wise ridge regression.
Initial groups come from K-means on
standardized six-entry client mean residuals around the Global fit.
The server receives those summaries, not the local matrices themselves.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Federated group-regression procedure.}
\label{tab:algorithm}
\begin{minipage}{.97\columnwidth}\footnotesize
\textbf{Client inputs:} Local speed/matrix pairs, fixed basis, candidate
$K=1,\ldots,5$, minimum group size five.\\
\textbf{1.} Aggregate local statistics and target moments. Fit Global,
broadcast it, and collect residual summaries.\\
\textbf{2.} For each $K$, use one initialization for $K=1$ or twelve
seeded K-means initializations otherwise.\\
\textbf{3.} Alternate group-statistic solves, map broadcasts, local score
returns, and hard assignments. Stop at unchanged assignments or 30
iterations. Discard initializations with groups smaller than five.\\
\textbf{4.} Refit the final admissible partition if needed. Retain the
lowest-BIC initialization for each $K$, then the lowest-BIC $K$.\\
\textbf{Outputs:} Group maps and client assignments, including $K=1$.
\end{minipage}
\end{table}

The BIC score uses the normalized assignment residuals:
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{BIC}=n\log\!\left(\max\left\{\mathrm{SSE}/n,10^{-12}\right\}\right)
 +p_K\log n,
 \label{eq:bic}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{SSE}=\sum_i q_{i,g_i}$, $n=6\sum_i n_i$, and
$p_K=6LK+K-1$. This is a heuristic
selection criterion for finite noisy matrix targets and within-group
variation. It does not certify a likelihood model, global convergence, or
physical-family recovery. Minimum group size does not imply full scheduling
rank, so group fit and group information must be assessed separately.

\subsection{Communication}
For $L=7$, each local Gram has 28 distinct entries and its right-hand side
has 42. Target moments, initialization summaries, group tags, assignment
scores, and model broadcasts also enter the accounting. Including all
candidate group counts and restarts, mean label-free traffic is \SearchTraffic~MB
per fleet at eight bytes per scalar. This exhaustive search is not shown
to be communication-efficient. The emulator retains raw records locally,
but informative statistics and scores are shared. No secure aggregation
or differential privacy is implemented.

\subsection{Nonlinear vehicles and calibration records}
\label{sec:calibration}

\begin{align}
 \dot\beta&=(F_f+F_r)/(mv)-r,\\
 \dot r&=(l_fF_f-l_rF_r)/I_z,\\
 F_a&=\mu F_{za}\tanh(C_a\alpha_a/(\mu F_{za})),\quad a\in\{f,r\}.

Here $\alpha_f=u-\beta-l_fr/v$, $\alpha_r=-\beta+l_rr/v$,
$F_{zf}=mg l_r/(l_f+l_r)$, $F_{zr}=mg l_f/(l_f+l_r)$,

Mass and inertia receive independent uniform multiplicative scatter of
$\pm2.5\%$ and axle stiffnesses $\pm5\%$. Geometry remains fixed.

Calibration follows curvature $\kappa_0=0.005$ m$^{-1}$. At each observed
speed, the simulator sets $r_i^\star=v\kappa_0$ and solves for
$\beta_i^\star,u_i^\star$ with $\dot\beta=\dot r=0$, using that client's
plant parameters. Each of 36 records starts independently from
$x_i^\star+\Delta x_j$, holds steering $u_i^\star+\Delta u_j$ and speed
fixed for one fourth-order Runge--Kutta (RK4) step of $T_s=0.01$ s, and supplies
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 x_{j,+}&=\mathcal F_i^{\rm RK4}
 (x_i^\star+\Delta x_j,u_i^\star+\Delta u_j;v),\\
 \widetilde{\Delta x}_{j,+}&=x_{j,+}-x_i^\star+\nu_j.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:calibration-record}
\end{equation}
The rows $[\Delta x_j^\top,\Delta u_j]$ and
$\widetilde{\Delta x}_{j,+}^\top$ form $Z$ and $X_+$ in
\eqref{eq:local}; the three-column regressor is checked for full rank.
These records are independently reset transitions, not consecutive driving
samples. In the primary study, only next-state targets receive noise $\nu_j$.
Zero-mean independent Gaussian perturbation
standard deviations are approximately $0.25^\circ$ for $\beta$, $2^\circ$/s
for $r$, and $0.30^\circ$ for steering. Added next-state noise has
standard deviations $0.01^\circ$ and $0.03^\circ$/s.

A prospective workflow is instrumented commissioning: hold selected speeds
near steady cornering, estimate state/steering centers from settled data,
and apply small steering perturbations with synchronized speed, steering,
yaw-rate, and sideslip measurements. Sideslip requires a reference sensor or
validated estimator. Local regression pairs supply the shared statistics.
This acquisition procedure remains untested: real maneuvers cannot reset
states arbitrarily, and correlated regressors, achievable excitation, and
center estimation require validation. The sensitivity tests perturb the
reset-record benchmark, not sequential driving or observer errors.

The four local speed blocks are $\{10,12,14\}$, $\{16,18,20\}$,
$\{22,24,26\}$, and $\{28,30\}$ m/s. Globally balanced random allocation
assigns them to eight, eight, seven, and seven clients, respectively,
without consulting family labels. Primary fleet seeds are 371--380.
Development representation selection uses separate seeds 301--310,
contiguous blocked-speed validation, a one-standard-error rule,
and a practical relative-error floor of $10^{-4}$.

\subsection{Endpoints, averaging, and baselines}
Reference matrices $m_i^\star(v)$ are numerical continuous-time Jacobians
at the prescribed equilibrium, discretized by an augmented matrix
exponential. They serve development-only representation and penalty selection
and evaluation, but do not enter finite-data group fitting.
Full-envelope matrix error is evaluated on the common speed grid:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 e_i(v)&=\frac{\|\widehat m_i(v)-m_i^\star(v)\|_2}
 {\|m_i^\star(v)\|_2},\\
 E_s&=\frac{1}{30\cdot11}\sum_i\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_{\rm grid}}e_i(v).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:error}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal V_{\rm grid}=\{10,12,\ldots,30\}$ m/s and $s$ indexes fleets.
The metric uses the stated physical coordinates without entry
normalization. It is coordinate dependent and does not directly measure
tracking performance. A post-hoc check averages
\[
 e_i^{\rm sc}(v)=\sqrt{\frac16\sum_{j=1}^{6}
 \left(\frac{\widehat m_{ij}(v)-m^\star_{ij}(v)}{d_j}\right)^2}
\]
over the same pairs. Each $d_j$ is the RMS reference entry across development
fleets 321--330 and all eleven speeds. These fixed scales balance entries,
without defining a control-weighted norm. Entry diagnostics use mean absolute error (MAE).
Observed/unobserved-speed summaries average only their respective
client-speed pairs within each fleet. Reported errors average $E_s$
over ten independent fleets. For method $M$ and baseline $B$, reported
gains average $100(1-E_s^M/E_s^B)$ over paired fleets, rather than taking
the ratio of grand mean errors. Confidence intervals resample those ten
fleet units 20,000 times.

Restricted Local uses $L=3$, ridge $10^{-8}$, and each client's records.
Global and both grouped methods use $L=7$ and the same fleet records.
These baselines separate complementary coverage, compatibility selection,
and distributed implementation. Prior-centered Local uses $L=7$ with ridge
toward its own $L=3$ fit expressed in the richer basis, denoted
$\Theta_{i,0}$. Leave-one-speed-out validation selects the penalty $\rho_i$ from
$\{10^{-6},10^{-4},10^{-2},1,10^2\}$; clients with only two speeds use
the fixed middle candidate $10^{-2}$.

To explain its near-equivalence to Restricted Local, define
$R_i=Y_i-\Phi_i\Theta_{i,0}$,
$\Delta_i=\widehat\Theta_i^{\rm P}-\Theta_{i,0}$, and
$P_i=\Phi_i^\top\Phi_i+(\rho_i+\epsilon)I$.
The centered-ridge normal equations imply
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 P_i\Delta_i&=\Phi_i^\top R_i-\epsilon\Theta_{i,0},\\
 N_i\Delta_i&=-\frac{\epsilon}{\rho_i+\epsilon}N_i\Theta_{i,0},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:prior-correction}
\end{equation}
where $N_i$ has orthonormal rows spanning $\ker\Phi_i$ and
$\epsilon=10^{-12}$. If $R_i=0$ and $\epsilon=0$, then $\Delta_i=0$ for every $\rho_i>0$.
The three-function design interpolates two or three distinct-speed targets;
ridge gives near-interpolation. The richer fit therefore retains the prior's
unsupported directions apart from numerical shrinkage.

To test sharing against a distinct local prior, Smooth Local penalizes
function curvature with $L=7$:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat\Theta_i^{\rm S}=\arg\min_\Theta\Bigl\{&
 \frac{\|Y_i-\Phi_i\Theta\|_F^2}{n_i}\\
 &+\alpha\|Q_2\Theta\|_F^2+\eta\|\Theta\|_F^2\Bigr\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:smooth-local}
\end{equation}
Here $\|Q_2\Theta\|_F^2$ uses 401-point trapezoidal quadrature for
$\tfrac12\int_{-1}^1\|d^2[\phi(v(z))^\top\Theta]/dz^2\|_2^2\,dz$;
$\eta=10^{-16}$ is numerical ridge. The curvature penalty leaves
$\operatorname{span}\{1,z\}$ unpenalized, distinct from Restricted Local's
$\operatorname{span}\{1,v^{-1},v^{-2}\}$. Smoothness supplies a prior,
without restoring local scheduling rank. A common
$\alpha$ is selected from $\{10^{-6},10^{-4},10^{-2},1,10^2,10^4,10^6\}$
on development fleets 321--330 using unobserved-speed matrix error.
The largest penalty within one fleet standard error of the best mean is
chosen: $10^6$, approaching an affine speed map. Reference Jacobians
serve this offline tuning; recipient fitting uses only its own noisy
targets at the original two or three speeds.
This matched check and the group-information audit reuse the ten primary
fleets and their saved calibration targets. They provide no independent confirmation.
A full-coverage recipient reference outperforms family pooling at
approximately equal transition budget, bounding the benefit to missing
local access rather than superiority at equal recipient information.

\subsection{Focused robustness protocol}
\label{sec:robustness-protocol}
To test the dependence on pooled coverage and calibration quality, ten fresh
fleets (seeds 1201--1210) are evaluated under seven predeclared paired
conditions. The reference repeats the primary procedure. All
conditions retain each client's two- or three-speed count, 36 transitions
per speed, physical parameters, basis, grouping settings, and the frozen
Smooth Local penalty. Perturbation and noise draws are paired by client and
speed-slot index; coverage changes alter speed locations while retaining
record budgets.

For family-coverage restriction, all ten heavy clients observe the first
two or three speeds in $\{10,12,14\}$ m/s; other clients retain their blocks.
Labels enter only this stress generator and a separate OracleFamily
diagnostic, never learned fitting or selection. Fleet-coverage restriction
maps the four blocks to $\{10,12,14\}$, $\{16,18,20\}$,
$\{10,12,14\}$, and $\{16,18\}$ m/s, respectively.
Four calibration conditions separately halve state/input excitation
standard deviations, double next-state noise standard deviations, add
current measurement noise with standard deviations
$(0.01^\circ,0.03^\circ/{\rm s},0.01^\circ)$ for $(\beta,r,u)$, or bias
their assumed centers by magnitudes
$(0.05^\circ,0.2^\circ/{\rm s},0.1^\circ)$.
Bias signs are drawn independently per component/client and held fixed
across its speeds. Center bias changes only the subtraction in
\eqref{eq:local}, retaining identical measured records and zero-intercept OLS.

A predeclared rank-only fallback replaces deficient selected group maps
with Global after selection, without changing grouping or fitting.
Global deficiency is flagged separately. The fixed full-grid error
\eqref{eq:error} is the main endpoint; recipient-unobserved sets change
with coverage. Intervals are pointwise 95\% paired-fleet bootstrap intervals.
No representation, prior, or controller is retuned, and no tracking rollout
is added.

\section{Identification Results}
\subsection{Why complementary coverage is needed}
At curvature 0.005 m$^{-1}$, development blocked-speed matrix error falls
from \BasisLowError\% at $L=3$ to \BasisHighError\% at $L=7$.
Figure~\ref{fig:basis-information}(a) contrasts this basis requirement
with the straight-line case, which retains order three. For seven
coefficients, the local blocks have ranks three, three, three, and two;
the development union has rank seven and condition \UnionCondition~
(Fig.~\ref{fig:basis-information}(b)). Complementary coverage can therefore
supply missing directions, conditional on the learned partition.

With finite transitions and known compatibility, oracle-family pooling

\section{Supporting LQI Validation}
Ten separate confirmation fleets (seeds 411--420) refit the maps and
apply speed-scheduled LQI to nonlinear 12 s maneuvers with available
noise-free physical-state feedback. Each fleet repeats the partial-speed
calibration above; maps remain fixed during tracking. ExactLPV is a
recipient-specific $L=7$ fit to exact Jacobians on the eleven-speed grid.
At each grid node, the method's fitted $\widehat A,\widehat B$ define
\begin{equation}
 A_{\rm a}=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat A&0\\T_sc_r&1\end{bmatrix},\quad
 B_{\rm a}=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat B\\0\end{bmatrix},\quad c_r=[0,1].
 \label{eq:lqi-augmentation}
\end{equation}
Discrete Riccati synthesis uses $Q=\operatorname{diag}(25,50,1000)$ for
$[\beta,r,\xi]^\top$, with yaw-error integral $\xi$, and steering weight
$R=0.15$. The yaw-reference
prefilter is $F=w+K_xh$, where
$(I-\widehat A)h-\widehat Bw=0$ and $c_rh=1$; $K_x$ denotes the first
two entries of the LQI gain. For Restricted Local at unobserved grid nodes, synthesis
evaluates its matrix map at the nearest observed speed. All methods then
linearly interpolate gains and prefilters on the common grid.
Weights were selected using only ExactLPV performance on development
seeds 381--385, subject to feasibility and saturation checks. A weaker
maneuver pilot on seeds 391--400 was discarded before the final confirmation.
Neither grouped-versus-Global results nor confirmation fleets select weights.
Smooth Local uses the same fleets and targets, frozen $\alpha=10^6$, and
all grid nodes. The common controller protocol remains frozen; this is neither
a separately tuned comparison nor an independent cohort.

With $r_k^{\rm ref}=a_{y,k}^{\rm req}/v_k$, the runtime law is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 u_k^\circ&=-K(v_k)[\beta_k,r_k,\xi_k]^\top+F(v_k)r_k^{\rm ref},\\
 \xi_{k+1}^{\rm cand}&=\operatorname{clip}_{[-0.6,0.6]}
 \{\xi_k+T_s(r_k-r_k^{\rm ref})\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:lqi-runtime}
\end{equation}
Steering is rate-limited relative to $u_{k-1}$, then amplitude-limited.
The integral candidate is accepted when the command is effectively
unsaturated or the integral increment counteracts limiting; otherwise
$\xi_{k+1}=\xi_k$. The integral bound is in radians. Each maneuver starts
with $x_0=0$, $\xi_0=0$, and $u_{-1}=0$; RK4 holds the applied steering
and current speed fixed within each sample.

Feedback acts on absolute states, with reference feedforward from the
homogeneous fitted matrices. Calibration equilibrium offsets are not used
online. The maps describe one cornering curve, whereas the maneuvers vary
the requested turn; tracking therefore tests empirical usability beyond
that curve, rather than exact equilibrium-offset compensation.

The moderate maneuver smoothly varies speed $11\to29\to15$ m/s and
uses an enveloped two-frequency lateral-acceleration demand with scale
4 m/s$^2$ and frequencies 0.35 and 0.72 Hz. The hard maneuver varies speed
$29\to12\to28$ m/s and uses alternating Gaussian demands centered at
$3.0,6.0,9.2$ s, with respective widths $0.40,0.48,0.55$ s and amplitudes
$7,-7.7,5.6$ m/s$^2$. Yaw reference is requested acceleration divided
by speed. Steering limits are $12^\circ$ and $120^\circ$/s, with a
$6^\circ$ sideslip feasibility bound. These remain fixed across methods.
Yaw RMSE uses the complete maneuver, then averages clients within fleet
and the ten fleet means.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
Complementary speed blocks supply missing local directions, while grouping
reduces aggregate pooling error relative to Global. The primary advantage
persists under development-scaled entry weighting, but does not extend to
every entry or recipient. Heavy and handling clients benefit while many
nominal clients deteriorate; learned groups need not recover physical families.
Centralized agreement verifies the distributed implementation.

\balance
The robustness study bounds the benefit: minimum group size does not ensure
rank, and Global fallback cannot restore fleet-wide missing information.
Smooth Local wins in that deficient condition; weaker calibration signal-to-noise
can collapse grouping to Global. Full selected-group rank is empirical,
selection is heuristic, and exhaustive search incurs measurable communication.
Ordinary driving, observer errors, real fleets, and frozen-library transfer
remain unvalidated.

The scheduled-LQI tests support usability with modest gains over Global.
The tested Smooth Local prior yields infeasible responses for some clients
under the frozen protocol, illustrating that matrix accuracy alone does not
establish controller suitability. Compatible sharing is useful when pooled
coverage and calibration quality support it, with explicit recipient tradeoffs.

\begin{abstract}
Individual vehicles may supply calibration data from only a narrow speed
region, although the fleet collectively covers the operating interval.
Pooling those records can recover missing scheduling information but also
mix incompatible vehicle dynamics. We investigate compatible federated
identification of linear-parameter-varying (LPV) maps under this joint
coverage and heterogeneity limitation. With available lateral states,
clients identify local matrices and exchange additive regression statistics
and assignment scores to learn shared group maps. A nonlinear vehicle
benchmark separates scheduling-rank deficiency from model mismatch and
finite-data error. Across ten label-free fleets, grouped fitting reduces
mean paired full-envelope matrix error by \SmoothLocalFullGain\% relative to
development-tuned Smooth Local and \IdentGlobalGain\% relative to Global. At locally
unobserved speeds, mean error is \PrimaryFedUnseenError\%, compared with
\SmoothLocalUnseenError\% for Smooth Local and \PrimaryGlobalUnseenError\% for Global.
The distributed implementation reproduces centralized grouped fitting.
All \ReviewGroups\ selected groups have full-rank scheduling designs.
Separate scheduled-LQI tests provide modest supporting tracking gains.
The validated setting is controlled equilibrium-centered calibration with
available states, exact current regressors, and noisy next-state targets.
\end{abstract}

federated identification, LPV systems, incomplete operating coverage,

\section{Introduction}
Vehicles with speed-dependent and heterogeneous dynamics may observe only
narrow speed regions. LPV identification then requires complementary
scheduling information while avoiding bias from pooling incompatible dynamics.

Basis-dependent LPV identification requires scheduling excitation
\cite{toth2010lpv,bamieh2002}. Local approaches identify fixed-operating-point
models before constructing a scheduling-dependent map \cite{ferranti2017}.
Federated learning retains records locally \cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}.
For heterogeneous systems, FedSysID quantifies the tradeoff between
collaboration and model bias \cite{wang2023fedsysid}. IFCA alternates client
assignment and cluster-model updates \cite{ghosh2020ifca}.
Clustered SysID applies alternating assignment and estimation to LTI systems
with identical dynamics within each true cluster, providing conditional
recovery and sample-complexity guarantees \cite{toso2023clusteredsysid}.
IC-SYSID adds incremental cluster formation and stability promotion,
including a vehicle-fleet study \cite{kececi2025clustercraft}.
Our previous vehicle study uses uncertainty-aware grouping for controller
design \cite{weber2026ifac}.

Here each client's fixed speed support leaves its LPV scheduling design
deficient. Repeating transitions at those speeds reduces noise but cannot
identify the missing coefficient directions. Compatible pooling can supply
them, conditional on group coverage, while within-group variation remains.
The problem couples scheduling-information deficiency with heterogeneity
bias. With common physical states, we specialize the established alternating
framework to LPV matrix regression. Its guarantees do not establish recovery
for our fixed deficient scheduling supports and approximate shared maps.
Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the procedure.

The contribution is a distributed LPV construction and controlled study
of joint coverage and heterogeneity. First, blocked-speed validation
separates representation error from information deficiency. Second, clients
fit group maps through additive statistics and assignment scores without
full local coefficient maps or family labels, followed by a group-rank audit.
Third, paired fleet comparisons separate coverage and compatibility benefits
at recipient-unobserved grid speeds. Centralized--federated agreement verifies
implementation. Nonlinear scheduled-LQI tests check downstream usability.

\section{Identification Problem}
\subsection{Available states and incomplete speed coverage}
Client $i$ has lateral states $x=[\beta,r]^\top$, steering input $u$, and
longitudinal speed $v$. The benchmark uses radians for $\beta,u$ and rad/s
for $r$. At a fixed cornering equilibrium, local perturbations satisfy
approximately
\begin{equation}
 \Delta x_{i,k+1}=A_i(v)\Delta x_{i,k}+B_i(v)\Delta u_{i,k}+e_{i,k},
 \label{eq:transition}
\end{equation}
where $A_i,B_i$ are discrete-time matrices and $e_{i,k}$ includes nonlinear
approximation error and next-state observation noise. Define
$m_i(v)=[A_{11},A_{12},A_{21},A_{22},B_1,B_2]$.
A group $g_i$, unknown during fitting, approximates the client map as
\begin{equation}
 m_i(v)\approx \phi(v)^\top\Theta_{g_i},\qquad
 \Theta_g\in\R^{L\times6}.
 \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
Within-group variation remains. Each client provides matrix estimates at
speeds in $\mathcal V_i\subset\mathcal V=[10,30]$ m/s. ``Full envelope''
means this speed interval along the prescribed cornering curve, rather
than an arbitrary nonlinear state/input domain.

\subsection{Fixed representation and local estimation}
The scheduling span is
\begin{equation}
 b(v)=[1,v^{-1},v^{-2},z,z^2,z^3,z^4]^\top,\quad z=(v-20)/10.
 \label{eq:basis}
\end{equation}
Let the rows of $B_{\rm ref}$ be $b(v)^\top$ on a fixed 401-point uniform
10--30 m/s grid, with thin QR factorization $B_{\rm ref}=QR$.
The implemented row basis is $\phi(v)^\top=b(v)^\top R^{-1}$.
This invertible transform changes conditioning, not the represented span.
Development-only blocked-speed validation selects $L=7$ for cornering,
while the straight-line negative control retains $[1,v^{-1},v^{-2}]$.
No control comparison selects the representation.

At each observed speed, a client stacks 36 independent transitions.
With rows $[\Delta x_k^\top,\Delta u_k]$ in $Z$ and next-state rows in
$X_+$, it estimates
\begin{equation}
 \widehat C=\arg\min_C\|X_+-ZC\|_F^2,\qquad
 [\widehat A,\widehat B]=\widehat C^\top.
 \label{eq:local}
\end{equation}
Current state/input regressors are exact, and only next-state targets
receive noise. These estimates, with their speeds, are the group-regression
targets. Vehicle parameters, exact evaluation matrices, and family labels
do not enter that fitting or assignment interface.

The generator computes each centering equilibrium from simulator
parameters. Thus available states alone do not imply identification from
ordinary driving records. A controlled experiment could instead measure
an operating-point center, but that alternative is not validated here.
The same clients contribute calibration records and receive the maps.
Evaluation at additional speeds tests operating-condition generalization;
fresh fleets repeat the whole procedure rather than testing a frozen
library on wholly new recipients.

\section{Federated Compatible Regression}
\subsection{Additive fitting and conditional identifiability}
Client $i$ forms $\Phi_i\in\R^{n_i\times L}$ and matrix-entry targets
$Y_i\in\R^{n_i\times6}$. It contributes
$G_i=\Phi_i^\top\Phi_i$ and $H_i=\Phi_i^\top Y_i$.
For a fixed partition, the group fit is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Theta_g=\left(\sum_{i:g_i=g}G_i+
 (\lambda+\epsilon)I\right)^{-1}\sum_{i:g_i=g}H_i,
 \label{eq:fed}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda=10^{-8}$ and numerical diagonal $\epsilon=10^{-12}$.
The same unweighted ridge objective and basis are used in centralized
and federated grouped fitting. The matrix estimates have equal weight;
local estimation covariance is not propagated. Unequal noise or excitation
could therefore motivate weighting, but is outside the present comparison.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:rank}
For a fixed partition and exact shared-map targets
$Y_i=\Phi_i\Theta_g$, the unregularized coefficients are unique if and
only if the vertically stacked group design has rank $L$, equivalently
$\rank(\sum_{i:g_i=g}G_i)=L$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Justification:} The aggregate Gram matrix is positive definite
exactly when the stacked design has no nonzero null direction. Otherwise,
adding a null-direction coefficient matrix leaves all observed predictions
unchanged. Additivity also makes the centralized and distributed solutions
identical under the same numerical conventions.

This statement is conditional on a partition and model class. It neither
establishes correct group discovery nor removes pooling bias. Ridge gives
a numerical answer in deficient groups but does not restore information.

\subsection{Assignment and model selection}
The server derives a common entry scale from additive counts, sums, and
squared sums of $Y_i$. For entry $j$, let $s_j$ be its pooled standard
deviation, floored at $0.05$ times the median standard deviation, and set
$D=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_6)$. Clients return scores
\begin{equation}
 q_{ig}=\|(Y_i-\Phi_i\widehat\Theta_g)D^{-1}\|_F^2,
 \qquad g_i\leftarrow\arg\min_g q_{ig}.
 \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
The common scaling balances entries during assignment. Fitting remains
entry-wise ridge regression. Initial groups come from K-means on
standardized six-entry client mean residuals around the Global fit.
The server receives those summaries, not the local matrices themselves.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Federated group-regression procedure.}
\label{tab:algorithm}
\begin{minipage}{.97\columnwidth}\footnotesize
\textbf{Inputs:} Local speed/matrix pairs, fixed basis, candidate
$K=1,\ldots,5$, minimum group size five.\\
\textbf{1.} Aggregate local statistics and target moments. Fit Global,
broadcast it, and collect residual summaries.\\
\textbf{2.} For each $K$, use one initialization for $K=1$ or twelve
seeded K-means initializations otherwise.\\
\textbf{3.} Alternate group-statistic solves, map broadcasts, local score
returns, and hard assignments. Stop at unchanged assignments or 30
iterations. Discard initializations with groups smaller than five.\\
\textbf{4.} Refit the final admissible partition if needed. Retain the
lowest-BIC initialization for each $K$, then the lowest-BIC $K$.\\
\textbf{Outputs:} Group maps and client assignments, including $K=1$.
\end{minipage}
\end{table}

The model-selection score uses the normalized assignment residuals:
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{BIC}=n\log\!\left(\max\left\{\mathrm{SSE}/n,10^{-12}\right\}\right)
 +p_K\log n,
 \label{eq:bic}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{SSE}=\sum_i q_{i,g_i}$, $n=6\sum_i n_i$, and
$p_K=6LK+K-1$. Ties choose the first group index. This is a heuristic
selection criterion for finite noisy matrix targets and within-group
variation. It does not certify a likelihood model, global convergence, or
physical-family recovery. Minimum group size does not imply full scheduling
rank, so group fit and group information must be assessed separately.

\subsection{Communication}
For $L=7$, each local Gram has 28 distinct entries and its right-hand side
has 42. Target moments, initialization summaries, group tags, assignment
scores, and model broadcasts also enter the accounting. Including all
candidate orders and restarts, mean label-free traffic is \SearchTraffic~MB
per fleet at eight bytes per scalar. This exhaustive search is not shown
to be communication-efficient. The emulator retains raw records locally,
but informative statistics and scores are shared. No secure aggregation
or differential privacy is implemented.

\section{Benchmark and Evaluation}
\subsection{Nonlinear vehicles and calibration records}
The plant is a two-state single-track model with nonlinear tanh tires:
\begin{align}
 \dot\beta&=(F_f+F_r)/(mv)-r,\\
 \dot r&=(l_fF_f-l_rF_r)/I_z,\\
 F_a&=\mu F_{za}\tanh(C_a\alpha_a/(\mu F_{za})),\quad a\in\{f,r\}.
\end{align}
Here $\alpha_f=u-\beta-l_fr/v$, $\alpha_r=-\beta+l_rr/v$,
$F_{zf}=mg l_r/(l_f+l_r)$, $F_{zr}=mg l_f/(l_f+l_r)$,
$g=9.81$ m/s$^2$, and $\mu=0.9$.
Each fleet has ten clients from each center in Table~\ref{tab:centers}.
Mass and inertia receive independent uniform multiplicative scatter of
$\pm2.5\%$ and axle stiffnesses $\pm5\%$. Geometry remains fixed.
Family labels are withheld from primary block allocation and fitting.

\subsection{Endpoints, averaging, and baselines}
Reference matrices $m_i^\star(v)$ are numerical continuous-time Jacobians
at the prescribed equilibrium, discretized by an augmented matrix
exponential. They are used for development representation and local-penalty selection and
evaluation, not finite-data group fitting. The primary relative error is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 e_i(v)&=\frac{\|\widehat m_i(v)-m_i^\star(v)\|_2}
 {\|m_i^\star(v)\|_2},\\
 E_s&=\frac{1}{30\cdot11}\sum_i\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_{\rm grid}}e_i(v).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:error}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal V_{\rm grid}=\{10,12,\ldots,30\}$ m/s.
The metric uses the stated physical coordinates without entry
normalization. It is a fixed-coordinate benchmark error, not a
coordinate-invariant measure or a tracking-performance surrogate.
Observed/unobserved-speed summaries average only their respective
client-speed pairs within each fleet. Reported errors average $E_s$
over ten independent fleets. For method $M$ and baseline $B$, reported
gains average $100(1-E_s^M/E_s^B)$ over paired fleets, rather than taking
the ratio of grand mean errors. Confidence intervals resample those ten
fleet units 20,000 times.

Restricted Local uses $L=3$, ridge $10^{-8}$, and each client's records.
Global and both grouped methods use $L=7$ and the same fleet records.
These baselines separate complementary coverage, compatibility selection,
and distributed implementation. Prior-centered Local uses $L=7$ with ridge
toward its own $L=3$ fit expressed in the richer basis, denoted
$\Theta_{i,0}$. Leave-one-speed-out validation selects the penalty $\rho_i$ from
$\{10^{-6},10^{-4},10^{-2},1,10^2\}$; clients with only two speeds use
the fixed middle candidate $10^{-2}$.

To explain its near-equivalence to Restricted Local, define
$R_i=Y_i-\Phi_i\Theta_{i,0}$,
$\Delta_i=\widehat\Theta_i^{\rm P}-\Theta_{i,0}$, and
$P_i=\Phi_i^\top\Phi_i+(\rho_i+\epsilon)I$.
The centered-ridge normal equations imply
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 P_i\Delta_i&=\Phi_i^\top R_i-\epsilon\Theta_{i,0},\\
 N_i\Delta_i&=-\frac{\epsilon}{\rho_i+\epsilon}N_i\Theta_{i,0},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:prior-correction}
\end{equation}
where $N_i$ has orthonormal rows spanning $\ker\Phi_i$ and
$\epsilon=10^{-12}$. If $R_i=0$ and $\epsilon=0$, then $\Delta_i=0$
for every $\rho_i>0$. The three-function local design has full row rank
at two or three distinct speeds and can therefore interpolate these
targets. The implemented ridge terms yield near-interpolation instead.
The richer fit preserves unsupported directions of the same prior,
apart from numerical shrinkage; extra basis functions add no local data.

\begin{samepage}
To test sharing against a distinct local prior, Smooth Local penalizes
function curvature with $L=7$:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Theta_i^{\rm S}=\arg\min_\Theta
 \frac{\|Y_i-\Phi_i\Theta\|_F^2}{n_i}
 +\alpha\|Q_2\Theta\|_F^2+\eta\|\Theta\|_F^2.
 \label{eq:smooth-local}
\end{equation}
\end{samepage}
Here $\|Q_2\Theta\|_F^2$ uses 401-point trapezoidal quadrature for
$\tfrac12\int_{-1}^1\|d^2[\phi(v(z))^\top\Theta]/dz^2\|_2^2\,dz$;
$\eta=10^{-16}$ is numerical ridge. The curvature penalty leaves
$\operatorname{span}\{1,z\}$ unpenalized, distinct from Restricted Local's
$\operatorname{span}\{1,v^{-1},v^{-2}\}$. Smoothness supplies a prior,
without restoring local scheduling rank. A common
$\alpha$ is selected from $\{10^{-6},10^{-4},10^{-2},1,10^2,10^4,10^6\}$
on development fleets 321--330 using unobserved-speed matrix error.
The largest penalty within one fleet standard error of the best mean is
chosen: $10^6$, approaching an affine speed map. Reference Jacobians
serve this offline tuning; recipient fitting uses only its own noisy
targets at the original two or three speeds.
This matched check and the group-information audit reuse the original ten
fleets and calibration protocol, with reconstructed parent errors verified
against saved client-speed results. They add no independent confirmation.
A full-coverage recipient reference outperforms family pooling at
approximately equal transition budget, bounding the benefit to missing
local access rather than superiority at equal recipient information.

\section{Identification Results}
\subsection{Why complementary coverage is needed}
At curvature 0.005 m$^{-1}$, development blocked-speed matrix error falls
from \BasisLowError\% at order three to \BasisHighError\% at order seven.
Figure~\ref{fig:basis-information}(a) contrasts this basis requirement
with the straight-line case, which retains order three. For seven
coefficients, the local blocks have ranks three, three, three, and two;
the development union has rank seven and condition \UnionCondition~
(Fig.~\ref{fig:basis-information}(b)). Complementary coverage can therefore
supply missing directions, conditional on the learned partition.

With finite transitions and known compatibility, oracle-family pooling
achieves \OracleUnseenError\% unobserved-speed error, versus
\LocalUnseenError\% for Local and \GlobalUnseenError\% for Global on
separate development fleets. The prior-centered diagnostic changes Local
negligibly there; this does not test a distinct extrapolation prior.
These oracle results motivate the
mechanism; the primary evaluation below withholds labels.

\subsection{Primary label-free results}
Table~\ref{tab:id} separates the full-envelope endpoint from its observed
and unobserved regions. Mean paired gains over Local and Global are
\IdentLocalGain\% and \IdentGlobalGain\%, respectively.
The Global-relative 95\% interval is $[\IdentGlobalLow,\IdentGlobalHigh]\%$,
with improvement in eight of ten fleets. Two groups are selected in eight
fleets and one in two. Recipient-only fits are more accurate at their own
observed speeds, whereas sharing substantially reduces Restricted Local's unsupported
extrapolation error. Grouped sharing additionally reduces Global's pooling
error. Figure~\ref{fig:coverage} locates these changes relative to each
client block. Figure~\ref{fig:fleet-client}(a) shows the paired fleet
errors, including the two $K=1$ ties. Aggregate improvement does not
extend to every client: 169 instances improve, 60 are numerical ties, and
\PrimaryUnseenWorseClients\ have larger unobserved-speed error than Global.
Figure~\ref{fig:fleet-client}(b) shows this tradeoff. Its 300 client
instances are nested within ten fleets and are descriptive, rather than
independent replicates for confidence intervals.

Prior-centered Local changes unobserved-speed error by only
\ReviewUnseenLocalChange~percentage points. Its prior's observed-target
relative residual has median \PriorResidualMedian\% and maximum
\PriorResidualMax\% across 300 clients. Equation~\ref{eq:prior-correction}
explains the prior retention; its correction and nullspace identities are
verified for all 300 saved fits. This diagnostic provides little independent
evidence about a different extrapolation prior.

Smooth Local substantially reduces Local's extrapolation error and has the
lowest observed-speed error (Table~\ref{tab:id}). Grouped fitting still
improves full-envelope error by \SmoothLocalFullGain\%, with paired
fleet-bootstrap interval $[\SmoothLocalFullLow,\SmoothLocalFullHigh]\%$,
and unobserved-speed error by \SmoothLocalUnseenGain\%, interval
$[\SmoothLocalUnseenLow,\SmoothLocalUnseenHigh]\%$, winning in all ten
fleets for both endpoints. Figure~\ref{fig:coverage} shows the remaining
unobserved-speed gap; other local priors remain untested.

\subsection{Scheduling information in selected groups}
All \ReviewGroups\ selected groups contain observations at all eleven
grid speeds and have scheduling-design rank seven. Their stacked design
condition numbers range from \ReviewConditionMin\ to \ReviewConditionMax\
in the fixed QR basis, as summarized in Fig.~\ref{fig:basis-information}(b).
The learned partitions satisfy Proposition~\ref{prop:rank}'s scheduling
condition for this benchmark. The reported conditioning concerns the
scheduling design, rather than its Gram or the full dynamical-identification
information matrix. Full rank neither removes within-group bias nor
guarantees physical-family recovery.

A known-family reference loses a speed block in one fleet and produces
an infeasible full-envelope controller, illustrating that compatibility
labels alone do not establish information coverage.

\section{Supporting LQI Validation}
Ten separate confirmation fleets (seeds 411--420) refit the maps and
apply speed-scheduled LQI to nonlinear 12 s maneuvers with available
physical-state feedback. At the eleven grid speeds, discrete Riccati
synthesis uses $Q=\operatorname{diag}(25,50,1000)$ for the two states and
yaw-error integral, with steering weight $R=0.15$.
Gains and reference prefilters are linearly interpolated in speed.
Weights were selected using only ExactLPV performance on development
seeds 381--385, subject to feasibility and saturation checks. A weaker
maneuver pilot on seeds 391--400 was discarded before the final confirmation.
Neither grouped-versus-Global results nor confirmation fleets select weights.

The moderate maneuver smoothly varies speed $11\to29\to15$ m/s and
uses an enveloped two-frequency lateral-acceleration demand with scale
4 m/s$^2$ and frequencies 0.35/0.72 Hz. The hard maneuver varies speed
$29\to12\to28$ m/s and uses alternating Gaussian demands centered at
3.0/6.0/9.2 s, widths 0.40/0.48/0.55 s, and amplitudes
$7/-7.7/5.6$ m/s$^2$. Yaw reference is requested acceleration divided
by speed. Steering limits are $12^\circ$ and $120^\circ$/s, with
conditional integration, integral clipping at 0.6, and a $6^\circ$
sideslip feasibility bound. These remain fixed across methods.

$[\ControlGlobalModerateLow,\ControlGlobalModerateHigh]\%$ and
$[\ControlGlobalHardLow,\ControlGlobalHardHigh]\%$.
Absolute mean improvements are \ControlAbsoluteModerate/\ControlAbsoluteHard~degrees/s,
so the control evidence supports usability with modest practical headroom.
All federated trajectories satisfy the tested constraints. Maximum
frozen-point spectral radius is \ControlRadius, which is not a scheduled

federated and Global controllers identical there.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
Complementary speed blocks supply directions absent from local regressions,
while compatibility selection reduces pooling error. Grouped fitting
improves full-envelope identification over Restricted Local, the distinct
Smooth Local comparator, and
Global, and federation reproduces centralized fitting on matched records.
The figures also expose the tradeoff: recipient-only fits are more accurate at observed
speeds, and some clients deteriorate relative to Global. The benefit is
aggregate access across the speed interval, rather than uniform client
improvement or recovery of the generating physical families.

The controlled calibration uses simulator-derived centering and exact
current regressors along one cornering curve. Ordinary driving, noisy state
estimates, real fleets, and frozen-library transfer remain unvalidated.
Group selection is heuristic: full rank is empirical, minimum size does
not guarantee it, and exhaustive search incurs measurable communication.

Compatible federated regression therefore supplies useful LPV maps across
the speed interval under the stated assumptions. Nonlinear scheduled-LQI
tests support usability with modest tracking gains. Smooth Local has only
been compared on matrix error; its downstream control performance remains
untested.

\begin{abstract}
Vehicle fleets exhibit two different sources of variation: measurable changes
in operating conditions and persistent differences in vehicle dynamics.
Treating both through one global model loses relevant structure, whereas fitting
one model per vehicle requires repeated local calibration and produces many
deployed controllers. This paper develops a federated linear-parameter-varying
(LPV) identification framework that represents speed dependence explicitly and
shares compact, family-compatible dynamic models without exchanging vehicle
trajectories. The intended central contribution is a control-aware
client-level-differentially-private aggregation mechanism whose privacy noise is
shaped using public closed-loop sensitivity information. The method is evaluated
by converting released models into scheduled controllers and testing them on
nonlinear heterogeneous vehicle fleets. The study asks not only whether a
private model estimate is accurate, but when privacy-preserving collaboration
retains tracking performance, feasibility, and closed-loop robustness. Existing
results establish the LPV structure, distributed-estimation equivalence, the
failure of naive private aggregation for small cohorts, and utility recovery
through public physical bounds and cohort scaling. The final manuscript will
add the proposed control-aware mechanism and a frozen deployment-robustness
comparison before making the corresponding performance claim.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
federated learning, LPV systems, vehicle dynamics, system identification,
differential privacy, vehicle control
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Intended Paper Story}

The paper is organized around one question:
\begin{quote}
\emph{How can heterogeneous vehicles collaboratively identify a compact
speed-dependent control model while keeping each vehicle's complete local
record protected?}
\end{quote}
The answer has three parts. First, measurable operating variation and persistent
fleet heterogeneity should not be absorbed into the same model dimension:
longitudinal speed is an LPV scheduling variable, whereas compatible vehicle
classes share separate parameter vectors. Second, the structured model permits
federated identification using only a small number of physically meaningful
ratios while retaining centralized numerical performance. Third, formal privacy
must be assessed at the controller level. Standard isotropic client-level noise
can make an accurate model unusable; useful privacy is recovered through
defensible public physical bounds, sufficient aggregation cohorts, and---in the
intended final method---noise shaping based on public control sensitivity.

The narrative deliberately includes informative failures. Local identification
is already strong in this structured benchmark, so neither family pooling nor
personalization should be advertised as an accuracy improvement. Likewise, a
ten-client isotropic private mean fails the predefined relative utility gate.
These negative results motivate the final method and define where federated
collaboration is useful: reducing per-vehicle calibration and model proliferation
while enabling a useful private fleet model, rather than universally outperforming
every local estimator.

\section{Claimed Contributions}

The final paper should make only the following contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A structured fleet model that separates speed scheduling from
    persistent compatibility classes and identifies the regimes in which each
    source of variation matters for control.
    \item A federated output-error identification implementation for the compact
    LPV parameterization, with numerical equivalence to centralized pooled
    estimation and explicit communication accounting.
    \item A client-level differential-privacy formulation evaluated end to end
    through model estimation, scheduled controller synthesis, nonlinear tracking,
    actuator feasibility, and frozen closed-loop robustness.
    \item A control-aware private aggregation mechanism that improves the
    privacy--control tradeoff over isotropic Gaussian aggregation under the same
    adjacency definition and privacy budget. This contribution remains subject
    to Experiments 8E--8G and must be removed or weakened if their gates fail.
\end{enumerate}

The manuscript will not claim private learned clustering, production-grade
cryptography, nonlinear stability certification, or universal superiority over
local identification. Compatibility classes are assumed to be available from
public vehicle platform or configuration information. This choice also keeps the
paper distinct from the authors' earlier uncertainty-aware clustered federated
identification work.

\section{Federated Structured LPV Identification}

\emph{Section role: present the non-private distributed baseline and establish
that federation does not change the intended pooled objective.}

Define the family-pooled output-error objective
\begin{equation}
 \widehat p_g=\arg\min_{p}\sum_{i:g_i=g}L_i(p;\mathcal D_i),
\end{equation}
and the client/server protocol in which trajectories remain local and clients
return aggregateable losses or gradients. Report communication in transmitted
scalars and use the centralized, federated, and aggregate-only paths from
Experiment 8B as an implementation-equivalence gate. Avoid presenting the
pairwise-mask emulator as deployed secure-aggregation cryptography.

\section{Control-Aware Client-Level Privacy}

\emph{Section role: contain the paper's main methodological novelty.}

Define replacement adjacency at the vehicle level: neighboring fleets differ in
one vehicle's complete dataset. Explain bounded log-parameter contributions,
clipping, the sensitivity of the family mean, analytic Gaussian calibration,
and privacy-preserving post-processing into a scheduled controller.

The proposed method should replace isotropic normalized noise by a transformation
or covariance fixed from public closed-loop sensitivity information. The final
version must provide:
\begin{itemize}
    \item an explicit public sensitivity or control-loss metric;
    \item the resulting clipping geometry and Gaussian covariance;
    \item a proposition establishing the same $(\epsilon,\delta)$ client-level
    guarantee as the isotropic baseline;
    \item a clear statement of trust, secure aggregation, and repeated-release
    assumptions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Protocol}

\emph{Section role: make the final comparison auditable without reproducing the
entire development history.}

Use fresh fleets and compare Local, Central pooled, non-private Federated,
isotropic DP-Federated, and control-aware DP-Federated models. Retain Global and
known-family scopes only where they answer a stated question. Evaluate multiple
speed-varying maneuvers on nonlinear held-out plants, with fleet seeds as the
independent statistical units.

The final protocol should include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item privacy budgets and fixed $\delta$;
    \item local data duration and speed coverage;
    \item cohort size and partial participation;
    \item public-bound misspecification or out-of-box clients;
    \item nonlinear plant mismatch;
    \item tracking, steering effort, constraint feasibility, frozen radius,
    communication, and clipping frequency.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results: From Structural Need to Private Control}

\emph{Section role: give a short causal chain rather than an experiment diary.}

\subsection{Why scheduled family models?}
Condense Experiments 2--6 into one regime-map result: scheduling addresses broad
speed variation, specialization addresses persistent dynamic separation, and
their combination is needed when both occur. Include the compactness result only
as supporting evidence.

\subsection{Does the federated implementation preserve the estimator?}
Use Experiment 8B to show centralized--federated numerical equivalence and then
move on; this should occupy a small table or several sentences, not a full main
result figure.

\subsection{What does privacy cost?}
Use Experiment 8C as the negative baseline: isotropic client-level noise with ten
clients does not satisfy the predefined tracking gate and can produce adverse
frozen-radius cases at strong privacy.

\subsection{When is private fleet learning useful?}
Use Experiment 8D to show the effects of public bounds and cohort size. The
current strongest passing point is $\epsilon=2$ with 100 clients per known
family, 6.05\% tracking degradation relative to the matching non-private mean,
complete amplitude feasibility, and worst frozen radius 0.9692. The final result
must add Experiments 8E--8G and directly compare the proposed control-aware
mechanism with Local and isotropic private baselines.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

Explain that privacy utility depends on the number of simultaneously
aggregateable compatible clients and on defensible public parameter knowledge.
Discuss the unusually accurate local estimator, known compatibility metadata,
full-participation assumptions in the present 8D result, privacy composition,
simulator-derived bounds, nonlinear-model mismatch, and the absence of a formal
nonlinear stability certificate. Explicitly distinguish this paper's
scheduling/privacy contribution from learned clustering.

\section{Conclusion}

The intended conclusion is conditional rather than universal: federated LPV
identification becomes useful for heterogeneous vehicle fleets when operating
variation is scheduled explicitly, only compatible vehicles are aggregated,
and the privacy mechanism respects downstream control sensitivity. The final
sentence should report the empirically supported privacy/cohort operating region,
not claim that federated estimation always improves local accuracy.

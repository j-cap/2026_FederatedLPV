\begin{abstract}
A newly deployed vehicle may lack sufficient excitation for controller identification, while a mature fleet has complementary data that cannot be pooled as raw trajectories. We propose federated personalized linear-parameter-varying (LPV) identification for this cold-start setting. Clients identify three structured log-parameters and local uncertainty; additive sufficient statistics learn a heteroscedastic latent mixture with unknown order. Each group supplies a controller-gain-aware rank-one backbone, while an unseen vehicle estimates its group and one private coordinate from a short record. Evaluation uses varying-speed controllers on nonlinear heterogeneous bicycle plants. In a frozen ten-fleet confirmation, 20\% rotating participation reduced yaw-rate tracking RMSE by 17.27\% relative to local identification with 0.75~s of client data and remained feasible in all tests. A paired retrospective analysis gives 16.90\% mean improvement (95\% fleet-bootstrap interval: 11.79--21.10\%; nine of ten wins). The advantage vanishes with sufficient local information, and a persistent narrow participant subset exposes a coverage failure. The result supports federated LPV learning as an amortized cold-start representation, not as a universal replacement for well-excited local identification.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}federated learning, LPV systems, system identification, personalized learning, vehicle control\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Vehicle dynamics vary with measurable operating conditions and persistent physical differences. Speed scheduling addresses the former; latent fleet structure addresses the latter. We ask whether a mature fleet can transfer a compact control representation to an unseen, information-limited vehicle without exchanging trajectories.

The contributions are: (1) unknown-order federated heteroscedastic mixture estimation for structured LPV summaries; (2) a control-gain-aware low-rank group backbone with a client-private local coordinate; (3) blind nonlinear closed-loop evidence under rotating partial participation; and (4) a coverage stress test and complete discovery-versus-update communication audit. Unlike our earlier uncertainty-aware clustering study, novelty is not claimed for clustering alone: it is the combination of explicit LPV scheduling, unknown-order federation, unseen-client low-rank personalization, and the participation-coverage boundary.

\section{Problem Formulation}
The evaluation plant is a nonlinear heterogeneous bicycle model with nonlinear tire forces. Identification and synthesis use a structured LPV approximation scheduled by longitudinal speed, with
\begin{equation}p_i=[C_{f,i}/m_i,\ C_{r,i}/m_i,\ m_i/I_{z,i}]^\top,\quad z_i=\log p_i.\end{equation}
Client $i$ retains $\mathcal D_i$ and obtains an output-error estimate $z_i$ and Gauss--Newton covariance $C_i$. A new client's objective is nonlinear yaw-rate tracking with only $T\in\{0.75,1.25\}$~s of local data.

\section{Federated Latent LPV Backbones}
The mature fleet is modeled by
\begin{equation}z_i\sim\sum_{g=1}^{K}\pi_g\mathcal N(\mu_g,C_i+S_g).\end{equation}
For each $K=1,\ldots,6$, clients compute responsibilities locally and transmit additive mass, precision, information-vector, and symmetric-scatter statistics. The server performs 30 fixed damped updates and selects $K$ by BIC subject to a minimum effective component size. Initialization, likelihood, BIC, and final group moments are also additive.

Finite-difference sensitivities of scheduled controller gains define a positive metric $W_g$. Weighted PCA gives the group backbone
\begin{equation}z_i\approx\mu_g+U_g a_i.\end{equation}
The new client downloads $(\mu_g,U_g)$, selects a group, and estimates scalar $a_i$ locally with a quadratic prior. No new-client trajectory or coordinate is uploaded. This is federated sufficient-statistic estimation, not FedAvg.

\subsection{Trust boundary}
Raw trajectories, individual summaries, and new-client coordinates remain local; the server receives cohort sums and broadcasts mixture/backbone states. Experiments emulate the roles in one process. Updates are compatible with additive secure aggregation, but cryptography and differential privacy are not implemented; no formal privacy claim is made.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
A mature fleet of 180 clients provides 4.75~s complementary records. Separate new clients receive 0.75 or 1.25~s records. Ten frozen fleet seeds are independent confirmation units. Baselines include Local, centralized latent backbones, full and partial federated backbones, one global component, and exact individual parameters. Tests use varying-speed maneuvers and nonlinear plants. Primary tracking is yaw-rate RMSE in rad/s; feasibility requires finite simulation and steering/acceleration limits.

Communication counts independent float64 payload scalars. Unknown-$K$ discovery includes every candidate order; fixed-$K$ update costs are separate. Headers, cryptographic expansion, retransmissions, and a new client's one-off download are excluded.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Structural motivation}
The earlier regime map shows that speed scheduling and persistent specialization solve distinct errors. Those studies use deg/s and are not numerically pooled with the final rad/s results.

\subsection{Blind cold-start confirmation}
At 0.75~s, Fed20 attained 0.01052~rad/s versus 0.01271~rad/s for Local: a 17.27\% reduction by the ratio-of-aggregate-means convention, with complete feasibility. Full and 50\% participation were similarly beneficial. At 1.25~s, Local had sufficient information and the federated advantage contracted to practical parity.

\subsection{Mechanism and participation}
The retrospective ablation assigns most early benefit to the learned group center. Rank-one personalization adds an uncertain increment at 0.75~s but becomes consistently useful relative to the center at 1.25~s. Rank two is not required. Random rotating 20\% cohorts preserve broad cumulative coverage; a persistent narrow subset can corrupt discovery and is the principal observed failure boundary.

\subsection{Communication}
For Fed100, complete unknown-$K$ discovery requires 7.342~MB upload and 4.536~MB download; reusing $K=3$ requires 1.068~MB and 0.648~MB. Fed20 discovery requires 1.502~MB and 0.907~MB; its mean selected-order update requires 0.241~MB and 0.134~MB. These supersede legacy selected-$K$ counts.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Federation supplies an amortized prior from more mature-fleet data; this is not a same-total-data contest. Ten fleets limit tail resolution, and synthetic generating families are used only for evaluation. The gain-weighted basis is not directly optimized for nonlinear tracking. Feasibility and frozen radii are diagnostics, not a nonlinear LPV stability certificate. Formal privacy, networked secure aggregation, richer plants, and real fleet data remain future work.

\section{Conclusion}
Federated latent LPV backbones reduce cold-start control error when local excitation is scarce and rotating participation covers the fleet. Once local data become informative, local fitting recovers parity; without coverage, federation can fail.

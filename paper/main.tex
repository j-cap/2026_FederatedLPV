\begin{abstract}
A vehicle's local calibration data may cover only a small part of its speed
range, even when the fleet collectively observes the complete operating
envelope. This study investigates federated identification of reusable
linear-parameter-varying (LPV) models under such incomplete local coverage.
Speed scheduling represents operating variation, while learned compatibility
groups account for persistent differences between vehicles. Assuming available
lateral states, clients estimate local state/input matrices and contribute
additive regression statistics and assignment scores to learn group LPV maps
without transmitting their trajectories. A nonlinear vehicle benchmark first
justifies a richer scheduling basis and shows that complementary coverage
supplies directions missing from individual client regressions. Under
label-free coverage, federated grouped fitting reduces mean paired fleet
full-envelope matrix error by \IdentLocalGain\% relative to restricted local
fitting and \IdentGlobalGain\% relative to one pooled model. The federated and
centralized grouped solutions agree numerically. Separate scheduled-LQI
confirmation provides a modest tracking benefit, supporting model usability
without making large control gains the primary claim. The evidence uses
controlled perturbations about simulator-computed equilibria, exact current
state regressors and noisy next-state targets. Identification from ordinary
driving records and robustness to state-estimation error remain outside the
validated scope.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
federated identification, LPV systems, incomplete operating coverage,

\section{Introduction}
Vehicles change their dynamics with speed while retaining persistent differences
in configuration, inertial properties and tires. LPV models expose measurable
operating variation to identification and controller design
\cite{toth2010lpv}. A single fleet-wide LPV model may still average incompatible
dynamics. Conversely, independent local identification requires each vehicle
to supply enough operating coverage to estimate its full model.

The information limitation considered here is incomplete scheduling coverage.
Each client observes only a contiguous speed block. The fleet union can span
the full interval, but useful sharing requires both sufficient aggregate
coverage and compatible dynamics. Neither more local repetitions at the same
speeds nor a known vehicle-family label necessarily resolves missing model
directions. We ask whether a federated protocol can construct full-envelope
LPV maps from these complementary local observations.

Federation retains raw trajectories locally and exchanges computed updates
\cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}. Learning several models is an established response to
heterogeneous clients \cite{ghosh2020ifca}. Our question is specifically how
compatible sharing compensates for incomplete operating coverage in a
speed-dependent dynamical model. We do not claim novelty for clustering,
polynomial regression, or additive sufficient statistics in isolation.

The study makes three connected contributions. First, a development-only
basis audit distinguishes a genuinely underdetermined local LPV representation
from a low-dimensional physical model that already extrapolates accurately.
Second, a federated hard-assignment regression protocol identifies compatible
group maps and selects the number of groups while allowing a single pooled
map. Third, finite noisy state-transition experiments quantify whole-envelope
model error, verify centralized--federated equivalence, and assess model
usability through a separate nonlinear LQI tracking test.

The primary contribution is identification. Available states are an explicit
assumption for both fitting and feedback. We exclude KF learning, tire-force
state inference, continuous local personalization and privacy mechanisms from
this formulation. The result should be read as a controlled calibration proof
of concept, with a precisely disclosed operating-point construction.

\section{Problem and Information Assumptions}
\subsection{Speed-dependent models and incomplete coverage}
Let client $i$ have available lateral states $x=[\beta,r]^\top$, steering
input $u$ and measured longitudinal speed $v$. Local state/input perturbations
about a cornering equilibrium approximately satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \Delta x_{i,k+1}=A_i(v)\Delta x_{i,k}+B_i(v)\Delta u_{i,k}+e_{i,k}.
 \label{eq:transition}
\end{equation}
Write the six stacked entries of $[A_i(v),B_i(v)]$ as a row $m_i(v)\in\R^6$.
Compatible clients are represented approximately by a shared map
\begin{equation}
 m_i(v)\approx m_{g_i}(v)=\phi(v)^\top\Theta_{g_i},
 \qquad \Theta_g\in\R^{L\times6},
 \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
where $g_i$ is unknown. Scheduling captures within-client speed dependence;
group differences capture persistent heterogeneity. This is an approximation:
within-group differences and finite-data error remain.

Each client supplies estimated local matrices at speeds in a subset
$\mathcal V_i$. The target is useful prediction throughout
$\mathcal V=[10,30]$ m/s without requiring every vehicle to collect observations
across that interval. Here ``full envelope'' means this speed range along the
audited cornering curve, not the entire nonlinear state/input domain.

\subsection{Frozen representation}
The existing basis-selection experiment retains the span
\begin{equation}
 \phi(v)\sim[1,v^{-1},v^{-2},z,z^2,z^3,z^4]^\top,
 \qquad z=(v-20)/10.
 \label{eq:basis}
\end{equation}
An invertible QR transformation on a fixed 401-point speed grid improves
conditioning without changing that span. The reciprocal terms retain known
speed dependence and the polynomial terms provide residual flexibility.
The selected order is $L=7$; straight-line development data retain $L=3$.
No control comparison selects the basis or its order.

\subsection{What information is available}
The benchmark provides exact current state/input perturbations and noisy
next-state observations. At each available speed, clients estimate
$[A_d,B_d]$ by least squares from 36 independent transitions. Group fitting
uses these matrix estimates and their speeds, not exact matrices, vehicle
parameters or family labels.

However, the transition generator computes the centering equilibrium using
simulator parameters. It then generates perturbations about that point and
subtracts the equilibrium for regression. This is privileged operating-point
setup information. Although an equilibrium might instead be measured in a
controlled experiment, that alternative has not been validated here. The
current results therefore do not establish a state-record-only learning
interface for ordinary sequential driving data. They also do not test noisy
current-state regressors or errors-in-variables identification.

The same clients provide restricted fitting records and receive the resulting
maps. Evaluation at additional speeds tests generalization across operating
conditions. Replicating the procedure on fresh fleets does not establish
transfer of a frozen model library to wholly held-out recipient vehicles.

\section{Federated Compatible LPV Regression}
\subsection{Client statistics and group fitting}
For observed speeds, client $i$ forms a basis matrix
$\Phi_i\in\R^{n_i\times L}$ and local matrix-entry targets
$Y_i\in\R^{n_i\times6}$. For a fixed partition, ridge regression gives
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Theta_g=
 \left(\sum_{i:g_i=g}G_i+\lambda I\right)^{-1}
 \sum_{i:g_i=g}H_i,
 \label{eq:fed}
\end{equation}
where $G_i=\Phi_i^\top\Phi_i$ and $H_i=\Phi_i^\top Y_i$.
These quantities are additive. Consequently, the federated and pooled
centralized solutions are algebraically identical for the same partition,
basis, regularization and arithmetic conventions.

The scheduling coefficients of an unregularized group map are identifiable
when $\rank(\sum_{i:g_i=g}G_i)=L$. Individual $G_i$ may be singular while
the sum has full rank. Regularization creates a numerical solution but does
not create missing information. Full rank also does not remove pooling bias
from genuinely incompatible dynamics.

\subsection{Unknown compatible groups}
The server broadcasts candidate maps. Each client returns its sum of squared
matrix-entry residuals, normalized by a common entry-wise scale derived from
additive target moments. Model fitting and hard residual-minimizing assignment
alternate for at most 30 iterations. Groups must contain at least five clients.
Candidate orders are $K=1,\ldots,5$, with twelve deterministic initializations
for $K>1$. The initialization uses standardized client residual summaries
around the global fit. These summaries are communicated as well.

The existing model-selection score is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{BIC}=n\log(\mathrm{SSE}/n)+p_K\log n,
 \qquad p_K=6LK+K-1,
 \label{eq:bic}
\end{equation}
with $n$ the number of scalar matrix-entry observations. This is the implemented
selection heuristic, not a guarantee of recovering physical families or the
globally optimal partition. In particular, the minimum-size rule does not
guarantee every group has adequate scheduling coverage. A single shared map is
an eligible and sometimes selected result.

\subsection{Communication and trust}
For $L=7$, the symmetric Gram matrix has 28 distinct entries and the
right-hand side has 42. The accounting includes initialization summaries,
group tags, scores, broadcasts and every tested candidate/restart, rather than
only the selected model. The label-free experiment uses a mean
\SearchTraffic~MB per fleet with 64-bit scalars. The exhaustive search is not
demonstrated communication-efficient.

The protocol is evaluated by an emulator. Raw trajectories remain local, but
matrix statistics, residual summaries and scores can reveal information.
Neither cryptographic secure aggregation nor differential privacy is
implemented. Data locality is the supported architectural property.

\section{Identification Evidence}
\subsection{Representation and missing scheduling directions}
The basis audit uses exact discrete Jacobians of the nonlinear bicycle plant
about steady cornering points, with contiguous speed regions withheld during
validation. These exact targets select a representation on development fleets;
they are not the finite-data fitting targets in the later experiments.
At curvature 0.005 m$^{-1}$, mean blocked-speed matrix error falls from
\BasisLowError\% at order three to \BasisHighError\% at order seven.
The straight-line negative control retains order three, preventing numerical
improvement in an already adequate model from rewarding unnecessary terms.

The four local blocks are $\{10,12,14\}$, $\{16,18,20\}$,
$\{22,24,26\}$ and $\{28,30\}$ m/s. Their basis matrices have ranks
three, three, three and two for seven coefficients. The eleven-speed union
has rank seven and condition \UnionCondition. Sharing can therefore add
unobserved scheduling directions rather than only repeat local observations.

\subsection{Finite-data compatibility prerequisite}
With finite nonlinear perturbation transitions, an oracle-family pool achieves
\OracleUnseenError\% matrix error at locally unobserved speeds, compared with
\LocalUnseenError\% for Local and \GlobalUnseenError\% for Global.
Local uses the locally supported reciprocal span; a higher-order locally
regularized alternative does not improve on it in this restricted setting.
This verifies the compatible-coverage mechanism before removing family labels.
It is a centralized oracle comparison, not the proposed federated result.

An equal-information-budget LocalFull reference records the recipient itself
throughout the envelope using approximately the family pool's total transition
budget. It is more accurate than FamilyPool. Sharing is useful because each
ordinary vehicle lacks those records, not because distributed data collection
is inherently better at equal recipient information.

\subsection{Primary label-free identification result}
Ten fresh 30-client fleets randomize speed blocks globally without inspecting
family labels. Learned group fitting is compared with restricted Local and a
single Global LPV map using the same available observations. Table~\ref{tab:id}
reports full-envelope relative matrix errors. These values must not be
relabelled as unseen-speed-only errors.

The federated and centralized protocols select identical group counts and

convergence or identifiability theorem for arbitrary grouped data.

\subsection{Personalization and coverage boundaries}
A separate development sweep increases recipient coverage while freezing its
family backbone. A personalized head initially worsens model error; at eight
and eleven observed speeds it improves prediction by 7.74\% and 13.66\%,
without a positive regulation benefit over the shared family model. At full
coverage independent Local is more accurate. Continuous personalization is
therefore unnecessary for the restricted-coverage contribution studied here.

Known family labels do not ensure sufficient coverage. Under label-free
assignment, one oracle family lacks the highest-speed block in an evaluated
fleet and produces an infeasible full-envelope controller. This is a boundary
of the oracle comparator, not proof that learned grouping always guarantees
coverage. The identified groups must be assessed jointly for fit and information.

\section{Supporting LQI Validation}
Separate confirmation applies the identified maps in speed-scheduled LQI on
nonlinear speed-varying tracking maneuvers. Available physical states provide
feedback. Controller weights were selected using only ExactLPV development
performance, with feasibility and saturation checks; the learned-versus-Global
comparison did not enter selection. Ten untouched fleets follow a disclosed
discarded maneuver pilot. Steering amplitude/rate limits and anti-windup remain
fixed across methods.

nonlinear stability certificate. Four confirmation fleets select one group;
federated and Global methods are then identical. The six remaining fleets
provide the positive mean tracking differences against Global.

\section{Discussion and Limits}
The evidence separates three effects. Complementary coverage supplies missing
scheduling directions. Compatibility specialization reduces pooling bias.
Federation reproduces centralized grouped fitting while retaining trajectories
locally. The main comparison is access to a full-envelope model from restricted
local records, not an equal-total-data contest or a guarantee that grouping
helps every vehicle.

The benchmark still assumes controlled perturbation data, simulator-derived
operating-point centering and available states. It covers one speed-dependent
map along a prescribed cornering curve. Exact Jacobians are used for
development representation selection and evaluation, and current state/input
regressors are uncorrupted. No claim is made for noisy pseudo-states, ordinary
driving records, real fleet data or frozen-library transfer to new recipients.
The model-selection criterion is heuristic, aggregate rank depends on group
coverage, and exhaustive search has measurable communication cost.

The narrower scope also distinguishes this formulation from our earlier
uncertainty-aware clustered identification work \cite{weber2026ifac}. The
present emphasis is missing scheduling information and reusable LPV maps,
with numerical distributed equivalence. A submission-level novelty comparison
must assess that combined contribution; clustering and additive statistics
alone are established components.

If broader state-record-only identification is required, the next validation
should isolate the learning interface using supplied state/input/speed arrays
and empirically available centering. The basis, primary model-error endpoint
and comparison methods should remain fixed. KF learning, higher-order plants
and controller retuning are separate questions and are paused in this study.

\balance
\section{Conclusion}
Compatible federated sharing can identify full-envelope LPV maps when local
speed coverage leaves individual regressions underdetermined. On the controlled
available-state benchmark, label-free grouped fitting improves whole-envelope
matrix error over Local and Global and reproduces centralized fitting.
Scheduled LQI provides supporting confirmation with modest Global-relative
tracking gains. The validated contribution is coverage-dependent model
identification under explicit operating-point assumptions, with general
state-record deployment left for a separate interface validation.

\begin{abstract}
Vehicles may supply calibration data from narrow speed regions while the
fleet collectively covers a wider interval. Pooling can recover missing
scheduling information but mix incompatible dynamics. We study federated
linear-parameter-varying (LPV) identification under these joint limitations.
Clients exchange additive regression statistics and assignment scores to learn
shared group maps. Across ten nonlinear vehicle fleets with family labels
withheld, grouped fitting reduces mean paired full-envelope matrix error by
\SmoothLocalFullGain\% relative to development-tuned Smooth Local and
\IdentGlobalGain\% relative to Global. Mean error at locally unobserved speeds
is \PrimaryFedUnseenError\%, versus \SmoothLocalUnseenError\% and
\PrimaryGlobalUnseenError\%, respectively. The distributed fit reproduces
centralized grouping. Benefits persist under alternative entry scaling but
are not uniform across entries or clients. The benchmark uses controlled
equilibrium-centered calibration with available states, exact current
regressors, and noisy next-state targets. Fresh stress tests expose fleet-wide
coverage failure and loss of grouping gains at lower calibration signal-to-noise.
Speed-scheduled linear-quadratic-integral (LQI) tests show modest gains over
Global; the tested Smooth Local prior yields infeasible responses for some
clients under the frozen controller protocol.
\end{abstract}

federated identification, LPV systems, incomplete operating coverage,

\section{Introduction}
\begingroup\color{\revisioncolor}
Model-based vehicle control requires dynamics models that remain useful as
operating conditions change. For lateral motion, longitudinal speed affects
the relationship between steering, sideslip, and yaw rate. A model identified
near one operating point may therefore be inadequate elsewhere. LPV models
represent this dependence explicitly, making identification across the intended
speed range a central requirement \cite{toth2010lpv,bamieh2002}.

Obtaining that coverage separately for every vehicle is a demanding calibration
task. A vehicle may provide measurements over only a narrow part of its
intended operating range. Those measurements constrain the observed response,
but a flexible speed-dependent model can remain undetermined outside that
region. Repeating measurements at the same speeds reduces noise without
resolving missing scheduling directions. The resulting problem is therefore
incomplete operating information as well as limited sample count.

Fleet collaboration offers a way to fill these gaps: one vehicle's unobserved
speeds may be covered by others. Yet differences in mass, inertia, and tire
properties make their measurements only approximately compatible. Pooling
all vehicles can introduce bias, while separating them too aggressively can
leave each group without sufficient speed coverage. What is needed is selective
sharing that supplies missing operating information without overwhelming a
recipient's dynamics with incompatible data. Crucially, the partners must be
chosen from partial observations, before complete local models are available.

We address this need through federated LPV identification. Vehicles retain
calibration records locally, exchange additive statistics, and evaluate candidate
group maps at their observed speeds. Alternating assignment and regression
learns shared maps over the wider interval. The central question is whether
groups inferred from partial data can provide both complementary coverage
and a useful approximation of their recipients' dynamics.
Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the procedure.

Existing work supplies important components of this approach. Local LPV
identification constructs scheduling-dependent maps from operating-point
models \cite{ferranti2017}. Federated learning provides a framework for
collaboration without centralizing records \cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}, and
FedSysID characterizes the effect of dynamical heterogeneity on collaborative
identification \cite{wang2023fedsysid}. IFCA alternates cluster assignment
and model optimization \cite{ghosh2020ifca}. Clustered SysID applies this
structure to LTI systems with identical dynamics within each true cluster
\cite{toso2023clusteredsysid}. IC-SYSID adds incremental clustering and
stability promotion \cite{kececi2025clustercraft}, while
\cite{weber2026ifac} uses uncertainty-aware grouping for vehicle controller design.

Our distinction is the joint requirement of compatibility and complementary
scheduling information. Each client's fixed speed support leaves its LPV
regression deficient, and shared maps remain approximate within groups.
The cited recovery guarantees do not establish recovery in this setting.
We specialize the established alternating clustering structure to this problem
and make the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item We formulate LPV identification from incomplete local speed coverage in
common physical-state coordinates. A conditional rank statement distinguishes
information supplied by pooling from extrapolation imposed by a local prior.
\item We implement label-free group regression using additive statistics and
local assignment scores, without requiring complete local LPV maps. Agreement
with centralized grouping verifies the distributed implementation.
\item We establish controlled vehicle-fleet evidence for the coverage--bias
tradeoff through comparisons with local priors and global pooling, learned-group
and recipient audits, and coverage and calibration stress tests. Supporting LQI
tests assess whether identification gains translate into tracking improvements.
\end{enumerate}
Sections~II and III present the identification problem and federated regression.
Section~IV describes the benchmark. Sections~V and VI report identification
and LQI tracking results. Section~VII discusses the findings and concludes.
\endgroup

\section{Identification Problem}
\begingroup\color{\revisioncolor}
\subsection{Local calibration information}
Consider a fleet of $M$ vehicles indexed by $i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}$.
The target speed interval is $\mathcal V=[v_{\min},v_{\max}]$, where
$0<v_{\min}<v_{\max}$. Each vehicle has state $x_i=[\beta_i,r_i]^\top\in\R^2$,
containing sideslip angle $\beta_i$ and yaw rate $r_i$, and steering input
$u_i\in\R$. States, steering, and speed are available during calibration.
At a fixed speed $v$, let $x_i^\star(v)$ and $u_i^\star(v)$ denote a prescribed
cornering equilibrium. Define deviations $\Delta x_{i,k}=x_{i,k}-x_i^\star(v)$
and $\Delta u_{i,k}=u_{i,k}-u_i^\star(v)$, where $k$ indexes sampled transitions.
The local discrete-time approximation is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta x_{i,k+1}=A_i(v)\Delta x_{i,k}+B_i(v)\Delta u_{i,k}+e_{i,k},
 \label{eq:transition}
\end{equation}
where $A_i(v)\in\R^{2\times2}$ and $B_i(v)\in\R^{2\times1}$ describe
perturbation dynamics. The residual $e_{i,k}\in\R^2$ includes nonlinear
approximation error and next-state observation noise. Let $m_i(v)\in\R^{1\times6}$ collect these matrix entries at speed $v$,
in the order $[A_{i,11},A_{i,12},A_{i,21},A_{i,22},B_{i,1},B_{i,2}]$.

Vehicle $i$ supplies calibration records at the finite speed set
$\mathcal V_i=\{v_{i,1},\ldots,v_{i,n_i}\}\subset\mathcal V$, where $n_i$
is the number of observed speeds. At each such speed, stack the rows
$[\Delta x_{i,k}^\top,\Delta u_{i,k}]$ into $Z_i(v)$ and the corresponding
observed next-state deviations into $X_{i,+}(v)$. For $N_i(v)$ records,
these matrices have dimensions $N_i(v)\times3$ and $N_i(v)\times2$.
Ordinary least squares (OLS) estimates the local matrices:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat C_i(v)&=\arg\min_{C\in\R^{3\times2}}
 \|X_{i,+}(v)-Z_i(v)C\|_F^2,\\
 [\widehat A_i(v),\widehat B_i(v)]&=\widehat C_i(v)^\top.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:local}
\end{equation}
Here $\|\cdot\|_F$ is the Frobenius norm. We require $\rank Z_i(v)=3$
for a unique local OLS solution. Vectorizing the estimates in the order of
$m_i(v)$ gives $\widetilde m_i(v)$. The local identification data are
$\mathcal D_i=\{(v,\widetilde m_i(v)):v\in\mathcal V_i\}$.

\subsection{Incomplete scheduling information and shared maps}
Let $\phi(v)\in\R^L$ be a fixed vector of $L$ linearly independent scheduling functions,
common to all vehicles. Define $\Phi_i\in\R^{n_i\times L}$ by row
$\ell$ equal to $\phi(v_{i,\ell})^\top$, and $Y_i\in\R^{n_i\times6}$
by row $\ell$ equal to $\widetilde m_i(v_{i,\ell})$, for
$\ell=1,\ldots,n_i$. The setting of interest has
\begin{equation}
 \rank\Phi_i<L.
 \label{eq:local-deficiency}
\end{equation}
Consequently, a nonzero coefficient perturbation $U_i\in\R^{L\times6}$
can satisfy $\Phi_iU_i=0$: coefficient maps that differ by $U_i$ agree
at every locally observed speed but can disagree elsewhere. More records
at those speeds improve the matrix estimates without removing this scheduling
ambiguity. Full rank of $Z_i(v)$ and full rank of $\Phi_i$ therefore address
different requirements: local state/input excitation and speed coverage.

Sharing introduces $K$ group maps, where $g_i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$ is
vehicle $i$'s assignment and $\Theta_g\in\R^{L\times6}$ contains the
coefficients of group $g$. Write
\begin{equation}
 m_i(v)=\phi(v)^\top\Theta_{g_i}+d_i(v),
 \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
where $d_i(v)\in\R^{1\times6}$ represents basis approximation error and
the difference between individual and shared dynamics. Complementary supports
can remove missing scheduling directions in a pooled design. However,
sharing also couples vehicles through a common approximation. The residual
$d_i(v)$ expresses the resulting recipient-dependent mismatch, distinct from
noise in the estimated targets $Y_i$.

\subsection{Identification objective and scope}
Given the distributed datasets $\mathcal D_i$, we seek a group count $K$,
assignments $g_i$, and coefficient matrices $\Theta_g$ that provide each
recipient with a map $\widehat m_i(v)=\phi(v)^\top\widehat\Theta_{g_i}$
throughout $\mathcal V$. The scientific objective is to improve identification
at locally unobserved speeds $\mathcal V\setminus\mathcal V_i$, while limiting
the mismatch introduced by pooling heterogeneous vehicles. Those speeds
have no local fitting targets. Section~III therefore fits observed data and
selects groups from their residuals; the evaluation tests whether this
procedure improves predictions outside each local support.

Vehicles retain calibration records locally and exchange the statistics and
scores defined in Section~III. Vehicle parameters, exact evaluation matrices,
and family labels do not enter that fitting or assignment interface. The
contributing vehicles also receive the maps. We evaluate generalization across
speeds within each fleet, with identification repeated for each fresh fleet.
Transfer of a fixed model library to new recipients is outside this study.
``Full envelope'' denotes the target speed interval along the prescribed
cornering curve. It does not denote an arbitrary nonlinear state/input domain.
\endgroup

\section{Federated Compatible Regression}
\begingroup\color{\revisioncolor}
Using $\Phi_i$ and $Y_i$ from Section~II, we alternate group-map fitting
and vehicle assignment, then select the group count by penalized residual
error. These steps use only the locally observed speeds.

\subsection{Additive fitting and conditional identifiability}
For a fixed partition, let $\mathcal I_g=\{i:g_i=g\}$ be the set of vehicles
in group $g$. Its map minimizes the regularized least-squares objective
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Theta_g=\arg\min_{\Theta\in\R^{L\times6}}
 \left\{\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_g}\|Y_i-\Phi_i\Theta\|_F^2
       +\rho\|\Theta\|_F^2\right\},
 \label{eq:group-objective}
\end{equation}
where $\rho=\lambda+\epsilon$, with ridge strength $\lambda=10^{-8}$
and numerical diagonal adjustment $\epsilon=10^{-12}$. Each matrix target
has equal weight; local estimation covariances are not used, including in
the sensitivity study. Client $i$ computes
$G_i=\Phi_i^\top\Phi_i\in\R^{L\times L}$ and
$H_i=\Phi_i^\top Y_i\in\R^{L\times6}$. The server sums these statistics
within each group and solves
\begin{equation}
 \left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_g}G_i+\rho I_L\right)
 \widehat\Theta_g=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_g}H_i,
 \label{eq:fed}
\end{equation}
where $I_L$ is the $L\times L$ identity matrix. For a fixed partition,
this gives the centralized solution in exact arithmetic without sharing
$Y_i$. Positive regularization ensures a unique solution even with missing
information. Identification from the data requires a separate rank condition.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:rank}
For a fixed nonempty group $\mathcal I_g$, let $\Phi_g$ be the vertical
stack of its local designs $\Phi_i$. The unregularized least-squares fit
($\rho=0$) has a unique minimizer if and only if
$\rank\Phi_g=L$, equivalently
$\rank(\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_g}G_i)=L$.
If, in addition, the targets satisfy $Y_i=\Phi_i\Theta_g$ for every group
member, that minimizer equals $\Theta_g$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof:} The aggregate Gram matrix is $\Phi_g^\top\Phi_g$,
which is positive definite exactly when $\Phi_g$ has full column rank.
Otherwise, a nonzero $U\in\R^{L\times6}$ with $\Phi_gU=0$ can be added
to any minimizer without changing its predictions. For exact shared-map
targets, $\Theta_g$ achieves zero residual and is therefore the unique
minimizer in the full-rank case.

Pooling can therefore resolve the missing directions in Section~II, but
full rank does not remove the mismatch $d_i(v)$. Ridge supplies a solution,
not missing data. The statement does not guarantee group discovery.

\subsection{Assignment and model selection}
Assignment balances the six matrix entries by scaling. For entry
$j\in\{1,\ldots,6\}$, let $\sigma_j$ be its standard deviation across
all observed target rows, using the population divisor $\sum_i n_i$.
The server computes these values from local row counts, entry sums, and
squared sums. Define
$s_j=\max\{\sigma_j,0.05\operatorname{median}(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_6)\}$
and $D=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_6)$.
These scales are positive in the evaluated fleets and fixed during each
search. Given the broadcast maps, client $i$ returns
\begin{equation}
 q_{ig}=\|(Y_i-\Phi_i\widehat\Theta_g)D^{-1}\|_F^2,
 \quad g=1,\ldots,K,
 \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
and is assigned to the lowest-scoring group, with ties choosing the lowest
index. Fitting still uses~\eqref{eq:group-objective}.

For initialization, fit all vehicles as one group to obtain the Global map
$\widehat\Theta_0$. Client $i$ sends its six-entry mean residual
\begin{equation}
 a_i=\frac{1}{n_i}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_i}
 \left[\widetilde m_i(v_{i,\ell})-
 \phi(v_{i,\ell})^\top\widehat\Theta_0\right].
 \label{eq:initial-summary}
\end{equation}
The server standardizes each entry across clients to zero mean and unit
variance, uses unit scale for constant entries, and applies K-means.
This scaling is separate from $D$. No complete local LPV map is needed.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:federated-lpv} summarizes the search. Define the residual sum
$\mathrm{SSE}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}q_{i,g_i}$ and scalar target count
$n=6\sum_i n_i$. The Bayesian information criterion (BIC) score is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{BIC}=n\log\!\left(\max\left\{\mathrm{SSE}/n,10^{-12}\right\}\right)
 +p_K\log n,
 \label{eq:bic}
\end{equation}
where $p_K=6LK+K-1$ counts map coefficients plus a nominal mixture-weight
term. The procedure does not estimate mixture weights; BIC is used as a
heuristic penalty for these noisy, heterogeneous targets. The lowest score
selects the restart and $K$, including global pooling at $K=1$.
Neither global optimality nor physical-family recovery is guaranteed.
Minimum group size does not ensure full scheduling rank, which is checked
separately, not enforced during the search.

\caption{Federated LPV Identification with Learned Groups}
\label{alg:federated-lpv}
\footnotesize
\KwIn{Local designs $\Phi_i$ and targets $Y_i$, $i=1,\ldots,M$;
common basis and regularization $\rho$.}
\KwOut{Selected $K$, assignments $g_i$, and maps $\widehat\Theta_g$,
$g=1,\ldots,K$.}
\Phase{Prepare statistics and initialization summaries}
\textbf{Clients:} send $G_i=\Phi_i^\top\Phi_i$, $H_i=\Phi_i^\top Y_i$,
row counts, and target entry sums and squared sums\;
\textbf{Server:} fit Global $\widehat\Theta_0$ by~\eqref{eq:fed} with all
vehicles; compute $D$ (Section~III-B); broadcast both\;
\textbf{Clients:} send mean residuals $a_i$ from~\eqref{eq:initial-summary}\;
\textbf{Server:} standardize the entries of $a_i$ across clients\;
\Phase{Search group counts and initial assignments}
\For{$K=1,\ldots,5$}{
  Use one start for $K=1$ and 12 seeded starts otherwise\;
  \For{each start}{
    \textbf{Server:} set $g_i=1$ if $K=1$; otherwise initialize
    by K-means on the standardized summaries\;
    \For{$t=1,\ldots,30$}{
      \textbf{Discard this start} if any group has fewer than five vehicles\;
      \textbf{Clients:} send $G_i,H_i$ and current group tags\;
      \textbf{Server:} fit each group by~\eqref{eq:fed}; broadcast all maps\;
      \textbf{Clients:} return scores $q_{ig}$ from~\eqref{eq:score} for every group\;
      \textbf{Server:} assign each $i$ to the lowest-scoring group,
      choosing the lowest index in a tie\;
      \textbf{Break} if no assignment changed\;
    }
    \textbf{Discard this start} if any final group has fewer than five vehicles\;
    If the last iteration changed assignments, refit final groups
    and recompute scores without reassignment\;
    \textbf{Server:} compute BIC by~\eqref{eq:bic}; save this candidate\;
  }
  Keep the lowest-BIC candidate for this $K$, if any\;
}
\Phase{Select and distribute the result}
\textbf{Server:} select the lowest-BIC candidate across $K$;
return each vehicle's assignment and its group map\;
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Exchanged information}
The messages $G_i$ and $H_i$ contain $L(L+1)/2$ distinct Gram entries
and $6L$ right-hand-side entries: 28 and 42 for $L=7$. Communication
accounting also includes row counts, target moments, initialization
summaries, group tags, scores, and model broadcasts. Including all
candidate group counts and restarts, mean label-free traffic is \SearchTraffic~MB
per fleet at eight bytes per scalar. This exhaustive search is not shown
to be communication-efficient. Records and matrix targets stay local in
the distributed emulator, but the shared statistics can reveal information
about them. No secure aggregation or differential privacy is implemented.
The benchmark next tests identification beyond locally observed speeds.
\endgroup

\section{Benchmark and Evaluation}
We test identification beyond observed speeds using local priors,
global pooling, and learned groups.

\subsection{Vehicle fleet and calibration data}
\label{sec:calibration}
The plant is a two-state single-track model with nonlinear tire forces:
\begin{align}
 \dot\beta&=(F_f+F_r)/(mv)-r,\\
 \dot r&=(l_fF_f-l_rF_r)/I_z,\\
 F_a&=\mu F_{za}\tanh(C_a\alpha_a/(\mu F_{za})),\quad a\in\{f,r\}.
\end{align}
Here $m$ is mass, $I_z$ is yaw inertia, and $l_f,l_r$ are the distances
from the center of mass to the front/rear axles. For axle $a$, $F_a$,
$F_{za}$, $C_a$, and $\alpha_a$ denote lateral force, normal load,
cornering stiffness, and slip angle, respectively, with
$\alpha_f=u-\beta-l_fr/v$, $\alpha_r=-\beta+l_rr/v$,
$F_{zf}=mg l_r/(l_f+l_r)$, and $F_{zr}=mg l_f/(l_f+l_r)$.
Gravity is $g=9.81$ m/s$^2$ and the friction coefficient is $\mu=0.9$.
Angles $\beta,u$ are in radians and $r$ is in rad/s.

Each primary fleet (seeds 371--380) has ten vehicles from each parameter
center in Table~\ref{tab:centers}. Mass and inertia have independent uniform
scatter of $\pm2.5\%$, axle stiffnesses $\pm5\%$, and geometry is fixed.
Over $\mathcal V=[10,30]$ m/s, the local blocks
$\{10,12,14\}$, $\{16,18,20\}$, $\{22,24,26\}$, and $\{28,30\}$ m/s
are randomly assigned to eight, eight, seven, and seven clients.
Family labels enter neither this allocation nor learned fitting.

At curvature $\kappa_0=0.005$ m$^{-1}$, simulator parameters determine
$x_i^\star,u_i^\star$ from $r_i^\star=v\kappa_0$ and $\dot\beta=\dot r=0$.
At each observed speed, $N_i(v)=36$ independent records are generated by
resetting to $x_i^\star+\Delta x_{i,k}$ and holding $u_i^\star+\Delta u_{i,k}$
and $v$ for one fourth-order Runge--Kutta step, denoted $\mathcal F_i^{\rm RK4}$,
with sample time $T_s=0.01$ s:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \widetilde{\Delta x}_{i,k+1}
 &=\mathcal F_i^{\rm RK4}(x_i^\star+\Delta x_{i,k},
 u_i^\star+\Delta u_{i,k};v)\\
 &\quad-x_i^\star+\nu_{i,k}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:calibration-record}
\end{equation}
The exact current deviations and noisy next-state targets form $Z_i(v)$
and $X_{i,+}(v)$ in~\eqref{eq:local}, with $\rank Z_i(v)=3$ checked.
Excitations and next-state noise are independent zero-mean Gaussian.
Their standard deviations are approximately
$(0.25^\circ,2^\circ/{\rm s},0.30^\circ)$ for $(\beta,r,u)$ and
$(0.01^\circ,0.03^\circ/{\rm s})$ for $\nu_{i,k}$.
Instrumented commissioning would require steady-cornering measurements,
estimated centers, and sideslip sensing or a validated observer.
These independently reset records do not validate sequential driving,
achievable excitation, or observer-based calibration.

\subsection{LPV representation and comparison methods}
\label{sec:baselines}
The common seven-function basis is
\begin{equation}
 b(v)=[1,v^{-1},v^{-2},z,z^2,z^3,z^4]^\top,\quad z=(v-20)/10.
 \label{eq:basis}
\end{equation}
Stacking $b(v)^\top$ on 401 uniformly spaced speeds gives
$B_{\rm ref}\in\R^{401\times7}$. Its thin QR factorization
$B_{\rm ref}=QR$ has orthonormal columns in $Q$ and invertible
$R\in\R^{7\times7}$. We use $\phi(v)^\top=b(v)^\top R^{-1}$ to improve
conditioning without changing the span.
Blocked-speed validation on development fleets 301--310 selects $L=7$
for cornering using a one-standard-error rule and relative-error floor
$10^{-4}$. The straight-line negative control selects
$[1,v^{-1},v^{-2}]$. Control results do not select the basis.

Restricted Local fits these three functions to each client's targets with
ridge $10^{-8}$. Global pools the fleet targets with $L=7$, while learned
groups select sharing partners. Centralized grouping uses the same data
and objective to check the federated implementation.
Smooth Local tests whether sharing adds value beyond a different local
prior, using $L=7$ and the curvature-penalized fit
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat\Theta_i^{\rm S}=\arg\min_\Theta\Bigl\{&
 \frac{\|Y_i-\Phi_i\Theta\|_F^2}{n_i}\\
 &+\alpha\|Q_2\Theta\|_F^2+\eta\|\Theta\|_F^2\Bigr\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:smooth-local}
\end{equation}
Here $Q_2\in\R^{401\times7}$ factors the 401-point trapezoidal approximation
of $\tfrac12\int_{-1}^1\|d^2[\phi(v(z))^\top\Theta]/dz^2\|_2^2\,dz$,
where $v(z)=20+10z$, and $\eta=10^{-16}$ is numerical ridge.
The curvature term leaves affine
functions in $z$ unpenalized, supplying a prior without restoring data rank.
Development fleets 321--330 select a common $\alpha$ from
$\{10^{-6},10^{-4},10^{-2},1,10^2,10^4,10^6\}$ using unobserved-speed
matrix error. The largest penalty within one standard error of the best
fleet mean is chosen: $10^6$, approaching an affine speed map.

Prior-centered Local is a diagnostic: it fits $L=7$ with penalty
$\rho_i\|\Theta-\Theta_{i,0}\|_F^2$, where $\Theta_{i,0}$ expresses its
own Restricted Local fit in the richer basis. Local leave-one-speed-out
validation chooses $\rho_i>0$ (fixed at $10^{-2}$ for two-speed clients).
If $Y_i=\Phi_i\Theta_{i,0}$ and the numerical diagonal $\epsilon=0$,
the centered solution equals $\Theta_{i,0}$. Near-interpolation motivates
checking this equivalence rather than treating it as a distinct prior.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics and statistical protocol}
Reference matrix vectors $m_i^\star(v)$ come from numerical continuous-time
Jacobians at the calibration equilibria, discretized by an augmented matrix
exponential. They support development-only basis/penalty selection and
evaluation. Recipient fitting uses noisy local targets, not these references.
On $\mathcal V_{\rm grid}=\{10,12,\ldots,30\}$ m/s, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 e_i(v)&=\frac{\|\widehat m_i(v)-m_i^\star(v)\|_2}
 {\|m_i^\star(v)\|_2},\\
 E_s&=\frac{1}{30\cdot11}\sum_i\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_{\rm grid}}e_i(v),
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:error}
\end{equation}
where $s$ indexes fleets. Observed/unobserved errors average their respective
client-speed pairs. To check coordinate sensitivity, we also average
\[
 e_i^{\rm sc}(v)=\sqrt{\frac16\sum_{j=1}^{6}
 \left(\frac{\widehat m_{ij}(v)-m^\star_{ij}(v)}{d_j}\right)^2},
\]
where $d_j$ is the root-mean-square reference entry over development fleets
321--330 and all grid speeds. Entry diagnostics use mean absolute error.
Neither metric directly measures tracking performance.

Reported errors average over ten independent fleets. Gains average
$100(1-E_s^{\rm method}/E_s^{\rm baseline})$ over paired fleets.
Pointwise 95\% confidence intervals resample fleets 20,000 times.
The local-baseline matched check and post-hoc diagnostics reuse the primary
fleets and saved targets, providing no independent confirmation.

\subsection{Coverage and calibration stress tests}
\label{sec:robustness-protocol}
Ten fresh fleets (seeds 1201--1210) test seven predeclared conditions:
a primary-protocol reference, two coverage restrictions, and four calibration
perturbations. All preserve each client's speed count, 36 records per speed,
physical parameters, basis, grouping settings, and Smooth Local penalty.
Perturbation/noise draws are paired by client and speed-slot index.

Family restriction assigns all heavy clients the first two or three speeds
in $\{10,12,14\}$ m/s, leaving other blocks unchanged. Labels enter only
this generator and an OracleFamily diagnostic that pools by true family.
Fleet restriction replaces the four blocks by $\{10,12,14\}$,
$\{16,18,20\}$, $\{10,12,14\}$, and $\{16,18\}$ m/s.
Calibration conditions separately halve excitation standard deviations,
double next-state noise standard deviations, add current measurement noise
with standard deviations $(0.01^\circ,0.03^\circ/{\rm s},0.01^\circ)$
for $(\beta,r,u)$, or bias their assumed centers by
$(0.05^\circ,0.2^\circ/{\rm s},0.1^\circ)$.
Bias signs are independent per component/client and fixed across speeds.
Center bias changes only the subtraction in~\eqref{eq:local}, keeping the
same records and zero-intercept OLS.

After selection, a predeclared fallback replaces rank-deficient group maps
with Global and flags Global deficiency. The main endpoint is fixed-grid
error~\eqref{eq:error}, since coverage changes unobserved sets.
Settings stay fixed and tracking is not tested.
\endgroup

\subsection{Why complementary coverage is needed}
At curvature 0.005 m$^{-1}$, development blocked-speed matrix error falls
from \BasisLowError\% at $L=3$ to \BasisHighError\% at $L=7$.
Figure~\ref{fig:basis-information}(a) contrasts this basis requirement

coefficients, the local blocks have ranks three, three, three, and two;
the development union has rank seven and condition \UnionCondition~
(Fig.~\ref{fig:basis-information}(b)). Complementary coverage can therefore
supply missing directions, conditional on the learned partition.

With finite transitions and known compatibility, oracle-family pooling
achieves \OracleUnseenError\% unobserved-speed error, versus

These oracle results motivate the
mechanism; the primary evaluation below withholds labels.

family pooling at approximately equal transition budget, bounding the
benefit to missing local access rather than superiority at equal recipient
information.}

\section{Supporting LQI Validation}
Ten separate confirmation fleets (seeds 411--420) refit the maps and
apply speed-scheduled LQI to nonlinear 12 s maneuvers with available
noise-free physical-state feedback. Each fleet repeats the partial-speed
calibration above; maps remain fixed during tracking. ExactLPV is a
recipient-specific $L=7$ fit to exact Jacobians on the eleven-speed grid.
At each grid node, the method's fitted $\widehat A,\widehat B$ define
\begin{equation}
 A_{\rm a}=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat A&0\\T_sc_r&1\end{bmatrix},\quad
 B_{\rm a}=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat B\\0\end{bmatrix},\quad c_r=[0,1].
 \label{eq:lqi-augmentation}
\end{equation}
Discrete Riccati synthesis uses $Q=\operatorname{diag}(25,50,1000)$ for
$[\beta,r,\xi]^\top$, with yaw-error integral $\xi$, and steering weight
$R=0.15$. The yaw-reference
prefilter is $F=w+K_xh$, where
$(I-\widehat A)h-\widehat Bw=0$ and $c_rh=1$; $K_x$ denotes the first
two entries of the LQI gain. For Restricted Local at unobserved grid nodes, synthesis
evaluates its matrix map at the nearest observed speed. All methods then
linearly interpolate gains and prefilters on the common grid.
Weights were selected using only ExactLPV performance on development
seeds 381--385, subject to feasibility and saturation checks. A weaker
maneuver pilot on seeds 391--400 was discarded before the final confirmation.
Neither grouped-versus-Global results nor confirmation fleets select weights.
Smooth Local uses the same fleets and targets, frozen $\alpha=10^6$, and
all grid nodes. The common controller protocol remains frozen; this is neither
a separately tuned comparison nor an independent cohort.

With $r_k^{\rm ref}=a_{y,k}^{\rm req}/v_k$, the runtime law is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 u_k^\circ&=-K(v_k)[\beta_k,r_k,\xi_k]^\top+F(v_k)r_k^{\rm ref},\\
 \xi_{k+1}^{\rm cand}&=\operatorname{clip}_{[-0.6,0.6]}
 \{\xi_k+T_s(r_k-r_k^{\rm ref})\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:lqi-runtime}
\end{equation}
Steering is rate-limited relative to $u_{k-1}$, then amplitude-limited.
The integral candidate is accepted when the command is effectively
unsaturated or the integral increment counteracts limiting; otherwise
$\xi_{k+1}=\xi_k$. The integral bound is in radians. Each maneuver starts
with $x_0=0$, $\xi_0=0$, and $u_{-1}=0$; RK4 holds the applied steering
and current speed fixed within each sample.

Feedback acts on absolute states, with reference feedforward from the
homogeneous fitted matrices. Calibration equilibrium offsets are not used
online. The maps describe one cornering curve, whereas the maneuvers vary
the requested turn; tracking therefore tests empirical usability beyond
that curve, rather than exact equilibrium-offset compensation.

The moderate maneuver smoothly varies speed $11\to29\to15$ m/s and
uses an enveloped two-frequency lateral-acceleration demand with scale
4 m/s$^2$ and frequencies 0.35 and 0.72 Hz. The hard maneuver varies speed
$29\to12\to28$ m/s and uses alternating Gaussian demands centered at
$3.0,6.0,9.2$ s, with respective widths $0.40,0.48,0.55$ s and amplitudes
$7,-7.7,5.6$ m/s$^2$. Yaw reference is requested acceleration divided
by speed. Steering limits are $12^\circ$ and $120^\circ$/s, with a
$6^\circ$ sideslip feasibility bound. These remain fixed across methods.
Yaw RMSE uses the complete maneuver, then averages clients within fleet
and the ten fleet means.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
Complementary speed blocks supply missing local directions, while grouping
reduces aggregate pooling error relative to Global. The primary advantage
persists under development-scaled entry weighting, but does not extend to
every entry or recipient. Heavy and handling clients benefit while many
nominal clients deteriorate; learned groups need not recover physical families.
Centralized agreement verifies the distributed implementation.

The robustness study bounds the benefit: minimum group size does not ensure
rank, and Global fallback cannot restore fleet-wide missing information.
Smooth Local wins in that deficient condition; weaker calibration signal-to-noise
can collapse grouping to Global. Full selected-group rank is empirical,
selection is heuristic, and exhaustive search incurs measurable communication.
Ordinary driving, observer errors, real fleets, and frozen-library transfer
remain unvalidated.

The scheduled-LQI tests support usability with modest gains over Global.
The tested Smooth Local prior yields infeasible responses for some clients
under the frozen protocol, illustrating that matrix accuracy alone does not
establish controller suitability. Compatible sharing is useful when pooled
coverage and calibration quality support it, with explicit recipient tradeoffs.

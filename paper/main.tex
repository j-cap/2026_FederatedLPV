\begin{abstract}
\begingroup
\rev{Lateral vehicle control across varying speeds requires models that describe
how steering responses change with longitudinal velocity. Yet each vehicle
may supply} identification data \rev{over} only \rev{a} narrow speed \rev{range. We develop}
\revred{a federated identification method that combines complementary observations
from heterogeneous vehicles into group-specific} linear-parameter-varying (LPV) models.
\rev{Each vehicle estimates} fixed-speed models from \rev{noisy state
measurements} and known applied steering \rev{using} output-error \rev{identification.
Clients then exchange additive} statistics and \rev{model-assignment} scores \rev{to
learn group-specific} LPV \rev{models} without \rev{transmitting} trajectories.
Across ten independent simulated vehicle fleets, mean matrix error at
locally unobserved speeds is \OEFederatedUnseen\%, compared with
\OESmoothLocalUnseen\% for \rev{local identification with smoothness regularization}
and \OEGlobalUnseen\% for \rev{pooling all vehicles}. Diagnostics show that
accurate local estimates alone do not ensure \rev{informative groups.
Closed-loop yaw-rate tracking uses} linear-quadratic-integral (LQI)
\rev{controllers designed from} the \rev{identified models without further adaptation. Under noise-free state feedback,
the proposed method satisfies} the tested constraints and improves mean
tracking over \rev{a} fixed nominal controller. \rev{Additional} gains over \rev{pooling
all vehicles} are small in absolute terms and depend on the recipient.
\endgroup
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\begingroup\color{\revisioncolor}
Model-based vehicle control requires dynamics models that remain useful as
operating conditions change. For lateral motion, longitudinal speed affects
the relationship between steering, sideslip, and yaw rate. A model identified
near one operating point may therefore be inadequate elsewhere. LPV models
represent this dependence explicitly, making identification across the intended
speed range a central requirement \cite{toth2010lpv,bamieh2002}.

Obtaining that coverage separately for every vehicle is a demanding calibration
task. A vehicle may provide measurements over only a narrow part of its
intended operating range. Those measurements constrain the observed response,
but a flexible speed-dependent model can remain undetermined outside that
region. Repeating measurements at the same speeds reduces noise without
resolving missing scheduling directions. The resulting problem is therefore
incomplete operating information as well as limited sample count.

Fleet collaboration offers a way to fill these gaps: one vehicle's unobserved
speeds may be covered by others. Yet differences in mass, inertia, and tire
properties make their measurements only approximately compatible. Pooling
all vehicles can introduce bias, while separating them too aggressively can
leave each group without sufficient speed coverage. What is needed is selective
sharing that supplies missing operating information without overwhelming a
recipient's dynamics with incompatible data. Crucially, the partners must be
chosen from partial observations, before complete local models are available.

We address this need through federated LPV identification. Vehicles keep
trajectories locally, exchange additive statistics, and evaluate candidate
group \rev{models} at their observed speeds. Alternating assignment and regression
learns \rev{speed-dependent models} over the \rev{target} interval. The central question
is whether groups inferred from partial \rev{observations} can \rev{supply} complementary
\rev{speed information while describing} their \rev{members}' dynamics \rev{accurately}.
Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the procedure.

Existing work supplies important components of this approach. Local LPV
identification constructs \rev{speed-dependent models} from operating-point
models \cite{ferranti2017}. Federated learning provides a framework for
collaboration without centralizing records \cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}, and
FedSysID characterizes the effect of dynamical heterogeneity on collaborative
identification \cite{wang2023fedsysid}. IFCA alternates cluster assignment
and model optimization \cite{ghosh2020ifca}. Clustered SysID applies this
structure to LTI systems with identical dynamics within each true cluster
\cite{toso2023clusteredsysid}. IC-SYSID adds incremental clustering and
stability promotion \cite{kececi2025clustercraft}, while
\cite{weber2026ifac} uses uncertainty-aware grouping for vehicle controller design.

Our distinction is the joint requirement of \rev{similar dynamics} and
complementary \rev{speed observations}. Each \rev{vehicle's observations leave} its LPV
regression \rev{underdetermined}, and \rev{vehicles assigned to one group need} not \rev{have
identical dynamics}. We \rev{adapt} the established alternating clustering structure
to this \rev{setting} and make the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}

\item We formulate LPV identification from \rev{limited} local speed \rev{ranges} in
common physical-state coordinates, \rev{distinguishing missing speed} information
from \rev{uncertainty in the fixed-speed estimates}.
\item We develop a federated procedure that \rev{combines local output-error
estimates into group-specific} LPV \rev{models}. Additive statistics and local
assignment scores allow \rev{vehicles to select sharing partners before each
has identified its own model across the target speed range}.
\item We evaluate \rev{identification at locally unobserved speeds} against local
\rev{regularization} and global pooling on ten independent vehicle fleets.
\rev{Closed-loop yaw-rate tracking with controllers designed from} the \rev{learned
models assesses whether improved} identification \rev{also benefits} control.
\end{enumerate}
Sections~II and III \rev{introduce} the identification problem and federated
\rev{method}. Section~IV describes the benchmark \rev{and evaluation protocol}.
Sections~V and VI report identification \rev{accuracy} and \rev{closed-loop} tracking.
Section~VII discusses the findings and \rev{limitations, and Section~VIII} concludes.
\endgroup

\section{Identification Problem}
\begingroup
\rev{Each vehicle needs a model across the target speed range but provides
measurements at only a few speeds. First, we identify a fixed-speed model}
from \rev{each trajectory. Second, we combine these estimates into} an LPV \rev{model,
using other vehicles}' \rev{observations to supply missing speed} information.

\subsection{Local identification from \rev{state measurements}}
Consider $M$ vehicles indexed by $i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}$ and the target
speed interval $\mathcal V=[v_{\min},v_{\max}]$, \rev{where}
$0<v_{\min}<v_{\max}$. Vehicle $i$ has state
$\bs{x}_i=[\beta_i,r_i]^\top\in\R^2$, comprising sideslip angle $\beta_i$
and yaw rate $r_i$, and \rev{scalar} front-wheel steering input \rev{$u_i$.
Both states} are \rev{measured}:
$\bs{y}_{i,k}=\bs{x}_{i,k}+\bs{\nu}_{i,k}$, where $k$ indexes samples
\rev{and $\bs{\nu}_{i,k}\in\R^2$ is measurement noise}.
Applied steering and longitudinal speed are assumed known.

Vehicle $i$ supplies trajectories at $n_i$ observed speeds
$\mathcal V_i=\{v_{i,1},\ldots,v_{i,n_i}\}\subset\mathcal V$.
\revred{At each observed speed $v$, vehicle $i$ records $N_i(v)+1$ state measurements}
$\bs{y}_{i,0},\ldots,\bs{y}_{i,N_i(v)}$, \revred{sampled every $T_s$ seconds,
and the $N_i(v)$ applied steering inputs}
$u_{i,0},\ldots,u_{i,N_i(v)-1}$ \revred{between successive measurements}.
Section~IV specifies \rev{how these data are collected}.

\rev{Output-error (OE) identification estimates an} affine \rev{state-space} model by
comparing its simulated \rev{response} with the \rev{state} measurements. \rev{Suppressing
$i$ and $v$ for} one vehicle--speed pair, write
\begingroup\color{revisiongreen}
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\bs{x}}_{k+1}=\bs{A}\widehat{\bs{x}}_k+\bs{B}u_k+\bs{c},
 \qquad \widehat{\bs{x}}_0=\bs{\zeta},
 \label{eq:transition}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
where $\bs{A}\in\R^{2\times2}$, $\bs{B}\in\R^{2\times1}$,
$\bs{c}\in\R^2$, and $\bs{\zeta}\in\R^2$ are \rev{estimated} jointly.
\rev{For measurement-noise} standard deviations \rev{$\sigma_\beta,\sigma_r>0$, define
$\bs{W}=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_\beta^{-1},\sigma_r^{-1})$}.
With $N=N_i(v)$, the \rev{OE} objective is
\begingroup\color{revisiongreen}
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L(\bs{A},\bs{B},\bs{c},\bs{\zeta})=
 \frac{1}{2(N+1)}\sum_{k=0}^{N}
 \|\bs{W}(\widehat{\bs{x}}_k-\bs{y}_k)\|_2^2,
 \label{eq:local}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
where $\|\cdot\|_2$ is the Euclidean norm and \rev{the simulated state} follows
\eqref{eq:transition} without resetting to \rev{measurements}.
The offset $\bs{c}$ avoids requiring a known equilibrium center, \rev{and
$\bs{\zeta}$ describes the state} at the \rev{start} of the \rev{identification segment,
after the vehicle has approached steady cornering}.

\rev{The estimated matrices $\widehat{\bs{A}}_i(v)$} and
\rev{$\widehat{\bs{B}}_i(v)$ form} the \rev{row vector
$\widetilde{\bs{m}}_i(v)\in\R^{1\times6}$, ordered as
$[\widehat A_{i,11},\widehat A_{i,12},\widehat A_{i,21},
\widehat A_{i,22},\widehat B_{i,1},\widehat B_{i,2}]$.
They supply} the scheduling dataset
\rev{$\mathcal D_i=\{(v,\widetilde{\bs{m}}_i(v)):v\in\mathcal V_i\}$.
The estimated} offsets and initial states remain local.

\subsection{\rev{Missing speed} information and \rev{group models}}
Let $\bs{\phi}(v)\in\R^L$ contain $L$ linearly independent scheduling
functions shared by all vehicles. Define
$\bs{\Phi}_i\in\R^{n_i\times L}$ and $\bs{Y}_i\in\R^{n_i\times6}$
with rows $\bs{\phi}(v_{i,\ell})^\top$ and
\rev{$\widetilde{\bs{m}}_i(v_{i,\ell})$}, respectively.
The setting of interest has
\begin{equation}
 \rank\bs{\Phi}_i<L.
 \label{eq:local-deficiency}
\end{equation}
\rev{Then some} nonzero $\bs{U}_i\in\R^{L\times6}$ \rev{satisfies}
$\bs{\Phi}_i\bs{U}_i=\bs{0}$. \rev{Adding $\bs{U}_i$ to a} coefficient
\rev{matrix leaves predictions} at observed speeds \rev{unchanged} but can \rev{change
predictions} elsewhere. Additional samples at the same speeds cannot remove
this ambiguity. \rev{Exciting the dynamics at a fixed speed} and \rev{observing their
variation with speed} are therefore separate requirements.

For $K$ \rev{group models}, let $g_i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$ assign vehicle $i$ to
a group with coefficients $\bs{\Theta}_{g_i}\in\R^{L\times6}$.
\rev{To describe approximation error, let $\bs{m}_i^\star(v)$ collect the
vehicle's linearized dynamic matrices, defined for evaluation in Section~IV}.
Write
\begingroup\color{revisiongreen}
\begin{equation}
 \bs{m}_i^\star(v)=\bs{\phi}(v)^\top\bs{\Theta}_{g_i}+\bs{d}_i(v).
 \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
\rev{The row vector} $\bs{d}_i(v)\in\R^{1\times6}$ \rev{accounts for} basis
approximation and \rev{differences between a vehicle and its group model.
Pooling} can supply missing \rev{speed information}, but this mismatch \rev{can reduce
the benefit for individual vehicles. It is distinct} from \rev{errors in the}
local \rev{OE estimates caused by measurement noise, finite-amplitude motion,
and numerical optimization}.

\subsection{\revred{Federated identification task}}
Given \rev{the local datasets}, we seek $K$, assignments $g_i$, and coefficients
\rev{$\widehat{\bs{\Theta}}_g$ giving
$\widehat{\bs{m}}_i(v)=\bs{\phi}(v)^\top\widehat{\bs{\Theta}}_{g_i}$
across $\mathcal V$}. Clients exchange the statistics and scores defined
\rev{below. True vehicle} parameters, reference matrices, and \rev{physical} family
labels are excluded from fitting and grouping. Each \rev{vehicle contributes}
its \rev{observed speeds, and evaluation} concerns \rev{predictions at its locally}
unobserved speeds. \rev{The target domain is} the \rev{prescribed} speed interval along
\rev{a cornering condition, rather than} the \rev{full} nonlinear state--input domain.
\endgroup

\section{Federated \rev{Learning of Group-Specific LPV Models}}
\begingroup
Using $\bs{Y}_i$ and $\bs{\Phi}_i$, we alternate \rev{group-model regression}
and \rev{vehicle assignment based on} agreement at observed speeds.
\rev{A complexity-penalized} residual \rev{score} selects the group count.
\rev{We call this procedure \GroupedFL{}. It selects} sharing partners from
partial observations; group coverage is assessed afterward rather than enforced.

\subsection{\rev{Group regression from additive statistics}}
\rev{Given} the \rev{current assignments, define the} vehicles in group $g$ \rev{as
$\mathcal I_g=\{i:g_i=g\}$. Their coefficient matrix} minimizes
\begingroup\color{revisiongreen}
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\bs{\Theta}}_g=\arg\min_{\bs{\Theta}\in\R^{L\times6}}
 \left\{\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_g}\|\bs{Y}_i-\bs{\Phi}_i\bs{\Theta}\|_F^2
       +\rho\|\bs{\Theta}\|_F^2\right\},
 \label{eq:group-objective}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
where $\|\cdot\|_F$ is the Frobenius norm and \rev{$\rho>0$ is a common} ridge
\rev{parameter}. Each speed--matrix target has equal weight. Client $i$ computes
$\bs{G}_i=\bs{\Phi}_i^\top\bs{\Phi}_i\in\R^{L\times L}$ and
$\bs{H}_i=\bs{\Phi}_i^\top\bs{Y}_i\in\R^{L\times6}$.
The server sums these statistics and solves
\begingroup\color{revisiongreen}
\begin{equation}
 \left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_g}\bs{G}_i+\rho\bs{I}_L\right)
 \widehat{\bs{\Theta}}_g=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_g}\bs{H}_i,
 \label{eq:fed}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
where $\bs{I}_L$ is the identity matrix. For \rev{the same assignments}, this \rev{is}
the \rev{centralized ridge-regression} solution in exact arithmetic, without
\rev{transmitting the local targets} $\bs{Y}_i$.

\rev{Let $\bs{\Phi}_g$ stack} the \rev{designs of the} group \rev{members}. The unregularized
fit \rev{is} unique \rev{exactly when} $\rank\bs{\Phi}_g=L$, \rev{since its} Gram matrix
is $\bs{\Phi}_g^\top\bs{\Phi}_g$. \rev{Thus complementary observations} can
resolve missing scheduling directions. \rev{Ridge regularization gives a unique
solution even without full rank, but does not add information. Conditioning,
local estimation} error, and \rev{within-group} mismatch also affect prediction accuracy.

\subsection{\rev{Vehicle assignment} and \rev{group-count} selection}
Assignment compares each candidate \rev{model with} a vehicle's observed dynamics.
\rev{A positive diagonal matrix $\bs{D}\in\R^{6\times6}$} balances the six
entries \rev{using their fleet-wide} standard \rev{deviations, with a small common
floor. It is computed} from local target \rev{moments} and fixed during \rev{the} search.
\rev{Unlike} the \rev{OE weights $\bs{W}$, it describes variation across vehicles.
After receiving the current group coefficients}, client $i$ returns
\begingroup\color{revisiongreen}
\begin{equation}
 q_{ig}=\|(\bs{Y}_i-\bs{\Phi}_i\widehat{\bs{\Theta}}_g)
             \bs{D}^{-1}\|_F^2,\quad g=1,\ldots,K.
 \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
\rev{The lowest score determines its assignment}, with ties \rev{resolved by} the
lowest \rev{group} index. \rev{Regression} uses unscaled \rev{errors whereas assignment
uses scaled errors. The} alternating \rev{procedure is therefore heuristic,
without a common-objective descent guarantee}.

\rev{Initialization first fits} one \rev{model to} all vehicles, \rev{denoted} \GlobalModel{},
\rev{with coefficients $\widehat{\bs{\Theta}}_0$}. Client $i$ sends its mean residual
\begingroup\color{revisiongreen}
\begin{equation}
 \bs{a}_i=\frac{1}{n_i}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_i}
 \left[\widetilde{\bs{m}}_i(v_{i,\ell})-
 \bs{\phi}(v_{i,\ell})^\top\widehat{\bs{\Theta}}_0\right].
 \label{eq:initial-summary}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
The server standardizes \rev{residual entries} across clients and applies K-means,
\rev{using only observed-speed information. Constant entries use unit scale}.

\rev{For} residual sum \rev{$\mathrm{SSE}=\sum_i q_{i,g_i}$} and scalar target count
$n=6\sum_i n_i$, the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) based score is
\begingroup\color{revisiongreen}
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{BIC}=n\log\!\left(\max\{\mathrm{SSE}/n,10^{-12}\}\right)
                 +p_K\log n,
 \label{eq:bic}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
where $p_K=6LK+K-1$ counts \rev{model} coefficients \rev{and} a nominal mixture-weight
term. \rev{This is a heuristic complexity penalty: mixture} weights are not
estimated, and OE target \rev{entries} need not be independent.
\rev{Algorithm~\ref{alg:federated-lpv}} selects the \rev{lowest-scoring candidate},
including \GlobalModel{} at $K=1$. \rev{A minimum} group size \rev{limits small groups
but} does not \rev{guarantee sufficient speed coverage}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]

\SetAlCapFnt{\footnotesize}
\SetAlCapNameFnt{\footnotesize}
\SetKwComment{Phase}{}{}
\SetCommentSty{textbf}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\caption{Federated Learning \rev{of Group-Specific LPV} Models}
\label{alg:federated-lpv}
\footnotesize
\KwIn{\rev{$M$ participating vehicles with local} designs $\bs{\Phi}_i$ and
OE targets $\bs{Y}_i$; common basis \rev{$\bs{\phi}(v)\in\R^L$;
ridge} $\rho$; \rev{maximum groups $K_{\max}$; restart budget $N_{\rm start}$;
iteration limit $T_{\max}$; minimum group size $M_{\min}$}.}
\KwOut{\rev{Group count} $K$, assignments $g_i$, and coefficients
\rev{$\widehat{\bs{\Theta}}_g$}, $g=1,\ldots,K$.}
\Phase{\rev{Initialize from locally observed dynamics}}
\textbf{Clients:} send \rev{$\bs{G}_i,\bs{H}_i$}, counts, and target \rev{moments}\;
\textbf{Server:} fit \GlobalModel{} by~\eqref{eq:fed}; compute $\bs{D}$;
\rev{send $\widehat{\bs{\Theta}}_0,\bs{D}$ to clients}\;
\textbf{Clients:} \rev{return} mean residuals $\bs{a}_i$ from~\eqref{eq:initial-summary}\;
\textbf{Server:} standardize \rev{residual} entries across clients\;
\Phase{\rev{Fit} and \rev{compare candidate groupings}}
\For{\rev{$K=1,\ldots,K_{\max}$}}{
 Use one start for $K=1$ and \rev{$N_{\rm start}$} starts otherwise\;
 \For{each start}{
  \rev{Set} $g_i=1$ \rev{for} $K=1$; otherwise initialize \rev{assignments} by K-means\;
  \For{\rev{$t=1,\ldots,T_{\max}$}}{
   Discard this start if any group has fewer than \rev{$M_{\min}$} vehicles\;
   \textbf{Clients:} send $\bs{G}_i,\bs{H}_i$ and group tags\;
   \textbf{Server:} fit \rev{groups} by~\eqref{eq:fed}; \rev{send} all \rev{coefficients to clients}\;
   \textbf{Clients:} return scores $q_{ig}$ from~\eqref{eq:score}\;
   \textbf{Server:} \rev{update assignments} to the lowest-scoring group\;
   Break if \rev{assignments are unchanged}\;
  }
  Discard this start if any final group has fewer than \rev{$M_{\min}$} vehicles\;
  If \rev{assignments changed at} the \rev{limit}, refit final groups and recompute scores\;
  \rev{Save the candidate and its score}~\eqref{eq:bic}\;
 }
}
\Phase{\rev{Distribute selected models}}
\textbf{Server:} select the lowest-BIC \rev{valid} candidate \rev{and} send \rev{its} assignments
\rev{and group coefficients to the} clients\;
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{\rev{Information exchanged}}
\rev{Clients send $\bs{G}_i\in\R^{L\times L}$ and
$\bs{H}_i\in\R^{L\times6}$}: 28 distinct \rev{symmetric} entries and 42 \rev{entries,
respectively, for $L=7$}. Initialization adds target moments and \rev{residuals.
Assignment exchanges} $K$ scores per client and $K$ \rev{models} of $6L$ \rev{coefficients.
Message size is} independent of trajectory length, \rev{while} total communication
\rev{depends on the search budget}. Trajectories and targets \rev{remain} local in the
emulator, \rev{but shared} statistics \rev{provide no formal} privacy \rev{guarantee}.
\endgroup

\begingroup
\section{Benchmark and Evaluation}
\rev{We test whether} selective sharing \rev{improves identification} at \rev{locally}
unobserved speeds. \rev{Every comparison method receives the same local} OE
\rev{estimates, so differences reflect} sharing \rev{and extrapolation rather than
different} local \rev{estimators. Settings were} fixed after \rev{development on} three
pilot fleets and \rev{then applied to} ten \rev{independently generated evaluation} fleets.

\subsection{Vehicle fleet and local speed coverage}
The \rev{nonlinear} single-track plant \rev{has} state $\bs{x}=[\beta,r]^\top$,
with sideslip measured at the center of mass, \rev{and steering input $u$}.
At fixed longitudinal speed $v$, its dynamics are
\begin{align}
 \dot\beta&=(F_f+F_r)/(mv)-r,\\
 \dot r&=(l_fF_f-l_rF_r)/I_z,\\
 F_a&=\mu F_{za}\tanh(C_a\alpha_a/(\mu F_{za})),\quad a\in\{f,r\}.
\end{align}
Here $m$ is mass, $I_z$ is yaw inertia, and $l_f,l_r$ are the distances
from the center of mass to the front/rear axles. For axle $a$, $F_a$,
$F_{za}$, $C_a$, and $\alpha_a$ denote lateral force, normal load,
cornering stiffness, and slip angle, with
$\alpha_f=u-\beta-l_fr/v$, $\alpha_r=-\beta+l_rr/v$,
$F_{zf}=mg l_r/(l_f+l_r)$, and $F_{zr}=mg l_f/(l_f+l_r)$.
Gravity is $g=9.81$ m/s$^2$ and friction coefficient $\mu=0.9$.
Angles are in radians and \rev{yaw rate} in rad/s.

Each fleet contains 30 vehicles, ten from each parameter center in
Table~\ref{tab:centers}. Mass and inertia have independent uniform scatter
of $\pm2.5\%$, axle stiffnesses $\pm5\%$, and geometry is fixed.
\rev{The target interval is} $\mathcal V=[10,30]$ m/s. \rev{Local speed} blocks
$\{10,12,14\}$, $\{16,18,20\}$, $\{22,24,26\}$, and $\{28,30\}$ m/s
are randomly assigned to eight, eight, seven, and seven \rev{vehicles,
without using family} labels. \rev{Thus 23 vehicles observe three speeds and
seven observe two, giving $23\cdot3+7\cdot2=83$} vehicle--speed datasets \rev{per fleet}.

\subsection{\rev{Identification trajectories} and local \rev{estimation}}
\label{sec:calibration}
\rev{At each} observed speed, a 44-s maneuver \rev{starts} at $\beta=r=0$.
\rev{One nominal} LTI model, linearized at straight driving and 20 m/s \rev{using
the Nominal parameter center}, supplies \rev{an} LQI \rev{controller} for all vehicles
and speeds. The \rev{state--integral} weights \rev{are}
$\bs{Q}=\operatorname{diag}(25,50,1000)$ and \rev{the} steering \rev{weight is}
$R_u=0.15$. \rev{The integral of} measured yaw-rate error is \rev{bounded by}
$\pm0.6$ rad with conditional anti-windup. \rev{Steering} is limited to
$\pm12^\circ$ and $30^\circ$/s.

\rev{During 0--4 s, the} yaw-rate reference \rev{increases smoothly from zero} to
$v\kappa_0$, \rev{where} $\kappa_0=0.005$ m$^{-1}$ \rev{is the target curvature.
The reference remains constant until 24 s, allowing the vehicle to approach
steady cornering. Only the final} 20 s \rev{are used} for \rev{identification.
In this segment, with local time $\tau=t-24$ s, the reference} is
\begingroup\color{revisiongreen}
\begin{equation}
 r^{\rm ref}(\tau)=v\kappa_0+\frac{a_{\rm exc}}{v}h(\tau),
 \qquad a_{\rm exc}=0.3~\mathrm{m/s^2}.
 \label{eq:acquisition-reference}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
The unit-peak waveform $h$ sums sinusoids at 0.2, 0.5, 0.9, and 1.4 Hz
with \rev{reproducibly randomized} phases. \rev{Two-second} sine-squared tapers \rev{switch
the variation smoothly on and off.} \revred{A separate maneuver with a 0.15--1.2 Hz
chirp supports local-model diagnostics and trajectory checks without entering
model estimation or selection}.
\rev{The LQI controller computes steering
from the reference} and \rev{measured states; sideslip follows} the \rev{vehicle dynamics}.

\revred{The 20-s identification segment provides} 2001 state \rev{measurements}
and 2000 applied inputs \revred{at $T_s=0.01$ s, giving $N_i(v)=2000$}.
Measurement noise is independent zero-mean Gaussian across samples and
components, with standard deviations $0.01^\circ$ for \rev{sideslip} and
$0.03^\circ$/s for \rev{yaw rate. Both feedback and OE use} these noisy \rev{state
measurements}. OE uses the known applied steering, without input measurement
noise. \rev{RK4} integrates the \rev{nonlinear dynamics} with steering held within each step.
OE \rev{minimizes~\eqref{eq:local} using four initializations} and \rev{selects} the
converged solution with the lowest training loss.

\revred{Both maneuvers must satisfy steering bounds, $|\beta|\leq5^\circ$, and
$|a_y|\leq6$ m/s$^2$, where $a_y=(F_f+F_r)/m=v(\dot\beta+r)$ at fixed speed.
Yaw-rate error and state drift must indicate steady cornering during the final
2 s before identification. Limiter activation is allowed because applied steering
is recorded. Failed fits or checks invalidate the fleet condition without removing
or replacing individual vehicles.}

\subsection{LPV representation and comparison methods}
\label{sec:baselines}
The common basis \rev{combines inverse-speed terms from the bicycle model with
polynomial terms that allow additional variation around cornering}:
\begin{equation}
 \bs{b}(v)=[1,v^{-1},v^{-2},z,z^2,z^3,z^4]^\top,
 \quad z=(v-20)/10.
 \label{eq:basis}
\end{equation}
\rev{A fixed QR transformation} on \rev{a dense speed grid defines the normalized
basis $\bs{\phi}(v)$, improving} conditioning without changing \rev{its} span.
Earlier blocked-speed validation \rev{on development} reference matrices selected
\rev{these seven functions} for cornering and $[1,v^{-1},v^{-2}]$ for straight
driving (Fig.~\ref{fig:basis-information}(a)).

\rev{The comparisons test three alternatives to selective sharing}.
\RestrictedLocal{} \rev{asks whether} a \rev{simpler speed dependence suffices:
it fits} the three straight-driving functions to each \rev{vehicle's own estimates}
with ridge $10^{-8}$. \GlobalModel{} \rev{tests whether sharing} all \rev{observations
is sufficient, fitting one seven-function model to the complete fleet}.
\SmoothLocal{} tests whether smoothness \rev{can replace collaboration},
using the \rev{same seven-function basis but only the recipient's own observations}:
\begingroup\color{revisiongreen}
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat{\bs{\Theta}}_i^{\rm S}=\arg\min_{\bs{\Theta}}\Bigl\{&
 \frac{\|\bs{Y}_i-\bs{\Phi}_i\bs{\Theta}\|_F^2}{n_i}\\
 &+\alpha\|\bs{Q}_2\bs{\Theta}\|_F^2+
 \eta\|\bs{\Theta}\|_F^2\Bigr\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:smooth-local}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
Here \rev{$\bs{Q}_2$ implements} the \rev{integrated squared second derivative} of
\rev{the matrix functions with respect to normalized speed $z$.
It penalizes curvature while leaving affine functions unpenalized};
$\eta=10^{-16}$ is numerical ridge. \rev{Development selected
$\alpha=10^6$ as} the largest penalty within one standard error of the best
mean \rev{unobserved-speed error. All seven tested values
$\{10^{-6},10^{-4},10^{-2},1,10^2,10^4,10^6\}$ are also evaluated in a
sensitivity analysis, without changing the primary setting}.

\rev{\GroupedFL{} follows Section~III with $K_{\max}=5$, $N_{\rm start}=12$,
$T_{\max}=30$, $M_{\min}=5$, and $\rho=10^{-8}+10^{-12}$.
These} fixed \rev{search budgets allow multiple initializations and early stopping
at unchanged assignments}.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics and statistical \rev{comparisons}}
Reference \rev{matrices are obtained} from \rev{the known nonlinear simulation model.
At each speed, we find steady cornering with yaw rate} $r_i^\star=v\kappa_0$,
\rev{linearize the dynamics with respect to state and steering, and discretize
the resulting matrices} at $T_s$ \rev{using a zero-order hold. Their entries form
$\bs{m}_i^\star(v)$ in the same order as the estimates}.
True \rev{recipient} parameters \rev{are used for simulation and} evaluation \rev{only},
not \rev{for OE identification, grouping, or} the \rev{nominal data-collection} controller.

On $\mathcal V_{\rm grid}=\{10,12,\ldots,30\}$ m/s, define relative
matrix error and \rev{mean} unobserved-speed \rev{error for} fleet \rev{$s$} as
\begingroup\color{revisiongreen}
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 e_i(v)&=\frac{\|\widehat{\bs{m}}_i(v)-\bs{m}_i^\star(v)\|_2}
                    {\|\bs{m}_i^\star(v)\|_2},\\
 E_s^{\rm U}&=\frac1M\sum_{i=1}^M\frac1{|\mathcal U_i|}
                    \sum_{v\in\mathcal U_i}e_i(v),
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:error}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
where $\mathcal U_i=\mathcal V_{\rm grid}\setminus\mathcal V_i$ and $M=30$.
Speeds are averaged within \rev{vehicles, vehicles} equally within fleets, and
fleets equally across the experiment. Observed-speed and full-grid errors
\rev{use} $\mathcal V_i$ and $\mathcal V_{\rm grid}$ \rev{instead}.
Separate $\bs{A}$, $\bs{B}$, and entrywise errors \rev{test} whether gains extend
beyond the combined, state-unit-dependent metric.

For each primary baseline \rev{$b\in\{\text{\SmoothLocal{}},\text{\GlobalModel{}}\}$,
use} the paired \rev{fleet} difference
\rev{$\Delta_s^{(b)}=100(E_s^{{\rm U},b}-E_s^{{\rm U},{\rm GF}})$}
in percentage points. \revred{Here, $\mathrm{GF}$ denotes \GroupedFL{}.}
Two Bonferroni-adjusted 97.5\% two-sided \rev{paired}-$t$
intervals \rev{provide at least 95}\% simultaneous \rev{coverage} under the paired-$t$
assumptions. \rev{Fleets are the independent} sampling units. \rev{Improvement} requires both lower
bounds above zero. \rev{Mean paired percentage} gains are reported descriptively.

\section{\rev{Closed-Loop Yaw-Rate Tracking with Identified Models}}
\begingroup
\rev{We assess whether the identified models support useful controller} design.
\rev{The same 300 evaluation vehicles now perform} tracking \rev{maneuvers} without
\rev{further model fitting or controller-weight tuning}. \NominalLTI{} tests
improvement over the \rev{data-collection} controller, \rev{and} \GlobalModel{} tests
\rev{the additional benefit of} selective sharing.

\subsection{Common controller design}
The four identification methods \rev{supply scheduled LQI controllers.
A} privileged \rev{\ReferenceLPV{} model fits the seven-function basis} to \rev{each
recipient's} full reference grid. \rev{It provides} a \rev{comparison}, not a performance
bound. \NominalLTI{} keeps the \rev{original} 20-m/s gain and prefilter.
\rev{Scheduled methods synthesize gains} at 10, 12, \ldots, 30 m/s.

At each node, the \rev{identified dynamic matrices} define
\begingroup\color{revisiongreen}
\begin{equation}
 \bs{A}_{\rm a}=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{\bs{A}}&\bs{0}\\T_s\bs{c}_r&1\end{bmatrix},
 \quad \bs{B}_{\rm a}=\begin{bmatrix}\widehat{\bs{B}}\\0\end{bmatrix},
 \quad \bs{c}_r=[0,1].
 \label{eq:lqi-augmentation}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
Discrete Riccati synthesis uses the \rev{weights from Section~\ref{sec:calibration}
and yields $\bs{K}=[\bs{K}_x,k_\xi]\in\R^{1\times3}$ for} state
$[\beta,r,\xi]^\top$, where $\xi$ integrates yaw-rate error.
\rev{For zero-integral equilibrium of} the \rev{fitted model at} unit yaw reference, \rev{solve}
\begingroup\color{revisiongreen}
\begin{equation}
 (\bs{I}_2-\widehat{\bs{A}})\bs{h}-\widehat{\bs{B}}w=\bs{0},
 \quad \bs{c}_r\bs{h}=1,\quad F=w+\bs{K}_x\bs{h},
 \label{eq:prefilter}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
\rev{with} $\bs{h}\in\R^2$ and \rev{scalar $w$. Then $\bs{x}_{\rm eq}=\bs{h}\bar r$} and \rev{$u_{\rm eq}=w\bar r$
satisfy} the \rev{model and feedback law at constant reference $\bar r$ and $\xi=0$}.
Gains $\bs{K}$ and \rev{scalar} prefilters $F$ are linearly interpolated between nodes.
\rev{Synthesis uses the dynamic matrices, while OE} offsets and initial states
remain \rev{parameters of the local identification problems}.

For \rev{yaw-rate} reference $r_k^{\rm ref}$, the \rev{command and candidate integral are}
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 u_k^\circ&=-\bs{K}(v_k)[\beta_k,r_k,\xi_k]^\top+F(v_k)r_k^{\rm ref},\\
 \xi_{k+1}^{\rm cand}&=\operatorname{clip}_{[-0.6,0.6]}
 \{\xi_k+T_s(r_k-r_k^{\rm ref})\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:lqi-runtime}
\end{equation}
Steering is rate-limited relative to $u_{k-1}$, then amplitude-limited.
Conditional anti-windup accepts \rev{the integral update} only when the command
is unsaturated or the increment counteracts limiting.
\rev{Unlike identification, these control tests use noise-free state feedback
to isolate} the \rev{consequences of the learned models}.

Two supporting 12-s \rev{stress} tests prescribe $11\to29\to15$ and

$10\times30\times2=600$ runs per method.} \rev{\GroupedFL{} satisfies} the bounds
in all 600 \rev{runs}.
Mean paired fleet-relative gains over \NominalLTI{} are
\OECtrlNominalModerateGain\% and \OECtrlNominalHardGain\%, versus
\OECtrlGlobalModerateGain\% and \OECtrlGlobalHardGain\% over \GlobalModel{}.
\rev{In} these stress tests, all 100 heavy-family \rev{vehicles} improve over
\GlobalModel{} and all 100 handling-family \rev{vehicles} worsen.
\rev{The control benefit therefore depends on the maneuver and} recipient,
\rev{even when fleet-average identification improves}.
\endgroup

\section{\rev{Discussion}}
\begingroup
\rev{The results support sharing observations that are both dynamically similar
and complementary in speed. Local accuracy alone does not resolve missing
scheduling directions, and physical family membership does not ensure a
well-conditioned group regression. The current assignment procedure does
not enforce these information requirements. Recipient-level coverage and
conditioning checks are therefore important before using a shared model}.

\rev{Closed-loop gains over \GlobalModel{} are small and recipient-dependent.
The tests use simulated vehicles, common controller weights, and noise-free
state feedback}.
Prescribed-speed \rev{lateral dynamics omit} longitudinal tire forces, braking
load transfer, and combined slip, \rev{so the variable-speed tests} are \rev{scheduling
stress tests rather than validation of complete maneuver feasibility.
Nonlinear and} varying-speed stability, \rev{observer-based control}, and \rev{full path
tracking remain untested. All vehicles contribute local data}, so transfer
to new vehicles \rev{requires separate evaluation}.
\endgroup

\section{Conclusion}
\begingroup
\revred{Limited local speed coverage leaves each vehicle's LPV model underdetermined.
We address this by sharing complementary observations among vehicles with similar
dynamics. Local OE identification supplies fixed-speed estimates, which are combined
into group-specific LPV models without exchanging trajectories.}
Across ten independent simulated fleets, mean error at locally unobserved
speeds is \OEFederatedUnseen\%, compared with \OESmoothLocalUnseen\%
for \SmoothLocal{} and \OEGlobalUnseen\% for \GlobalModel{}.
\rev{Controllers designed from the learned models improve mean yaw-rate tracking
over a fixed nominal controller} and satisfy the tested constraints in all
1500 \rev{grouped-model runs}. \revred{Additional tracking gains over global pooling
are small and recipient-dependent.} Future work should assess measured vehicle data
and develop recipient-level checks for harmful transfer before \rev{controller} deployment.
\endgroup

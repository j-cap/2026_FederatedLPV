Can vehicles learn useful models beyond their own observed speed?

Purpose: test whether information from peers improves four-state physical-model prediction at speeds absent from a vehicle's own data. This is an exploratory identification experiment. The existing controller issue and manuscript are outside its scope.

Population and data access

Use nine existing vehicle realizations from each of pilot fleets 1301, 1302 and 1303: nominal_00…02, heavy_00…02 and handling_00…02. Mass, inertia, stiffness and axle geometry are copied into a frozen configuration and checked against their original source. No previous estimated parameters or broad-speed fits are reused.

Each vehicle receives exactly one 12 s broadband steering record at 13, 21 or 29 m/s. Each generating family has one vehicle at each speed. Assignments and opaque client IDs are randomized by a frozen seed before execution. The learning routines receive no family labels or physical ground truth. This balanced allocation is a controlled simulation design, not evidence that deployment data have this distribution.

Retain the previous nonlinear tanh-force model, force relaxation lengths 0.4/0.7 m, 10 ms sampling, RK4 substeps ≤1 ms, known applied steering, yaw noise standard deviation 0.03 deg/s and known settled zero initial states. Steering has 0.35° peak, 0.35/0.7/1.2 Hz components and one-second endpoint tapers. All 1201 yaw samples enter the local loss. No sideslip, force or acceleration measurements enter identification.

The generating relaxation lengths are identical across vehicles. Sharing those parameters is therefore favored by this simulation assumption. We are not testing transfer across different tire-relaxation characteristics, and no new heterogeneity is added for this pilot. A failed training operating check is saved and stops the affected fleet; it never silently replaces or drops a vehicle.

Model and objective

For each vehicle, zᵢ is the elementwise logarithm of θᵢ=[Cf/m, Cr/m, m/Iz, μ, σf, σr]. Lᵢ(zᵢ) is the sum over local samples of squared nonlinear yaw prediction errors divided by the yaw-noise variance. Equal record lengths give equal observation counts per client.

F = Σᵢ Lᵢ(zᵢ) + Σᵢ ‖W(zᵢ − ηgᵢ)‖²
W = diag(10, 10, 10, 0, 4, 4)

ηg is a learned group center in log-parameter coordinates. The log scales 0.1 for the three CT ratios and 0.25 for the two relaxation lengths are fixed engineering choices; their weight is not selected from evaluation data. μ remains a free local parameter and has zero sharing weight, because the preceding gentle-excitation pilot did not identify it reliably. Global, which uses one complete common parameter vector, does estimate a common μ.

All methods use the same physical bounds as the preceding pilot. Three common starting guesses are fixed in the configuration; they do not depend on the vehicle, its family, its true parameters or older fits. LocalOE selects the lowest training SSE among converged starts. The nominal prior is the same vector for every vehicle, including nominal lengths 0.6/0.6 m rather than the generating 0.4/0.7 m.

These methods share one physical model class and differ in access to peer information and regularization. The global/federated methods receive the same nine local records within a fleet. Fleets are processed separately; they never share data or parameters with one another.

Federated implementation

Global: at a broadcast candidate z, each client computes its loss, Jᵀr and the upper triangle of JᵀJ. The server adds these messages and performs a bounded damped Gauss–Newton step. Backtracking uses scalar client losses. This matches the centralized objective and Gauss–Newton information algebraically, without uploading trajectories. Three starts are compared by training loss; each has at most 50 steps.

FedPersonal/FedGroups: start with local OE solutions. Initialize one center by averaging log parameters, or three centers by k-means in W-scaled log coordinates, with two fixed initialization seeds. Each round broadcasts the assigned center. Each client solves its exact nonlinear regularized OE problem, warm-started from its previous parameters. Clients return their six log parameters and local data SSE. The server reassigns each client to its nearest center in the W distance, then recomputes means within groups. Friction does not affect distances or the penalty.

Use at most eight rounds and 25 function evaluations per local round update. A finite update must not increase its own regularized objective. The complete alternating objective is checked for increases. Convergence requires stable assignments, small relative objective change and parameter movement, and converged local optimizers. Empty centers stay unused; active group counts are recorded. K=3 is a fixed pilot choice, not a learned group count or a claim of automatic model selection. The two group initializations are selected only by regularized training objective.

Finite federation models at the iteration budget are evaluated with an explicit budget_exhausted status. They must not be described as converged. Numerical failures remain unavailable, with no fallback model. If any local OE fit is unavailable, the two personalized algorithms cannot initialize and are marked unavailable for that fleet. Global can still run from common starting guesses.

This is a single-machine emulation of client-local computations and server messages. It is not an actual network deployment, secure aggregation, or a privacy guarantee. Logical payload accounting counts float64 values in both directions and includes attempted starts and backtracking; transport overhead, integer assignments and status fields are excluded. Global uses 28 uplink floats per information message; personalized rounds use seven uplink floats per client.

Use two held-out steering records at every speed, with disjoint frequencies/phases and independent yaw noise. The slow probe uses 0.16/0.42 Hz and the broadband probe 0.45/0.85/1.35 Hz. No test observation enters optimization, group initialization, stopping, regularization strength or method selection. Clean yaw and sideslip are used only for evaluation. There are 27 vehicles × 7 speeds × 2 probes × 6 methods = 2268 prediction comparisons.

Report clean-yaw prediction RMSE first, paired by client/speed/probe and summarized by fleet. Also save measured-yaw RMSE, sideslip prediction error, parameter errors, bound flags and optimizer diagnostics. Withhold unconditional means when a model is unavailable; preserve finite-case means and counts explicitly. Inspect learned group composition by both speed and family only after fitting.

Credit fleet information only when it improves peer-speed prediction beyond both LocalOE and NominalPrior without unacceptable local degradation. Credit grouping only if it adds to FedPersonal. A good Global result may make grouping unnecessary. A strong LocalOE result may leave little benefit from sharing. No outcome is assumed, and three previously used pilot populations do not support confirmatory statistical claims.

Efficiency and resumability

Three worker processes handle the three fleets independently, with BLAS/OpenMP threads limited internally to one per process. Nonlinear simulation batches derivative candidates. During Global optimization, model predictions and Jacobians for the common prescribed input are computed together for the three public speeds and cached across clients. Only simulated responses are cached; private measurements and residuals remain client-specific. Unit tests verify equality with independent client computation.

Checkpoint after acquisition, every local start, every global step, every personalized client update and every evaluation trajectory. Restarting skips completed work, including partial rounds. Evaluation batches all available methods in one simulation call per test trajectory. Raw arrays are compressed and ignored by git; model parameters, hashes, metrics, provenance and selected previews are committed.

Runtime depends on convergence and hardware, so no fixed completion time is promised. The output prints each saved stage. No package installation or manual thread-limit command is needed in the existing environment.

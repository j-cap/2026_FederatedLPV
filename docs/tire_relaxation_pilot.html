Small controlled comparison of tire-force relaxation models

Purpose: determine whether representing tire-force dynamics improves held-out prediction and yaw-rate tracking, and whether a local yaw-only estimator can recover a useful augmented model. This pilot does not test federation.

Scope and model

Use nominal_00, heavy_00 and handling_00 from pilot seed 1301. The nonlinear plant retains the existing tanh tire law, known axle geometry and physical ratios p₁=Cf/m, p₂=Cr/m, p₃=m/Iz and μ. It adds normalized axle-force states qf=Fyf/m and qr=Fyr/m, with dqj/dt=(v/σj)(qj,ss−qj). The generating relaxation lengths remain σf=0.4 m and σr=0.7 m. No new Pacejka coefficients, load transfer, actuator lag or heterogeneity is introduced.

All runs use fixed positive speed, 10 ms samples, zero-order-held applied steering and RK4 substeps of at most 1 ms. Only yaw measurement noise is injected (0.03 deg/s standard deviation). All states start at zero after an assumed settled straight-running initialization. That known initial condition is a deliberate pilot assumption, not inferred from noisy yaw or a claim about arbitrary driving records.

Six matched methods

Exact models are privileged parameter references, not guaranteed lower bounds on nonlinear control cost. The newly fitted two-state baseline prevents a comparison against an unnecessarily stale baseline. The four-state conditional and joint fits distinguish representation error from compensation for erroneous CT ratios.

Identification and finite parameter checks

Reuse the nine saved 12 s broadband steering records at 13, 21 and 29 m/s: three records per vehicle. Minimize the sum of squared yaw prediction residuals divided by the yaw noise variance across all three speeds. Predictions use the full nonlinear tanh model, known applied steering and known zero initial conditions. No sideslip/force observations, offsets, test trajectories or generating parameters enter the optimizer. μ remains free in both joint fits; bound hits and weak sensitivity are reported rather than interpreted as friction recovery.

Three deterministic starts per fitted method, chosen before evaluation. Select the lowest training SSE among converged starts only; retain all starts, including failures. Use positive log parameters, bounded least squares and complex-step Jacobians of the same RK4 recursion. Bounds: p₁,p₂ ∈ [20,100], p₃ ∈ [0.25,1.2], μ ∈ [0.4,1.5], σf,σr ∈ [0.15,1.5] m. Maximum 100 residual evaluations per start. These are simulation search bounds, not certified physical uncertainty intervals.

For each selected joint fit, hold each relaxation length at 0.75, 1 and 1.25 times its estimate and reoptimize the other five parameters. The center reuses the selected fit. This adds at most 12 optimizer runs across the three vehicles. Out-of-bound points are recorded as unavailable. Sparse profiles use one local start and are not global profile likelihoods or confidence intervals. A negative profile delta SSE indicates an optimizer issue; the original selected model is preserved for review.

Held-out prediction and closed-loop tests

At 13, 17, 21, 25 and 29 m/s, evaluate two independent steering probes with frequencies/phases distinct from training. The slow probe uses 0.16/0.42 Hz; broadband uses 0.45/0.85/1.35 Hz. Both have 0.35° peak steering and one-second sine-squared endpoint tapers. Their noisy yaw records are evaluation-only; clean yaw and sideslip are used only for error measurement. No warmup fitting or model selection uses these records. This gives 180 model-prediction cases.

At the same five speeds, run two 12 s tapered sinusoidal yaw references: 0.2 Hz and 0.7 Hz, normalized so peak v·rref=1 m/s². These are diagnostic fixed-speed references, not a standardized lane-change maneuver. Each of six methods controls the identical nonlinear four-state plant, giving 180 closed-loop runs. Noise is paired across methods and independent of training and open-loop validation.

Design each model's LQI controller and linear Kalman observer at straight running with exact-ZOH matrices. Use the existing cost weights 25 on β², 50 on r², 1000 on squared yaw-error integral and 0.15 on steering². The extra force states have zero direct LQI penalty. The prefilter is calculated from the model's unit-yaw equilibrium and the complete state gain. Only measured yaw enters the online observer and integrator; observer predictions use the applied steering after limits.

Observer Q is fixed before evaluation: per-step standard deviations 0.002° in β, 0.01°/s in yaw and, for augmented models, 0.02 m/s² in each normalized force state. R is the simulated yaw sensor variance and P₀=0. These are engineering allowances, not injected process noise or fitted covariances. The force allowance is an added design assumption, and this pilot does not test its robustness. Comparisons assess the complete controller/observer design, not only the state-feedback gain.

Steering limits: 12° and 120°/s; integral bound 0.6 rad with conditional integration. Feasibility requires peak sideslip ≤5° and peak lateral acceleration ≤6 m/s². All finite violations remain in full-duration metrics. Synthesis/fit/numerical failures are explicit, have no fallback, and prevent unconditional aggregate means. Report tracking, sideslip-estimation error, steering effort, limiting, feasibility and failure counts before drawing conclusions. Also save the true-plant linearized closed-loop spectral radius with the steady observer gain and inactive limits as an offline diagnostic; it is not a nonlinear stability certificate.

Run locally

Use the existing federated_lpv environment. No new dependency or manual thread-limit command is needed.

git pull --ff-only origin main
python code\experiments\paper_tire_relaxation_pilot.py preflight
python code\experiments\paper_tire_relaxation_pilot.py smoke
python code\experiments\paper_tire_relaxation_pilot.py run --workers 3

Three workers process one vehicle each. The 27 multistart fits and up to 12 profile fits dominate runtime. The experiment uses batched simulation across speeds and derivatives; completed starts and evaluation cases are checkpointed. For a smaller first run, use run --workers 1 --client nominal_00, followed by the full command. Repeating the run command resumes completed checkpoints. status reports progress. Never run two instances simultaneously. After a forced termination, remove results\data\paper_tire_relaxation_pilot\run.lock only after confirming no pilot process remains.

Source hashes normalize only CRLF/LF line endings. Frozen checkpoints and manifests are never rewritten to bypass a mismatch.

Decision after the run

Check convergence, bounds, multistart consistency and sparse profiles first. Then ask whether JointCT4 improves held-out prediction over LocalCT2, whether it approaches ExactCT4, and whether any improvement matters for control without excessive steering or violations. Similar control results are a valid outcome. Weak identification alone does not show a benefit from federation. Do not add more physics or a larger fleet until these questions have been answered.

The report's time-series example is fixed in advance: nominal_00 at 21 m/s on the 0.7 Hz reference. No manuscript text or publication figure is changed by this pilot.

Restricted coverage hurts identification, but does not create a broad tracking deficit

Single-speed local CT models still track almost as well as full-coverage models. Two low-speed adjacent-pair fits expose a genuine controller robustness problem that the original feasibility flag does not detect.

1. Complete, matched results

All 588 fitted conditions and 1,764 control case records are complete. There are 477 new fits and 1,431 new nonlinear rollouts; 111 fitted conditions and 333 control case records are reused. All 588 schedules were synthesized. The three fleets contain 90 vehicles. Every condition is tested at the same previously unobserved speeds, 13, 21 and 29 m/s, with the same 12-s left-right maneuver, LQI weights, gain interpolation, steering limits and noise-free full-state feedback.

Full coverage uses the original 2–3 speeds and 40–60 s of identification data. Single speed uses each available speed separately for 20 s. Adjacent pair uses each neighboring pair for 40 s. Multi-speed 20 s divides 20 s total across the original speeds using central contiguous windows. All available variants are included; none is chosen by test performance. Means average variants within vehicles, then vehicles within fleets, then fleets equally.

Yet mean tracking RMSE changes from 0.012832 to 0.012842°/s: only 0.0000097°/s, or about 0.076%. The selected single-speed solutions therefore work well for this maneuver despite the exact stiffness/friction ambiguity already established by the identification audit. They do not establish that every equally fitting single-speed solution would work well. Parameter identifiability, model extrapolation accuracy and performance of a particular feedback controller are distinct questions.

3. Two adjacent-pair cases are control-relevant

Both occur when fitting only low-speed data and controlling at 29 m/s: fleet 1302, heavy_08, trained at 12/14 m/s; and fleet 1303, handling_00, trained at 10/12 m/s. Their fitted friction values are about 0.563 and 0.529, versus the simulated value 0.9. Their common-unseen increment errors are 19.49% and 24.71%.

The true simulated plant's augmented closed-loop Jacobian at straight running has dominant real poles about −1.180 and −1.725. Thus the unsaturated origin equilibrium is locally unstable for these interpolated controllers, even though synthesis stabilizes each learned model at its design nodes. These diagnostics use the RK4 step's exact derivative at zero, matching the simulation. They are local diagnostics, not global nonlinear stability guarantees.

The corresponding saved rollouts activate the steering-rate limiter for 56.5% and 58.75% of their samples. Steering-rate RMS rises above 100°/s, with total steering variation above 1,075° over 12 s. Negative unstable poles and these metrics are consistent with rapid alternating steering; the raw time series were not saved here, so the waveform has not been independently inspected. This behavior is undesirable even though yaw-rate RMSE remains below 0.101°/s.

All 1,764 cases are marked feasible because that flag checks applied steering angle/rate and sideslip bounds; the limiter enforces the input bounds. It does not test tracking quality, absence of limiter activation or local stability. Consequently, “all feasible” must not be reported as “all controllers are satisfactory.” Only these two cases activate a limiter or have origin spectral radius above one.

These two cases account for 100.48% of the weighted adjacent-pair-minus-full mean tracking gap. The percentage slightly exceeds 100% because the other cases make a small net contribution in the opposite direction. The official mean includes both. This is a decomposition using the independently computed local stability diagnostic, not removal of poor results.

The low-speed block has the largest adjacent-pair degradation. Full-rank sensitivity at two speeds is not enough to ensure reliable friction extrapolation under noisy finite data. Conversely, single-speed ambiguity can be masked by a favorable selected solution. The results do not justify a monotone claim that fewer speeds always produce worse control.

5. What this means for the paper

This diagnostic does not establish a general need for federation under tighter speed coverage. The physics-based local model remains a strong baseline, including with one observed speed. The two pair failures establish a narrower, meaningful issue: uncertain low-speed extrapolation can produce controllers with undesirable high-speed behavior despite successful estimation and synthesis.

A sharing method would need a matched comparison using the same restricted client data and the same physical model class, together with a credible explanation of which quantities can be shared between vehicles. This experiment contains no such comparison. Existing unrestricted Federated results cannot demonstrate a sharing benefit under the new restrictions.

Before adding another plant or tightening coverage further, decide whether the intended contribution is reliable extrapolation with uncertainty, or identification under a realistic mismatch between the available vehicle model and the plant. If the aim is vehicle-model learning for control, the strong local CT baseline must remain in the evaluation. A more complicated tire model would need a physical motivation and a suitably strong local comparator; these results alone do not select that next experiment.

Scope remains three reused pilot fleets, a correctly specified analytical local model family, known applied inputs, and full-state measurements. Control uses noise-free state feedback and one prescribed maneuver at three fixed speeds. Model error is evaluated around the acquisition curvature; the control maneuver varies yaw rate around straight running. Good tracking here is not proof of accurate tire/friction recovery or robustness across other maneuvers.

6. Reproducibility and audit limits

Verified envelopes, source provenance, completeness, reused controls, all 588 model grids and their saved errors, all 6,468 synthesis matrices, learned-model closed-loop poles and prefilters, and the original fit/control summary CSV values. Computed true-plant origin Jacobians for all 1,764 cases. The original identification checkpoints match the previous audit, which also checked the sampled exact single-speed equivalence families.

No refits, controller retuning or nonlinear rollouts were performed. Saved trajectory metrics were checked for consistency and finiteness, but cannot be independently recomputed without the original trajectory arrays. The earlier cropped identification arrays also remain unavailable. Frozen experiment code, checkpoints and original analysis outputs are unchanged.

Does limited local coverage impair useful physical models?

All 90 existing pilot vehicles. Same tanh plant, structured CT estimator, four starts and bounds. No sharing or new acquisition.

Interpret the contrasts

Full: original 2–3 speeds, 40–60 seconds; single: each observed speed, 20 seconds; adjacent pair: each neighboring pair, 40 seconds; multi_20s: all original speeds, 20 seconds total. The two-speed full block is reused for its pair condition. Single variants and pair variants are equally averaged within each vehicle first.

The multi-speed 20-second condition distributes exactly 2000 transitions across central contiguous windows. It controls recording duration, but changes waveform portions, initial states and nuisance-parameter count. It is a quick existing-data diagnostic, not an identical-excitation or equal-information experiment.

The primary error uses the same original-unobserved grid speeds for every condition of a vehicle. This prevents the evaluation set from changing when observations are removed. All-grid and condition-specific unseen errors are also saved. Conditions are not selected by their test errors.

Decision

Look for increased error at common unseen speeds and meaningful control degradation, together with ambiguity and conditioning. A poor friction estimate alone does not establish a need for federation. If multi-speed 20 s improves over single-speed 20 s, this supports a benefit from operating-condition diversity under this windowing protocol. A subsequent sharing comparison must give all local and shared methods the same restricted client data and the same physical model class.

At fixed curvature, speed also changes lateral acceleration κv². This study varies both speed and tire utilization. Three reused fleets provide descriptive pilot evidence, not a fresh confirmation or a general stability claim.

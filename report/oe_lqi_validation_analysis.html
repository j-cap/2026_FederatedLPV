Interpretation: the benefit against the fixed nominal controller is consistent at fleet level. Against Global, the moderate maneuver improves in every fleet, but the hard maneuver improves in only eight of ten. The hard-maneuver absolute difference is small. The fact that Federated approaches the ExactLPV reference in aggregate does not establish equivalence or an optimality bound.

All 100 handling-family clients have higher Federated RMSE than Global in both maneuvers. All 100 heavy-family clients improve. Nominal-family outcomes improve for 91/100 clients in the moderate maneuver and 52/100 in the hard maneuver. Thus, an aggregate benefit cannot be described as a benefit for every recipient. Family labels are used only for this post-fit diagnostic.

5. What the results establish — and what remains outside scope

The useful conclusion is that the frozen shared models support feasible scheduled control across varying speed and improve aggregate tracking. Reduced identification error does not guarantee reliable control from a locally extrapolated map. Both local baselines synthesize successfully on their own fitted models, yet some schedules fail when applied to the nonlinear recipient plant.

  • Previously analyzed fleets: describe a supporting control extension, not a new independent control confirmation.
  • Available states: noise-free physical-state feedback isolates model quality. No robustness to measurement noise, state estimation or an observer is established.
  • Prescribed speed and yaw reference: this is nonlinear yaw-rate tracking, not validated full path tracking or longitudinal dynamics.
  • Shared controller settings: Q = diag(25, 50, 1000), R = 0.15 come from the nominal acquisition design. These outcomes concern the common frozen design, not the best individually tuned controller for each model.
  • Local extrapolation: Restricted Local uses its frozen three-function map everywhere. The old nearest-observed-speed fallback is absent. Its failure counts are therefore not directly comparable to the legacy table.
  • Actuator protocol: control uses 12° and 120°/s, whereas acquisition used 30°/s. State this difference. Feasibility additionally requires |β| ≤ 6°.
  • Operating-curve mismatch: the maps were identified around a prescribed cornering condition; the control maneuvers vary the requested turn and apply homogeneous A/B feedback with a steady prefilter. The test is empirical usability beyond that condition.

Compact result paragraph for the next manuscript edit

Using the frozen OE-derived maps, all 600 federated rollouts satisfy the tested constraints. Mean paired fleet-relative yaw-rate RMSE reductions against the fixed nominal LTI controller are 24.25% and 25.33% for the moderate and hard maneuvers. The corresponding reductions against Global are 8.36% and 1.29%, with gains in 10/10 and 8/10 fleets. The absolute Global-relative gains are 0.00374 and 0.00203 degrees/s, with similar steering effort. Restricted Local and Smooth Local violate the sideslip bound in 51/77 and 70/62 moderate/hard cases, respectively. These results support the control usability of compatible sharing under the common design, while showing that aggregate improvements do not extend to every recipient.

What this experiment isolates

SharedPrior learns one regularization center from 18 clients. OracleFamily learns one center per known family from six clients. MixedGroups also uses three six-client centers, but each group has two clients per family and per speed. All methods use identical local records, bounds and penalty weights. Local OE and the population-centered nominal prior establish whether learned sharing adds value.

The oracle is a privileged family partition, not an upper bound over all partitions. Failure of family grouping does not rule out dynamic similarity groups. Success supports investigating learned grouping; it does not establish that groups can be recovered from the short noisy records.

Population transfer and scope

The Compact, Sport and SUV anchors use the corresponding CFL-FF vehicle parameters. Mass, inertia, geometry, payload and tire-slope variability follow its declared distribution, with new draws. Lateral Pacejka zero-slip slopes B C μ Fz are mapped into the current tanh model; the nonlinear Pacejka curve is not imported. E is recorded but does not enter the slope. Plant and estimator remain matched four-state models. Geometry is known per vehicle.

Relaxation lengths remain 0.4/0.7 m for all vehicles; the nominal prior is centered at these values. No family-specific relaxation lengths are invented. Thus this tests CFL-FF-style physical heterogeneity, not heterogeneity of force-relaxation time constants. The engineering prior is built from independent population-design draws, never from the simulated test vehicles or evaluation outcomes.

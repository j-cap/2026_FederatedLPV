Structured local models are a strong control baseline

The four-parameter local model gives the lowest mean tracking error in all three pilot fleets. Its large pooled advantage over Federated is dominated by one learned group at 29 m/s; typical differences are small.

1. Completeness and matched comparison

All 1,620 planned runs completed: 90 vehicles × six methods × three speeds. All 540 schedules were synthesized. This audit validates checkpoint provenance, reconstructs all 5,940 stored synthesis matrices from frozen source models, checks saved model closed-loop poles and prefilters, and reproduces all six archived CSV tables. No controller retuning, identification or nonlinear rollouts were performed.

All methods use the same 12-s smooth left-right reference, fixed speeds 13/21/29 m/s, LQI weights, limits, zero initialization and noise-free full-state feedback. Known vehicle parameters are used only for the post-hoc plant-Jacobian diagnostic below, never for the learned controllers.

3. Why the Federated mean is large

Seven nominal-family vehicles in fleet 1302 share learned group 0 and exactly the same Federated schedule. At 29 m/s their tracking RMSE is 5.37–5.51°/s. For each saved controller, the audit computes the augmented closed-loop Jacobian at straight running on the true simulated plant. It uses the RK4 step's exact derivative at zero, matching the simulator, and the interpolated gains. Exactly these seven Federated cases have spectral radius above one. This establishes a local linear instability of that equilibrium and supports a control-relevant explanation of the tail. It does not isolate the relative contribution of prefilter mismatch and unstable feedback to the finite-duration RMSE, or imply a nonlinear global stability result.

As a descriptive decomposition only, the remaining 263 paired cases average 0.013573°/s for Federated and 0.013003°/s for structured CT: a difference of 0.000570°/s. The primary averages above still include all 270 cases. These seven cases are identified by the computed local stability diagnostic; no cases are removed from the official result.

5. What this changes for the paper

The current vehicle benchmark does not establish that federation is needed for useful models or good closed-loop tracking under limited local speed coverage. The structured local estimator uses the same local acquisition data and four physically coupled parameters, and performs strongly without sharing. The original regression rank argument remains a statement about its chosen flexible representation; it is not evidence that the physical identification problem must require shared fleet information.

The control evidence strengthens Markus's baseline concern. It also shows why a pooled matrix-error mean alone is insufficient: Global tracks well despite its larger matrix error, while a single learned-group schedule can cause a localized control defect. Model accuracy, controller sensitivity, interpolation and closed-loop performance must be distinguished.

The structured result assumes the correct four-parameter model family, known geometry, full-state measurements and the present simulated fleet. It does not establish that local physical identification will always dominate sharing, nor that friction is well identified. These three fleets were already used in development, so this is a pilot result without confirmatory inference.

6. Recommended next decision

Keep this strong structured local baseline and the unmodified pilot results. Before expanding the campaign, decide whether the paper should focus on the present vehicle model or on a more general identification problem. For the vehicle scope, discuss the changed evidence with Markus and formulate a physically justified information limitation that remains after using model structure. Only then consider a matched structured local/pooled/group comparison. Increasing heterogeneity or weakening the local model solely to create a federation gain would not answer that question.

Separately, a future controller-design pass should validate interpolated gains on the relevant model/plant family; successful Riccati solves at grid nodes alone are insufficient. This audit does not repair the saved controllers or claim that a repair would recover the identification motivation.

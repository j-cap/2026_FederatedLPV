Removing sideslip from fitting leaves matched tracking almost unchanged

All 270 new yaw-only-model rollouts complete. Neither method activates the steering limiter or produces a locally unstable straight-running equilibrium. The mean tracking difference is about 0.000020°/s.

1. Matched execution and main result

The comparison contains 90 pilot vehicles × three test speeds × two identification methods = 540 case records. The 270 full-state CT results are reused exactly. The 270 yaw-only-model runs use the same LQI design rules, 12-s left-right reference, 13/21/29 m/s speeds, initial states, gain interpolation, steering limits and noise-free true-state feedback. Identification, physical parameters and tuning settings are unchanged.

Mean yaw-rate RMSE is 0.01283193°/s for full-state CT and 0.01281197°/s for yaw-only CT. The latter is lower by 0.1555%, but the absolute difference is only 0.00001996°/s. This is not persuasive evidence that removing a measurement improves control. It shows no practically apparent aggregate tracking penalty in this particular benchmark; no formal equivalence or noninferiority margin was specified.

3. Steering effort and local stability

All 540 cases meet the applied steering/rate and sideslip bounds. Unlike the two earlier restricted-coverage pair failures, none activates either limiter. Mean steering-rate RMS is 0.820845°/s for full-state CT and 0.820827°/s for yaw-only CT, with nearly identical maxima around 1.519°/s.

The maximum true-plant origin spectral radius is 0.966152 for full-state CT and 0.966193 for yaw-only CT. These Jacobians use the simulator RK4 derivative and interpolated gains at each test speed. They establish local linear stability at straight running for the tested controllers; they do not establish global nonlinear stability or robustness over arbitrary trajectories.

The definition of feasibility remains limited to physical bounds. Here, inspecting limiter activity and the local Jacobians independently finds no hidden counterpart of the earlier adjacent-pair problem.

4. Relation to the identification result

The preceding yaw-only identification audit found a 28.4% relative increase in unseen increment error and approximately doubled local sideslip pulse-response error, despite only a small held-out yaw-prediction change. The present runs show that those model differences do not produce a corresponding aggregate tracking loss for this LQI design and maneuver.

Matrix error and closed-loop tracking evaluate different properties. Tracking also depends on which model directions change the synthesized gains, integral action and feedback. This comparison does not isolate the causal contribution of those mechanisms. A slightly worse matrix estimate can produce slightly better tracking without being a better physical model.

5. What has and has not been established

The structured local CT baseline remains strong after removing sideslip from its identification loss. We have no demonstrated need for fleet sharing from this sensing ablation alone. The result closes the specific matched state-feedback question without adding a new plant or changing heterogeneity.

The evaluation still provides true sideslip to the controller. Acquisition also used noisy sideslip feedback. Therefore this is not evidence that the complete workflow can operate without sideslip sensing, or that an observer will reconstruct sideslip accurately from these identified models.

If the practical sensing assumption is the next priority, the next distinct stage is observer-based operation: first check acquisition with one common nominal LTI observer/controller using only available measurements, then evaluate identified models under common observer-design rules. New observer settings should be established without selecting them from the final test outcomes. Adding geometry variation at the same time would obscure that comparison.

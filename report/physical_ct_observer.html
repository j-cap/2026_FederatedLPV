Do identified models help when sideslip must be estimated?

What this test isolates

90 reused pilot vehicles × 5 combinations × 3 speeds = 1350 new rollouts. All use yaw measurements only, with 0.03°/s Gaussian sensor noise, known applied steering and known prescribed speed. The same noise sequence is used for every method within a vehicle/speed case. No true-state feedback. Existing controller gains and CT fits are unchanged.

Nominal/Nominal uses the fixed 20 m/s model throughout. FullStateCT/Nominal and YawOnlyCT/Nominal change the controller only. FullStateCT/Matched and YawOnlyCT/Matched also use their fitted physical model for observation, evaluated at the actual fixed speed and straight-running trim. OE nuisance offsets never enter the observer. At this trim friction does not enter the linear observer model.

The sensor covariance R is known. Q is a shared engineering allowance for model discrepancy: per-step standard deviations 0.002° for sideslip and 0.01°/s for yaw rate at 0.01 s. No process noise is injected into the plant and no method-specific tuning is performed. P0=0 reflects the known zero initial state. Measurement correction precedes control; prediction uses the applied steering. The integrator uses measured yaw. Innovation statistics are descriptive under model mismatch.

These fits were acquired previously under full-state feedback. This comparison does not establish a fully yaw-only acquisition-to-learning workflow. FullStateCT remains a privileged training comparator. No new federated estimator is tested. A single paired noise realization per case supports a pilot comparison, not a sensor-noise robustness claim.

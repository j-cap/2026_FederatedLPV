Do complementary peer speeds improve the target model?

Same nine physical vehicles in both conditions. For each target, its original local record is fixed. Complementary reuses the converged balanced 13/21/29 m/s fleet. SameSpeed learns from all nine records at the target's speed. The eight peers retain their identities, input, sample count and paired measurement-noise realization; six peers change speed, while two already share the target speed.

Each condition has 108 s of fleet data, including the target's 12 s. Three uniform-speed fits per fleet serve the three target-speed subsets; no target uses its own extra counterfactual records from the other fits. Test conditions and local baselines are unchanged. Peer labels and true parameters are not supplied to identification.

Interpretation

Credit complementary coverage when it improves target-unseen prediction beyond SameSpeed and NominalPrior, consistently across the pilot fleets and without unacceptable target-observed degradation. A gain only over LocalOE is insufficient. If SameSpeed is equally good, extra peer observations or regularization may explain the result without requiring complementary speeds.

Steering amplitude is matched, so yaw response amplitude and sensitivity can differ with speed; these differences are part of the speed intervention. Do not claim a separate geometric-coverage mechanism independent of excitation or signal-to-noise ratio. Common true relaxation lengths favor transfer. Three reused pilot fleets provide development evidence, not independent confirmation. No controller is tested.

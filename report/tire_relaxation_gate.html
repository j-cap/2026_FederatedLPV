Tire-force relaxation: what can yaw measurements distinguish?

Scope

This is a matched synthetic information diagnostic: three reused pilot vehicles, three fixed speeds, two prescribed open-loop steering probes. State is [β,r,Fyf/m,Fyr/m]. The existing tanh force law is unchanged. True relaxation lengths are 0.4 and 0.7 m; constant axle values are declared simulation assumptions within a published experimental range, not calibrated values for these vehicles. No Pacejka change, controller synthesis, federation, or manuscript update is included.

Only yaw is used for the information calculations and objective surfaces; applied steering and speed are known. Generated initial states are zero. The target force states and sideslip are not supplied to an estimator. Noise SD is 0.03°/s. Open-loop probes are controlled excitation, not claims about ordinary driving. Operating-bound failures remain visible and do not count as feasible identification procedures.

How to read the diagnostics

State observability: finite-horizon sampled yaw observation matrix at straight running, with known parameters and declared state scales. Full numerical rank is a local linear-model property. It is not parameter identifiability or global nonlinear observability.

Parameter distinguishability: analytic complex-step derivatives of the nonlinear yaw trajectory with respect to log parameters and scaled initial states. Relaxation columns are projected away from nuisance sensitivities. The smallest singular value measures the least visible local log-length direction in noise units. Conditional assumes exact CT ratios and initial states. Unknown CT permits variations in Cf/m, Cr/m, m/Iz and μ. Unknown initial permits all four initial states. No prior regularization is used. Rank and conditioning depend on scaling, horizon and the declared relative SVD threshold; 1e−8 and 1e−10 alternatives are saved too.

Objective surfaces: weighted yaw SSE over a fixed length grid, with clean and noisy targets. True CT fixes other parameters to generating values; Previous CT fixes them to saved pre-change estimates. Comparing them reveals how baseline error can shift the preferred lengths. Marginal grid minima are coarse conditional profiles, not continuously optimized fits, nuisance-profile likelihoods or confidence intervals. The generating lengths are included as reference coordinates.

These results cannot prove global structural identifiability. Low residuals alone do not prove correct force or sideslip reconstruction.

Analytical negative checks

For equal relaxation times, det(O) for physical-force coordinates is (lf+lr)(lf Cf−lr Cr)²/(Iz³ m τ² v). The normalized-force implementation has the same rank. At lf Cf=lr Cr the sampled four-state observability rank is 2 (expected 2). With zero steering and zero initial state, parameter sensitivity rank is 0 (expected 0). Both assertions must pass.

Decision before any control comparison

Inspect whether the two relaxation directions remain distinguishable after CT and initial-state uncertainty, whether Previous CT moves the best lengths substantially, and whether the required probe remains within the prescribed operating bounds. A rank check alone is not a pass criterion. Broad or boundary-reaching profiles require a narrower parameterization or a separately justified sensor/input change. A useful distinction under ideal assumptions must then survive estimation and independent prediction tests before a control benefit can be claimed.

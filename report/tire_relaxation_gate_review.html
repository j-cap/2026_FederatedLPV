Tire-force states are observable;
joint parameter recovery remains conditional

The extension is viable as a model. The main uncertainty is separating relaxation from errors in the existing CT parameters, rather than reconstructing four states from one output.

Decision

Proceed with the augmented model, but do not claim reliable joint identification from yaw alone yet. All nine distinct vehicle–speed linearizations have full state-observability rank. Broadband steering distinguishes the relaxation lengths well when CT parameters are exact. Allowing those parameters to vary substantially reduces the remaining information, even though numerical rank stays two.

The next small comparison should include a strong local augmented-model fit and independent prediction checks. A known-parameter augmented reference can establish whether the extra physics matters for control; it cannot validate the local identification method. Fleet learning is not justified by this diagnostic alone.

1. State observability and operating conditions

All 18 probe records pass their operating checks. The maximum sideslip is 0.457°, lateral acceleration 1.088 m/s², and steering rate 1.338°/s. These are gentle, prescribed open-loop probes. They do not demonstrate robust control or normal-driving identification.

The nine distinct known-parameter linearizations have rank 4, with scaled finite-horizon condition numbers 20.3–39.2. The two probes repeat each same state-observability calculation. The analytical balanced negative case has rank 2, and zero steering has zero relaxation-parameter information. Thus a single yaw output is not by itself an obstacle to a four-state model.

This is local linearized observability at straight running, with known parameters and the documented state scales. It does not establish global nonlinear observability, observer accuracy with uncertain parameters, or structural parameter identifiability.

The smallest singular value measures the least visible local combination of log front/rear relaxation lengths, after whitening by yaw noise. Larger values mean a larger change in measured yaw for the same local parameter perturbation. Full rank alone says nothing about adequate precision.

After allowing CT and initial-state variations, the median ratio of smallest information eigenvalues to the exact-CT result is 3.3% for slow steering and 4.7% for broadband steering. The weakest direction can rotate, so this ratio compares minima rather than a fixed physical direction. Initial-state uncertainty alone changes the broadband smallest singular values by less than 0.2%; uncertainty in the CT dynamics dominates.

A log-length perturbation of Euclidean norm 0.1 produces a minimum locally predicted weighted squared output change of 11.25 with exact CT parameters, but only 0.53 after CT/initial-state compensation. This is an infinitesimal sensitivity calculation extrapolated to a small illustrative step, not an observed finite-parameter experiment, confidence interval, or statistical acceptance rule.

All relaxation sensitivity matrices have rank 2 at all three saved relative SVD cutoffs. The reduction in information is therefore not a numerical rank-threshold artifact. Holding μ fixed in an additional decomposition of the same derivatives does not remove the loss: for nominal_00 at 13 m/s broadband, the smallest singular value is about 2.72 with μ fixed, versus 2.72 with μ free, compared with 35.69 under exact CT parameters.

3. Existing model errors can shift the preferred relaxation lengths

The generating lengths are 0.4 m front and 0.7 m rear. With exact CT parameters and noise-free data, all 18 grid surfaces have their minimum at these values. Because the generating values were explicitly included in the grid, this is a consistency check, not proof that an optimizer recovers unknown parameters or that the continuous optimum is globally unique.

These are coarse grid minima, so the percentages are not continuous-estimator bias estimates. All grid minima are interior, which does not exclude a broad valley.

Noise adds further variability. Across slow probes with previous CT fixed, the largest front-length grid deviation is +36.9% and the largest positive rear deviation is +20.5%. The broadband results are much closer to the generating values, but each record uses only one noise realization; no estimator variance claim follows.

4. Implications for the next controlled comparison

Keep the force-state extension. The state-observability concern is not a blocker for these vehicles and speeds. Use the broadband records for identification development. The slow records are a useful weak-information diagnostic, not a strong basis for learning two relaxation lengths.

Avoid presenting a two-length fit with the old CT coefficients frozen as physical parameter recovery. The saved CT errors are large enough to move the preferred lengths. The next pilot should compare this conditional fit with a joint structured local fit of the relevant CT ratios and relaxation lengths, using the available multiple-speed records and reporting the treatment of μ and initial states explicitly. Nonlinear objective profiles and held-out trajectory predictions must check whether the local sensitivity warning persists at finite parameter changes.

That identification stage can be part of the small controlled model/control comparison already planned. Keep a known-parameter augmented reference to separate the value of representing the physics from the quality of estimation. Do not add Pacejka coefficients, fleet priors, or more heterogeneity simultaneously. If the joint local model remains weakly distinguished, discuss a justified reduced parameterization or an acceleration measurement before expanding the study.

No joint fit, additional-speed pooling, new trajectory, control comparison, or federation experiment was performed in this review. The proposed next stage has not been validated by these results.

\section{Phase 0: plant and fleet validation}
\label{sec:phase0}
Phase~0 verifies the equations, discretization, operating envelope, and physical
family design before identification or control is introduced.

\subsection{Experiment 0A: nominal-vehicle validation}
\paragraph{Objective.}
Validate the nominal continuous and zero-order-hold-discretized bicycle model on
$v_x\in[10,30]\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ with initial sample time
$T_s=0.01\,\mathrm{s}$.

\paragraph{Predeclared expectation.}
Matrix entries, poles, time constants, and steady-state gains vary smoothly with
speed. The nominal model is physically plausible and numerically well conditioned
over the complete envelope.

\paragraph{Setup and planned evidence.}
On a fine speed grid, compute continuous and discrete eigenvalues, controllability,
steady-state steering-to-yaw-rate and steering-to-sideslip gains, and an understeer
or equivalent handling measure. Plot selected $A$ and $B$ entries, pole
trajectories, and steady-state gains versus speed. Check equations, units, and sign
conventions independently.

\paragraph{Decision gate.}
No unintended instability, singularity, loss of controllability, discontinuity,
or implausible gain may occur. Any failure blocks all following experiments.

\paragraph{Results, interpretation, and decision.}
The audit was executed on 201 uniformly spaced frozen speeds. The implementation
uses an augmented matrix exponential for exact zero-order-hold discretization.
All continuous-time models are asymptotically stable. The least-negative pole real
part is $-3.911\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ at the high-speed end of the envelope. The
corresponding maximum discrete-time spectral radius is $0.96164$, which remains
below one with a clear numerical margin. The pole pair remains complex throughout
the envelope. Its real part moves from $-11.733$ to
$-3.911\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, while its imaginary magnitude changes from $2.989$ to
$3.517\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. The model therefore becomes less strongly damped in
absolute time as speed increases, without approaching open-loop instability.
The complete speed-dependent audit is shown in \cref{fig:experiment0a}, with
representative numerical values collected in \cref{tab:experiment0a}.

\begin{figure}[ht]

 \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{experiment_0a_nominal_validation.pdf}
 \caption{Experiment 0A nominal-vehicle audit. The continuous pole pair remains in

The controllability matrix has full rank at every grid point. Its smallest
singular value is $2.506$. The largest reported condition number is $173.1$ at
$10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Because the controllability columns mix quantities with
physical units, this condition number is retained only as a numerical diagnostic,
not as an invariant measure of control authority. Direct matrix conditioning is
benign: $\max_v\kappa_2(A)=6.93$ and
$\max_v\kappa_2(A_d)=1.13$.

The steady-state yaw-rate gain rises from $3.260$ to
$5.757\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, but its ratio to the kinematic value $v_x/L$ falls from
$0.913$ to $0.537$. This agrees exactly with the positive understeer coefficient
\begin{equation}
 K_u=\frac{m(C_r l_r-C_f l_f)}{C_f C_r(l_f+l_r)}
 =2.679\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s^2\,m^{-1}},
\end{equation}
for which the steady-state relation is
$r/\delta=v_x/(L+K_u v_x^2)$. The sideslip gain changes from $0.260$ to
$-1.081\,\mathrm{rad/rad}$ and crosses zero at approximately
$14.11\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Independent steady-state solution of the force and
yaw-moment balances gives the same gains and signs.

Experiment 0A therefore \textbf{passes}. The equations, sign convention, exact
discretization, sample time, and speed envelope can be retained for Experiment
0B. This pass establishes internal physical and numerical consistency of the
benchmark. It does not claim calibration to a particular production vehicle;
absolute parameter realism remains a limitation to revisit before the eventual
paper-level nonlinear study. Machine-readable speed-grid results and the summary
statistics are stored with the experiment outputs.

\subsection{Experiment 0B: structural-family validation}
\paragraph{Objective.}
Verify that the nominal, heavy/high-inertia, and changed-handling-balance centers
create distinct but physically interpretable dynamics.

\paragraph{Predeclared expectation.}
The heavy family differs mainly in response speed and gain. The handling family
changes yaw/sideslip balance rather than merely rescaling the nominal response.

\paragraph{Setup and planned evidence.}
At $v_x\in\{12,20,28\}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, apply a small steering step, a smooth
sine sweep, and a lane-change-like input. Compare yaw rate, sideslip, poles,
steady-state gains, and family-to-family dynamic distance. Report a compact table
of absolute physical parameters and relative changes.

\paragraph{Decision gate.}
All centers must remain controllable and physically plausible. Differences must
be large enough to affect prediction and control, but not so extreme that the
benchmark makes family recovery trivial or unrealistic.

\paragraph{Results, interpretation, and decision.}
All three centers remain asymptotically stable and controllable at all 201 tested
speeds. Their worst discrete spectral radii are $0.9616$ (nominal), $0.9666$
(heavy), and $0.9578$ (handling). Thus, the heavy center has the slowest absolute
decay, while the changed-handling center has the fastest. The minimum
controllability singular values are $2.506$, $2.854$, and $1.942$, respectively.
As in Experiment 0A, these dimensioned singular values are used only to detect
loss of rank or gross numerical degeneration.

All three centers are understeering, but their balance is distinct. Relative to
the nominal center, the heavy model has a slightly smaller understeer coefficient
and therefore a slightly higher steady-state yaw gain. Its least-negative pole is
closer to zero and its high-speed sideslip magnitude is larger. The handling
center has a $63\%$ larger understeer coefficient. Its yaw gain consequently
flattens and then decreases slightly at high speed, while its sideslip magnitude
is substantially reduced. These effects agree with the intended physical roles;
the heavy family is not simply a uniform scaling, and the handling family changes
the yaw--sideslip balance.

The normalized frozen-model distance from nominal is nearly independent of speed:
$0.086$--$0.089$ for the heavy center and approximately $0.056$ for the handling
center. This is desirable for the experimental decomposition. Speed moves each
vehicle along its own model curve, whereas structural offsets persist throughout
the scheduling envelope, as shown in \cref{fig:experiment0b-characteristics}.

The step, sine-sweep, and lane-change-like inputs produce the same qualitative
ordering at $12$, $20$, and $28\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. For the representative
lane-change-like maneuver at $28\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, the heavy center increases
peak yaw rate by $2.7\%$ and peak sideslip magnitude by $23.6\%$ relative to
nominal. The handling center reduces the corresponding peaks by $15.2\%$ and
$33.4\%$. At $20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, the respective changes are $+2.0\%$ and
$+27.6\%$ for the heavy center, and $-12.6\%$ and $-33.5\%$ for the handling
center. The complete response metrics for all 27 family--speed--maneuver cases are
stored in machine-readable form.
The representative time responses in \cref{fig:experiment0b-responses} make this
family ordering visible for the lane-change-like input.

Experiment 0B therefore \textbf{passes provisionally}. The centers are stable,
controllable, interpretable, and dynamically distinct without being separated by
orders of magnitude. The qualifier is important: the handling center is only
about $5.6\%$ from nominal in the normalized frozen-model metric. Experiment 0C
must therefore determine whether the proposed within-family scatter obscures this
separation. The family centers are retained unchanged until that population-level
test is complete.

\subsection{Experiment 0C: within-family client population}
\paragraph{Objective.}
Construct a 30-client fleet with ten clients per family and determine whether the
desired hierarchical structure is actually present.

\paragraph{Predeclared expectation.}
Client envelopes overlap modestly within each family. Persistent differences
between families remain visible over relevant parts of the speed range.

\paragraph{Setup and planned evidence.}
Sample bounded, fixed client parameters with initial ranges
$m,I_z:\pm2.5\%$ and $C_f,C_r:\pm5\%$. Inspect physical parameters, frozen-model
distances, response envelopes, and controllability over speed. The primary figure
shows family centers and within-family bands for selected dynamic characteristics.

\paragraph{Decision gate.}
Within-family variation should be smaller than between-family variation without
perfect separation on every scalar feature. Invalid samples are not silently
discarded; the sampling rule must be revised if they occur systematically.

\paragraph{Results, interpretation, and decision.}
The bounded sampling rule was evaluated for the development fleet (seed~1) and
nine additional fleet seeds, giving 300 clients in the robustness audit. All
clients are stable and controllable at every one of the 101 evaluated speeds. No
sample was rejected or repaired. The exact fleet-generation settings are now
stored in the Phase-0 configuration file.

For structural comparison, each client is represented by its exact-ZOH $A_d$ and
$B_d$ matrices at $10$, $15$, $20$, $25$, and
$30\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The distance separately normalizes the $A_d$ and $B_d$
differences at each speed before averaging over matrices and speeds. Across the ten
fleets, the mean between-family distance is $2.88$--$3.57$ times the mean
within-family distance, with mean ratio $3.28$. The true-label silhouette ranges
from $0.548$ to $0.623$ and has mean $0.590$. Hence the hierarchy is consistent
across seeds rather than being an artifact of the development realization.

Nearest-center classification using the three exact, known family centers is
correct for all 300 clients. The smallest correct-to-incorrect center-distance
margin is $0.00585$. This perfect oracle-center result does not imply that cluster
learning from short noisy trajectories will be trivial. It uses full-envelope,
noise-free matrices and known centers. Moreover, in every tested fleet the largest
within-family pairwise distance exceeds the smallest between-family distance. The
population is therefore not separated by a universal scalar-distance threshold.

The response envelopes in \cref{fig:experiment0c-envelopes} show the intended
balance. A single frozen scalar characteristic is not sufficient everywhere:
nominal and heavy yaw-gain bands overlap substantially, and their high-speed
sideslip bands overlap. The handling family becomes distinct mainly through the
joint speed-dependent yaw and sideslip behavior. The pole envelopes remain stable
and relatively compact for all families.

For visualization only, the seed-1 model vectors were coefficient-wise
standardized and projected with PCA. The first two components explain $98.2\%$ of
the variance ($78.6\%$ and $19.6\%$ individually) and show three coherent groups
in \cref{fig:experiment0c-pca}. PCA is not used to define the oracle labels or to
claim performance of the future clustering algorithm.

Experiment 0C therefore \textbf{passes}. The proposed scatter preserves a stable,
reproducible hierarchy in multivariate dynamics while retaining overlap in
individual characteristics and pairwise distances. Together, Experiments 0A--0C
support freezing the linear benchmark for Phase~1. This conclusion remains scoped
to exact full-envelope models. Limited coverage, estimation noise, and learned
clustering are deliberately deferred and may substantially reduce recoverability.

\subsection{Phase-0 benchmark freeze}
After Experiments 0A--0C pass, the equations, sample time, speed envelope, family
centers, scatter model, and development seed are frozen. Later changes require an
explicit report entry explaining the failure or new evidence that motivated them.

All three experiments passed. The frozen Phase-0 benchmark uses
$T_s=0.01\,\mathrm{s}$, $v_x\in[10,30]\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, the three centers in
\cref{tab:experiment0b-parameters}, ten clients per family, bounded independent
$\pm2.5\%$ scatter in mass and yaw inertia, bounded independent $\pm5\%$ scatter
in front and rear cornering stiffness, and development seed~1. The next task is
Experiment 1A: frozen-grid LPV basis validation.

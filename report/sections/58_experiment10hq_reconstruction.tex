\section{10H-Q reconstruction: the feedback bottleneck}
\label{sec:10hq}
The frozen diagnostic tests all combinations of controller and state source
using the audited reconstructed P parent at \texttt{6afb002}. Controllers are
public nominal, learned N and simulator oracle; supplied states are nominal
KF, learned KF or true plant state. Three additional learned-Both cases replace
only beta, both axle forces, or beta plus both forces in the controller input.
These interventions never overwrite the observer's internal state. The measured
yaw integrator, noise draws, clipping, costs, speed schedules and assignments
remain fixed. Q performs no fitting or membership selection.

The 4,800 rollouts cover five development fleets, ten recipients, two separate
donor deployments, two maneuvers, two fitting panels and twelve cases. A further
2,400 common-input replays compare nominal, learned and oracle KFs on the same
nominal and Both records. The original M--P artifacts were unavailable: this is
a fresh reconstruction from intact L inputs, not reproduction of lost historical
results. Confirmation seeds 591--600 and 611--620 remain sealed.

Entries are mean paired fleet tracking gains in percent against nominal output
feedback. First average the two deployment RMSEs per recipient, then ten
recipients per fleet; percentage gains are paired within each of five fleets.
Positive entries mean lower tracking RMSE. The two panels change both learned
plants and KF design settings and are not a causal comparison of fitting Q.

The prespecified full-state comparison is decisive in the primary panel:
learned versus nominal control gives $-172.36\%$ broadband and $-333.60\%$
transient mean fleet gains. Only one of five fleets improves; recipient counts
are 19/50 and 12/50. Supplying true states does not rescue learned control.
Correcting Both's forces instead gives $-160.93\%$ and $-294.92\%$ against Both,
with only 14/50 and 13/50 recipients improving. Beta correction has little
effect. In the comparison panel, learned full-state control improves four of
five fleets, but large harm in the fifth produces negative fleet-average gains
of $-26.39\%$ and $-31.34\%$ against nominal full-state control. The tails matter.

Oracle full-state control improves on nominal full-state control by only
1.42\% broadband and 1.68\% transient in the primary panel. All five fleet means
improve, but only 25/50 and 26/50 recipients do. Its gains against nominal
output feedback are 5.89\% and 7.75\%. Thus headroom is modest under these fixed
tasks and LQI costs; the oracle is not an optimal tracking bound.

On the same Both records, learned KF beta RMSE improves from 0.03672 to
0.02581 degrees broadband and from 0.06199 to 0.04558 degrees transient.
However, front/rear force-per-mass RMSEs increase from 0.02325/0.01174 to
0.05325/0.05438 $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ broadband and from 0.03366/0.01392 to
0.09006/0.08881 $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ transient. These are mean recipient RMS values
after deployment averaging, not a pooled trajectory RMS.

With the same learned state gain, total projected steering RMS is 0.759/3.896/
0.190 degrees for nominal/learned/oracle KF broadband and 1.336/7.719/0.190
degrees transient. The learned KF's signed joint-force contributions are 3.795
and 7.639 degrees. The same ordering holds on nominal records and in the
comparison panel. All five component second moments and their cross terms are
retained: summing component RMS values would discard cancellation.

The combination of poor learned full-state control, harmful force corrections
and larger common-input force projections is consistent with compensation
between controller-model and observer errors. It does not establish an additive
causal tracking decomposition or prove that bounds alone caused the failure.
Better beta reconstruction is insufficient evidence of a useful complete
physical state estimate for the learned LQI.

\subsection{Validation, limits and consequence}
All 4,800 rollouts are finite, with no design failures or beta/steering limit
violations. Saturation is retained in the per-case tables. All 1,600 overlapping
P records agree exactly in every array. Independent saved-step and metric
errors are below $2.0\times10^{-15}$. An independent observer replay and
projection audit covers 7,200 diagnostic rows, with maximum metric error
$2.85\times10^{-14}$. A separate verification checks 620 provenance references,
the two-stage paired aggregation, and 400 cross-panel nominal/oracle full-state
trajectory pairs, which agree exactly despite differing unused KF internals.

Strict independent full-path replay at tolerance $10^{-8}$ passes for 4,684
records; 116 remain sensitive to floating-point ordering, with maximum
tracking-RMSE change 0.11949 degrees/s. The principal primary-panel learned
Both, learned full-state and force-correction paths are not sensitive. Nominal
full-state paths include sensitive cases. Conservative bounds using each
record's observed alternate-path RMSE change preserve the signs of the three
primary prespecified contrasts above. These bounds are neither confidence
intervals nor a general robustness guarantee. Tolerances were not relaxed.

Frozen-speed checks do not certify scheduled stability. Primary learned
full-state control has maximum frozen radius 0.9964 despite poor tracking;
comparison-panel learned full-state control has five of 100 deployed pairs
with radius at least one and maximum 4.426. Finite clipped rollouts therefore
do not establish a usable controller family.

The inherited coefficient bounds are heuristic, even with exact physical
coupling. Post hoc inspection of deployed primary models gives front lengths
down to 0.223 m and rear lengths up to 3.713 m. These are inferred model
coordinates, not measurements of client geometry. Output prediction and the
coupling identity do not ensure plausible control-design models. The next
priority is independently justified physical-coordinate constraints and
controller-relevant validation before adding observer complexity. This
diagnostic does not demonstrate the intended iterative federated improvement
of KF and controller matrices.

recipient tails and provenance are committed. The numerical environment is
Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.3.5, SciPy 1.17.0 and pandas 2.2.3 with one BLAS thread.
The repository suite passes 170 tests, and Q sources pass scoped lint.

\section{Path to paper consolidation}
During development, this report favors traceability over page limits. Failed
experiments, revised assumptions, and negative findings remain documented. Paper
consolidation begins only after the oracle gates and at least one convincing
non-oracle federated result.

The consolidated paper should center on one precise claim and retain only the
decisive evidence. A likely structure is: problem and motivation, proposed
federated LPV method, experimental methodology, decisive oracle result, federated
result under partial operating coverage, limitations, and conclusion. Candidate
contributions are the scheduling--structure decomposition, an uncertainty-aware
clustered federated LPV identification method, and evidence that a small number of
LPV families replaces many frozen LTI regimes. The development report remains the
complete experimental record and supplies supplementary details.

\section{Current execution status}
Phases~0--3 are complete on the linear speed-dependent bicycle plant. The plant
and client population passed validation (\cref{sec:phase0}); the reciprocal-speed
LPV basis passed pointwise and trajectory validation (\cref{sec:phase1}); and the
oracle model and controller comparisons support complementary benefits from
scheduling and family specialization (\cref{sec:phase2,sec:phase3}). The
nonlinear tire plant passed its equation, force-limit, and integration audit in
Experiment 4A. Experiment 4B then established and froze three feasible,
quantitatively separated maneuver-severity levels
(\cref{sec:experiment4b}). Experiment 4C shows that the clustered LPV model
retains its one-step, free-run, and worst-family advantages on nonlinear-plant
data and an unseen speed--maneuver combination (\cref{sec:experiment4c}).
Experiment 4D finds that the unchanged clustered LPV controller transfers
successfully, but direct redesign from unconstrained nonlinear-data regressions
fails the primary stability and performance gate (\cref{sec:experiment4d}).
Experiment 4D-R shows that a
common physics regularizer restores stability and feasibility, but does not make
redesigned M4 outperform clustered LTI (\cref{sec:experiment4dr}). Experiment 4E
shows that solver roundoff and tire nonlinearity alone do not explain the failure.
Experiment 4F restores M4's initial tracking advantage through structured
parameter-ratio identification (\cref{sec:experiment4e,sec:experiment4f}).
Experiment 4G validates that repair on ten independent train/test fleet pairs
and three held-out trajectories, including the original fitted LTI baselines.
Its predeclared recovery gate passes (\cref{sec:experiment4g}). This establishes
oracle support within the tested distribution, not a federated result.
Experiment 5A finds that the first tested frozen grid matching M4 within the
aggregate 5\% tolerance uses 30 gain vectors versus M4's 15, with higher peak
steering rates (\cref{sec:experiment5a}). This is a result for the tested grid
search and switching rule. Experiment 6A completes the oracle regime map:
global LTI is sufficient at low separation and narrow speed range, global LPV
at zero separation and wider ranges, family LTI at separated families and
narrow range, and family LPV at nominal separation and wider ranges
(\cref{sec:experiment6a}). All 129,600 tested runs satisfy amplitude feasibility.
Experiment 6B checks four corner cells with frozen 6A controllers, corrected
lateral-momentum integration, and slower references. Its robustness gate
passes, and the corresponding regime choices persist
(\cref{sec:experiment6b}). The largest moderate aggregate tracking change from
the plant correction is 0.16\%, much smaller than the architecture differences.
Prescribed longitudinal speed and pure lateral tire forces remain limitations.
The federated extension (\cref{sec:phase7}) remains planned.

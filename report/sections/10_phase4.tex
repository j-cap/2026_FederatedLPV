\section{Phase 4: nonlinear-plant escalation}
\label{sec:phase4}
Phase~4 replaces the linear plant, not the compact identified model class. It tests
whether the structural conclusion survives realistic plant--model mismatch.

\subsection{Experiment 4A: nonlinear tire-model validation}
\paragraph{Objective.}
Implement and validate saturating tire forces
\begin{equation}
 F_{y,j}=C_j\alpha_{\mathrm{sat},j}
 \tanh\left(\frac{\alpha_j}{\alpha_{\mathrm{sat},j}}\right),
 \qquad j\in\{f,r\}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Predeclared expectation.}
The nonlinear plant agrees with the linear model at small slip and departs smoothly
as tire saturation becomes relevant.

\paragraph{Setup and planned evidence.}
Check the force--slip curves, small-signal slope, force limits, integration
accuracy, sideslip, yaw rate, and lateral acceleration. Preserve the Phase-0
structural families initially so that the plant change is isolated.

\paragraph{Decision gate.}
The model must have correct small-slip behavior, smooth saturation, plausible force
limits, and numerically converged simulations. Otherwise Phase~4 is blocked.

\paragraph{Results, interpretation, and decision.}
The nonlinear plant retains the Phase-0 state, input, structural family
parameters, and sign conventions. A common dry-road friction coefficient
$\mu=0.9$ is used. Static axle loads are
\begin{equation}
 F_{z,f}=mg\frac{l_r}{l_f+l_r},\qquad
 F_{z,r}=mg\frac{l_f}{l_f+l_r},
\end{equation}
and the saturation angles are selected as
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{\mathrm{sat},j}=\frac{\mu F_{z,j}}{C_j}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, each tanh law has the original cornering stiffness at zero slip
and approaches the axle friction limit $\mu F_{z,j}$ smoothly. The resulting
family-center scales and force limits are listed in \cref{tab:experiment4a-tires}.

Central finite differences were used to linearize the nonlinear right-hand side
at zero sideslip, yaw rate, and steering for all family centers at
$v_x\in\{12,20,28\}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The maximum relative Jacobian error
against the analytical Phase-0 matrices is $9.74\times10^{-13}$. Numerical force
curves are odd, monotone, have the prescribed zero-slip slope, and remain within
their friction limits.

Open-loop simulations use a smooth steering doublet at the same three speeds.
Three diagnostic amplitudes expose the transition from the linear limit to tire
saturation. Their worst results over all family--speed combinations are reported
in \cref{tab:experiment4a-responses}. These amplitudes validate the plant only;
Experiment 4B will independently select the final near-linear, moderate, and
strong-but-feasible maneuver levels.

The small input produces virtually identical linear and nonlinear trajectories.
The moderate input introduces a visible but controlled departure. The strongest
case drives at least one axle close to its friction limit and therefore separates
sharply from the unbounded linear-tire prediction, especially at high speed. All
27 nonlinear family--speed--amplitude simulations remain finite. These behaviors,
together with the tire curves, are summarized in
\cref{fig:experiment4a-validation}.

Integration was audited for the nominal $20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $6^\circ$
case using RK4 steps from $40$ to $5\,\mathrm{ms}$ against a $1.25\,\mathrm{ms}$
reference. The relative trajectory error decreases by approximately a factor of
16 whenever the step is halved. At the retained $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ step it is
$7.10\times10^{-7}\%$, well below the predeclared $0.1\%$ gate.

Experiment 4A therefore \textbf{passes}. The nonlinear equations recover the
validated linear plant locally, provide bounded and physically interpretable tire
forces, and are numerically converged at the existing sample time. Experiment 4B
may proceed without changing the Phase-0 family definitions.
\subsection{Experiment 4B: maneuver-severity calibration}
\label{sec:experiment4b}
\paragraph{Objective.}
Define reproducible near-linear, moderately nonlinear, and strongly nonlinear but
feasible operating levels.

\paragraph{Setup and planned evidence.}
Scale the lateral reference by
$\gamma_m\in\{0.5,1.0,1.5\}$. Report peak slip, lateral acceleration, tire-force
utilization, steering limits, and the fraction of time outside a predeclared
near-linear tire region. Adjust the absolute base maneuver once, globally, if the
three intended regimes are not obtained.

The test uses the unseen smooth S-curve from the Phase-3 generalization case and
the M4 family-specific scheduled controller without retuning. Two continuously
varying speed profiles are applied to every family center:
$12\rightarrow28\rightarrow16\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and
$28\rightarrow16\rightarrow24\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The tire is declared
near-linear while its local slope retains at least $95\%$ of the zero-slip
cornering stiffness. Because
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial F_{y,j}}{\partial \alpha_j}/C_j
 =\operatorname{sech}^2(\alpha_j/\alpha_{\mathrm{sat},j}),
\end{equation}
this definition is independent of axle stiffness and load scaling.

\paragraph{Decision gate.}
Severity levels must represent distinct regimes without making the strongest case
physically meaningless or infeasible for every linear controller.

\paragraph{Results, interpretation, and decision.}
An initial coefficient of $8.0\,\mathrm{deg\,s^{-1}}$ did not quite reach the
strong-regime utilization gate. In accordance with the protocol, the coefficient
was adjusted once, globally, to $8.5\,\mathrm{deg\,s^{-1}}$ and then frozen. Due
to the asymmetric S-curve shape, the resulting unscaled reference peak is
$9.735\,\mathrm{deg\,s^{-1}}$. The calibrated worst-case results across both
speed profiles and all family centers are given in
\cref{tab:experiment4b-calibration}.

largest trajectory fraction for which at least one tire has lost more than
$5\%$ of its zero-slip slope.}

The maximum steering magnitudes are $1.205^\circ$, $2.428^\circ$, and
$3.692^\circ$, respectively. Thus even the strongest tier remains below the
predeclared $12^\circ$ steering limit and the $0.9g$ lateral-acceleration limit.
All 18 family--profile--severity simulations remain finite. Maximum utilization
increases monotonically through the declared bands: at most $0.35$ for the
near-linear tier, between $0.35$ and $0.75$ for the moderate tier, and between
$0.75$ and $0.98$ for the strong tier. The largest near-linear outside fraction
is $7.75\%$, below its $10\%$ gate. The trajectories and regime separation are
shown in \cref{fig:experiment4b-calibration}.

Experiment 4B therefore \textbf{passes}. These three reference scales are now
frozen for Experiments 4C and 4D. They are calibrated excitation regimes, not
claims that the entire strong trajectory remains inside the linear tire region.

\subsection{Experiment 4C: oracle models under nonlinear mismatch}
\label{sec:experiment4c}
\paragraph{Objective.}
Repeat M1--M5 using nonlinear-plant trajectories while retaining linear LTI/LPV
identified models.

\paragraph{Predeclared expectation.}
Absolute errors grow with maneuver severity, but the complementary benefits of
scheduling and specialization remain under moderate nonlinear excitation.

\paragraph{Setup and decision gate.}
Evaluate one-step and free-run error versus speed, family, and severity. The M4
advantage must survive at least the moderate regime and unseen speed--maneuver
combinations. If all linear LPV models fail, revise the representation before FL.

\paragraph{Results, interpretation, and decision.}
The same 30-client fleet from seed 1 is simulated with the validated nonlinear
plant. Training data combine a lane change under
$12\rightarrow20\rightarrow15\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and a sinusoidal maneuver
under $20\rightarrow28\rightarrow18\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Each maneuver is
run at all three frozen 4B severities. The M4 controller generates bounded
closed-loop excitation, but only the resulting $(x_k,u_k,v_k)$ samples and oracle
family labels are used for identification.

M1--M5 are refitted directly by least squares on these nonlinear trajectories.
M1 and M2 use constant matrices, M3 and M4 use the reciprocal-speed basis, and
M5 uses five family-specific nearest-speed models at
$\{12,16,20,24,28\}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The held-out test combines the unseen
$28\rightarrow16\rightarrow24\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ profile with the unseen
S-curve. One-step predictions are reset to the measured state at every sample.
Free-run predictions start at the true initial state and replay only the recorded
steering and speed sequences. Thus the latter expose accumulated model mismatch
without conflating it with controller redesign.

For comparability between $\beta$ and $r$, normalized RMSE divides each state
error by that trajectory's state standard deviation, with small fixed floors for
nearly unexcited signals. The moderate-severity results are summarized in
\cref{tab:experiment4c-moderate}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering

30 clients. The final column is the largest family-mean free-run NRMSE.}
\label{tab:experiment4c-moderate}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Method & One-step NRMSE & Free-run NRMSE & Worst family\\
\midrule
M1 global LTI       & 0.01418 & 1.0093 & 1.5173\\
M2 clustered LTI    & 0.01289 & 0.7305 & 0.7806\\
M3 global LPV       & 0.00699 & 0.5540 & 0.7549\\

\end{tabular}

Against M3, M4 reduces moderate one-step NRMSE by $59.9\%$ and free-run NRMSE
by $79.8\%$. Against M2, the reductions are $78.2\%$ and $84.6\%$. M4 also
outperforms the 15-model M5 representation. Its worst family mean is less than
one fifth of M3's in free run. This supports the intended complementarity:
continuous scheduling removes operating-point error, while family specialization
removes persistent structural bias.

The normalized M4 errors remain low at all three severities. Nevertheless, the
absolute yaw-rate mismatch grows monotonically, as physically expected. M4's
one-step yaw RMSE is $0.0054$, $0.0108$, and
$0.0184\,\mathrm{deg\,s^{-1}}$ from near-linear to strong excitation. Its
free-run yaw RMSE similarly rises from $0.136$ through $0.266$ to
$0.388\,\mathrm{deg\,s^{-1}}$. The nonlinear mismatch is therefore visible,
but does not erase the architecture ranking. The full severity trends,
representative free-run trace, and worst-family comparison appear in
\cref{fig:experiment4c-models}.

Experiment 4C therefore \textbf{passes}. The benefit of jointly representing
speed scheduling and structural families survives moderate and strong tanh-tire
nonlinearity on an unseen speed--maneuver combination. Experiment 4D may now test
controller transfer and nonlinear-data redesign without revising the compact
model representation.

\subsection{Experiment 4D: controller transfer and redesign}
\label{sec:experiment4d}
\paragraph{Objective.}
Separate robustness of the Phase-3 controller from the achievable performance of
controllers redesigned using LPV approximations identified on nonlinear data.

\paragraph{Setup.}
The transfer test applies Phase-3 controllers unchanged. The redesign test refits
the compact models on nonlinear trajectories and synthesizes controllers with the
same common weights. The redesign test is primary; transfer is diagnostic.

\paragraph{Decision gate.}
The qualitative M4 benefit should survive moderate excitation. If it survives only
near the linear region, the claim and operating envelope are restricted
explicitly. Frozen-grid checks are necessary but are not presented as a formal LPV
stability certificate.

\paragraph{Results, interpretation, and decision.}
The transfer and redesign controllers are evaluated on all 30 clients using the
same held-out $28\rightarrow16\rightarrow24\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ S-curve and
the three frozen severity scales. No controller receives method-specific tuning.
The original Phase-3 controllers retain their $10$--$30\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$
design grid. Redesigned controllers are synthesized only on the
$12$--$28\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ identification support. An initial redesign
attempt that extrapolated the identified LPV models to $10$ and
$30\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ was rejected because the global LPV controller became
unstable. Restricting synthesis to observed support is a common methodological
correction, applied identically to M3 and M4, rather than controller tuning.

The moderate-severity tracking results are shown in
\cref{tab:experiment4d-moderate}. Feasibility requires finite trajectories,
peak steering below $12^\circ$, and peak lateral acceleration below $0.9g$.

The transfer diagnostic is positive. Unchanged M4 reduces moderate tracking
error by $12.1\%$ relative to M3 and $8.0\%$ relative to M2. All transferred
controllers remain feasible through strong excitation. This shows that the
Phase-3 clustered scheduled controller is robust to the tested tire nonlinearity.

The primary redesign gate, however, \textbf{fails}. Redesigned M4 remains
feasible and improves on redesigned M3 by $87.0\%$, but its moderate tracking
error is $52.7\%$ higher than redesigned M2. Redesigned M3 is infeasible for all
moderate test clients. M5 remains amplitude-feasible, but nearest-grid switching
produces steering-rate peaks above $800\,\mathrm{deg\,s^{-1}}$, which further
weakens it as a practical alternative.

A frozen small-signal audit over every client and 161 speeds from $12$ to
$28\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ explains why prediction quality from Experiment 4C is
not sufficient for controller synthesis. All transferred designs have worst
spectral radius below $0.967$. For the redesigned controllers, the worst radii
are $0.967$, $0.960$, $5.888$, $1.032$, and $19.869$ for M1--M5. Thus the
unconstrained trajectory regressions can fit one-step and replay behavior well
while perturbing control-relevant matrix directions enough to destroy robust
closed-loop stability. M4 happens to remain bounded on the tested maneuver, but
its frozen-grid radius above one prevents treating the redesign as acceptable.
The tracking, severity, and family effects are summarized in
\cref{fig:experiment4d-controllers}.

Experiment 4D therefore records a \textbf{mixed result and a failed primary
gate}. The representation result survives in 4C, and the original M4 controller
transfers robustly, but direct certainty-equivalent redesign from unconstrained
nonlinear-data regression is not justified. Before advancing the controller
claim, a corrective Experiment 4D-R should add control-oriented regularization
or robust/stability-constrained synthesis and repeat the same frozen evaluation.

\subsection{Experiment 4D-R: stability-screened physics regularization}
\label{sec:experiment4dr}
\paragraph{Objective.}
Test whether a minimal, predeclared control-oriented correction can stabilize
nonlinear-data redesign while preserving useful adaptation.

\paragraph{Method and selection rule.}
For every architecture, blend the nonlinear-data model from 4C with its validated
Phase-3 physics model,
\begin{equation}
 [A_\lambda(v)\ B_\lambda(v)]
 =(1-\lambda)[A_{\mathrm{data}}(v)\ B_{\mathrm{data}}(v)]
 +\lambda[A_{\mathrm{phys}}(v)\ B_{\mathrm{phys}}(v)].
\end{equation}
The candidate set is
$\lambda\in\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$. One common $\lambda$ is used for all
methods. The selected value is the smallest candidate for which every M1--M5
controller has worst frozen small-signal spectral radius at most $0.99$ over all
training clients and 161 speeds in $[12,28]\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. No held-out
tracking result enters selection. This is an oracle diagnostic: the final
federated method would require uncertainty sets or another deployable robustness
screen in place of known training plants.

\paragraph{Decision gate.}
M4 must use $\lambda<1$, remain feasible for every moderate test client, and
improve moderate tracking RMSE over both regularized M2 and M3 by at least $5\%$.

\paragraph{Results, interpretation, and decision.}
At $\lambda=0$, M3, M4, and M5 have worst radii $5.888$, $1.032$, and $19.869$.
At $\lambda=0.25$, M4 and M5 pass, but M3 remains unstable with radius $1.484$.
The common selection rule therefore chooses $\lambda=0.5$. The resulting worst
radii are $0.957$, $0.962$, $0.967$, $0.963$, and $0.961$ for M1--M5. The
regularization thus repairs the frozen-grid stability failure for every method.

Held-out moderate results are given in \cref{tab:experiment4dr-results}. All
methods are amplitude-feasible, and the M5 steering-rate peak falls from more
than $800$ to $24.5\,\mathrm{deg\,s^{-1}}$.

Experiment 4D-R therefore \textbf{fails the full performance gate}, despite
passing the stability and feasibility gates. This rules out unconstrained matrix
regression followed by simple post-hoc blending as the main controller-redesign
method. The project now has a clean choice: retain the successful physics-model
controller transfer as the Phase-4 robustness claim, or add a separate
structure-preserving identification study before claiming data-driven redesign.

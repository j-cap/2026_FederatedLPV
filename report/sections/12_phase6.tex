\section{Phase 6: two-dimensional regime map}
\label{sec:phase6}

\subsection{Experiment 6A: scheduling--heterogeneity sweep}
\label{sec:experiment6a}
\paragraph{Objective.}
Determine when each architecture is warranted rather than claiming that M4 is
universally best.

\paragraph{Setup.}
Scale structural separation using
\begin{equation}
 \vartheta_c(\gamma_h)=\vartheta_0+\gamma_h(\vartheta_c-\vartheta_0),
 \qquad \gamma_h\in\{0,0.5,1,1.5\},
\end{equation}
and use speed envelopes
$[18,22]$, $[15,25]$, and $[10,30]\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Compare M1--M4 in every
cell using model error, closed-loop performance, worst-family result, and
complexity.

\paragraph{Predeclared expectation.}
Global LTI is sufficient for low heterogeneity and narrow scheduling variation.
Global LPV dominates for broad scheduling variation but low heterogeneity.
Clustered LTI dominates for high heterogeneity over a narrow speed range. Clustered
LPV becomes necessary when both dimensions are large.

\paragraph{Planned evidence and decision gate.}
The main figure is a two-dimensional map of the best architecture and the relative
advantage of M4. The map must show coherent transitions rather than one method
winning due to numerical noise. Unexpected regions are investigated, not smoothed
away.

\paragraph{Implemented protocol.}
The structured identification repair from Experiments 4F--4G is refitted in
every cell. Training seeds 21--30 and test seeds 121--130 provide ten independent
fleet pairs, each with 30 training and 30 test clients. The same random draws
are reused across cells to make contrasts paired. Mass/inertia scatter remains
$\pm2.5\%$ and stiffness scatter remains $\pm5\%$, multiplicatively around the
scaled centers. Thus $\gamma_h=0$ removes systematic family separation while
retaining individual variation. Labels then partition draws from the same
distribution. Family fits may differ through finite-sample variation, but
have no population-level structural advantage.

For an envelope $[v_-,v_+]$, all previous speed profiles are transformed by
\begin{equation}
 v_{\rm new}(t)=v_-+\frac{v_{\rm old}(t)-12}{16}(v_+-v_-).
\end{equation}
The two identification profiles collectively cover each envelope. They retain
their lane-change and sinusoidal references at severity 0.5. A training-fleet
physics controller collects these data. Its family parameters are the average
physical parameter ratios of the training clients. The estimator receives only
the resulting trajectories, known geometry, and oracle family labels.
The positive three-ratio fit, two starts, residual scaling, sampling time,
and common LQI weights remain fixed. No test result selects a model or gain.

M1 and M2 freeze the global and family structured fits at
$20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. M3 and M4 interpolate gains from five equally spaced
anchors spanning the current envelope. Thus the broad-speed study does not
extrapolate the previous 12--28 identification support. As in 4F, these LTI
baselines are frozen structured models, not independently optimized constant
regressions or the training-selected single-anchor baseline of 5A.

All methods are evaluated on the three held-out reference shapes from 4G at
severities 0.5, 1, and 1.5. The former constant-22 scenario becomes constant
20.5, 21.25, and 22.5 in the three envelopes. The references and duration remain
unchanged, so envelope width also changes speed rate and lateral acceleration
demand. This is an operating-envelope experiment, not an isolated test of speed
rate. The historical frozen-speed nonlinear plant convention remains unchanged,
including omission of the longitudinal-acceleration term in the sideslip
coordinate transformation.
Peak speed rates across the chosen profiles are 0.79, 1.96, and
$3.93\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, respectively. The broad envelope therefore exceeds
the earlier illustrative $2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ target. Its interpretation is
limited to this prescribed-speed benchmark. A time-stretched trajectory check
with consistent longitudinal dynamics remains necessary for a stronger physical
claim.

\paragraph{Metrics and descriptive selection rule.}
Primary yaw-rate RMSE averages the three moderate scenarios equally within
each seed. We also retain each client/scenario/severity result, worst-family
RMSE, steering RMS, peak steering rate, and amplitude feasibility. The latter
uses the existing 12-degree steering and $\mu g$ lateral-acceleration limits,
without an actuator rate limit or clipping. A 161-point audit spans each full
envelope. Frozen spectral radii below one are a diagnostic, not a certificate
for varying-speed nonlinear stability.

Model errors use common moderate test trajectories collected by the
training-fleet physics controller. Each fitted model predicts one step using
the same RK4 convention. Separate sideslip and yaw-rate RMSE prevent a change
in closed-loop state distribution from favoring a model in this comparison.

For a descriptive regime classification, let $b$ be the empirical method with
lowest mean moderate error. A method $m$ is within tolerance when the upper
paired bootstrap bound on $J_m-1.05J_b$ is nonpositive, all its tested severities
are feasible, and its worst frozen spectral radius is below one. Bootstrap
resampling uses ten seed pairs, 10,000 draws, and seed 6101. We select the first
eligible method in the order M1, M2, M3, M4, corresponding to 1, 3, 5, and 15
stored gain vectors (each with a prefilter). Identified parameter counts are
3, 9, 3, and 9, respectively, so this order is explicitly a controller-storage
preference. Selection against an empirical best and multiple cell comparisons
are descriptive, without a simultaneous confidence guarantee.

\paragraph{Results, interpretation, and decision.}
All 120 seed/cell combinations completed, giving 129,600 closed-loop
client evaluations. All satisfy the amplitude-feasibility checks at all three
severities. The worst audited frozen spectral radius is 0.97071.
\Cref{tab:6a-regimes} reports the simplest eligible method, while
\cref{fig:6a-map} separates the two sources of benefit. The map broadly follows
the predeclared expectation without requiring M4 everywhere.

At zero separation, M4 is 0.07--0.08\% worse than M3: arbitrary family labels
provide no useful benefit. Global scheduling improves tracking by 1.29\%,
8.03\%, and 25.65\% as the envelope widens. Consequently global LTI remains
sufficient in the narrow cell and global LPV is selected in the wider cells.
This is the intended negative control for structural specialization.

With nominal separation, M4 improves on M3 by 12.44\%, 13.71\%, and 16.37\%
across the three envelopes. Its improvement over M2 is only 1.24\% in the
narrow cell, increasing to 7.79\% and 25.03\%. The paired confidence intervals
for these six mean differences exclude zero. At 1.5-times separation the
specialization benefit reaches 23.29--29.10\%, while the scheduling benefit
within family models remains approximately 1.26--24.99\%. Structural
specialization and scheduling therefore address distinguishable effects,
although their benefits need not be statistically independent or additive.

The boundary at half separation deserves care. In the broad envelope, M4's
mean reduction relative to M3 is 4.73\%. Expressed relative to the better M4,
M3's excess is approximately 4.97\%, and its confidence bound exceeds the 5\%
tolerance. Thus M4 is selected in that cell. This is a tolerance-boundary
classification, not evidence of a sharp physical transition.

\Cref{tab:6a-nominal} gives the absolute results for nominal separation and the
broad envelope. M4's worst-family RMSE is 26.4\% below M3's and 24.2\% below
M2's. Steering RMS differs by less than 0.05\% across the methods. The tracking
gain is therefore not purchased through a large increase in RMS steering,
although peak steering rate is slightly higher for M4.

The common-data prediction diagnostic is consistent with an LPV advantage:
in this cell the one-step yaw-rate RMSE is 0.04013, 0.04545, 0.02786, and
0.01173 degrees/s for M1--M4, respectively. M4 improves on M3 by 57.9\%.
However, M2's one-step yaw error exceeds M1's even though its closed-loop
tracking is better. This retained counterexample reinforces the lesson of
4D: model-prediction ranking alone does not determine controller quality.
The separate sideslip metric and all remaining cells are retained in the
prediction summary CSV.

\paragraph{Decision and limitations.}
The oracle regime study supports investing in learning persistent LPV families:
both mechanisms matter when both variations are present, and simpler methods
are sufficient in the appropriate limits. This remains evidence for the
specified structured model class, controller weights, trajectory distribution,
and prescribed-speed plant. It does not establish federation, recoverability
of unknown labels, formal nonlinear stability, or superiority over optimized
LTI freeze points and alternative controller designs. The next methodological
step is limited local scheduling coverage and imperfect local identification,
followed by testing whether aggregation and learned grouping recover the oracle
benefit. Before strengthening the vehicle-level claim, the speed-rate and
longitudinal-coordinate limitation above should receive a targeted check.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The entry point is \texttt{experiment\_6a\_regime\_map.py}, with its frozen JSON
protocol under \texttt{code/config/}. Compressed per-seed CSVs retain client
metrics, fit diagnostics, common-data prediction errors, and stability audits.
The seed summaries, paired comparisons, regime classifications, and figure
can be rebuilt with \texttt{--summarize-only}. The implementation and existing
suite pass 30 tests, including checks that nominal separation reproduces the
original fleet, collapsed centers preserve only scatter, and synthesis spans
the intended speed support.

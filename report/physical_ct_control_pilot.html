Does improved local identification benefit closed-loop tracking?

90 pilot vehicles × 6 methods × 3 speeds = 1620 cases. No model refitting or controller tuning.

Protocol and interpretation

Identical 12-s left-right yaw-rate reference at 13, 21 and 29 m/s, with nominal peak lateral demand 2.5 m/s². Common LQI weights, gain grid, steering bounds (12°, 120°/s), sideslip bound (6°) and zero initial conditions. Noise-free full-state feedback. Structured CT models are fitted around fixed-curvature cornering and are used here without adaptation.

Check synthesis/numerical failures and feasibility before mean tracking. Limiter activation alone is not infeasibility. Lateral acceleration is reported, but is not an additional feasibility gate in the inherited control benchmark. These are reused pilot vehicles, not independent confirmation.

Next decision

If structured local CT matches or improves Federated tracking without worse feasibility or effort, use it as the practical local baseline in the paper. If identification gains do not translate to tracking, quantify the absolute difference and investigate control sensitivity before changing the experiment. Friction recovery, production sideslip sensing and the need for future federation remain separate questions.

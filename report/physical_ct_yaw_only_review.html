Yaw-only identification remains strong, with a modest loss of extrapolation accuracy

All 90 local fits converge. Removing sideslip measurements raises mean unseen increment error from 1.555% to 1.996%, while held-out yaw prediction changes from 0.003081 to 0.003210°/s. Closed-loop consequences are evaluated in the separate matched-control review.

1. What was isolated

Three reused pilot fleets, 90 vehicles and 249 original speed segments. The same four-parameter physical CT model, known geometry/curvature, full local 2–3-speed blocks, bounds, four starts and optimizer settings are used. Full-state CT fits are reused; yaw-only fits receive only yaw measurements and applied steering at each known speed.

Unmeasured sideslip remains a simulated internal state. Fixing its initial value to zero is a gauge made possible by the free affine offsets; it adds no information about true initial sideslip. The recorded acquisition controller still used noisy sideslip feedback. This result is about removing sideslip from the identification objective, not a complete sideslip-sensor-free workflow.

Mean combined matrix error increases from 0.386% to 0.501% (+29.7% relatively). Identity-free increment error increases from 1.555% to 1.996% (+28.4%). Scaling states and input by the predeclared physical scales gives 1.147% versus 1.693% (+47.6%). Thus the loss is present after removing the identity and changing coordinate scaling; a single combined percentage understates its structure.

The unseen-speed linear pulse-response error for sideslip increases from 1.636% to 3.331%, while yaw-response error increases from 1.022% to 1.199%. This concerns perturbation responses of the local linear models under identical inputs and initial deviations; it is not a measured observer sideslip error. Full-state fitting has lower increment error for 62/90 vehicles and lower scaled-increment error for 70/90. Mean matrix and increment errors worsen in each of the three fleets. This is a consistent descriptive effect, not an independent confirmation or statistical equivalence test.

3. Held-out yaw prediction remains close

method measured_yaw_rmse_deg_s true_yaw_rmse_deg_s segments complete
FullStateCT 0.0302255 0.00308094 249 249
YawOnlyCT 0.0302375 0.00320996 249 249

All 249 independent validation segments are scored for both methods. Their dynamics are fixed at training estimates. Each model uses only the first 2 s of validation yaw/input data to initialize affine nuisance quantities, then predicts the remaining 18 s without measurement updates. No suffix measurements affect initialization or centering; no true sideslip is used.

Against true simulator yaw, the equal-vehicle/fleet mean increases by only 0.000129°/s (about 4.19%). Full-state fitting is better for 59/90 vehicle means. Against measured yaw, RMSE is 0.030225 versus 0.030238°/s, close to the imposed 0.03°/s measurement-noise standard deviation. Measurement noise therefore largely masks the small model-prediction difference; the evaluation-only true-yaw score is useful here.

Validation occurs at the observed identification speeds. These prediction results do not establish equally accurate prediction at unobserved speeds or unchanged closed-loop tracking. Good output prediction also does not imply equal recovery of the latent physical state or all matrix entries.

4. Identifiability, optimization and coverage

FullStateCT 90 1 55.1582 520.491 1.12498e-05

All 360 yaw-only optimization attempts converge. All 90 selected joint fits have relative sensitivity rank four at 10⁻⁸, as do the full-state fits. Across converged starts, the maximum log-parameter range is about 0.00032 for yaw-only fitting (roughly 0.032% multiplicative spread). These results give no indication that different local optimizer solutions explain the observed accuracy loss. They do not prove global uniqueness.

Median sensitivity condition number increases from 55.2 to 59.1; the maximum rises from 520.5 to 606.8. One fit in each method lies near a parameter bound. Yaw-only friction estimates span about 0.714 to 1.5, although the simulator uses 0.9. Numerical full rank does not guarantee precise parameter recovery. Absolute sensitivity information and residual dependence also matter; these condition numbers are descriptive, not calibrated uncertainty bounds.

5. Implications and matched control

Once available, see the separate matched-control review for closed-loop results. This page audits identification only.

The local CT baseline does not depend critically on directly observing sideslip in these identification conditions. Sideslip information improves physical-model accuracy, but removing it does not cause broad estimation failure or a large yaw-prediction deficit. This result alone provides no evidence of a federated advantage.

The separate matched state-feedback control diagnostic addresses the remaining control question: reuse the full-state CT rollouts and run the 270 yaw-only-model rollouts at 13/21/29 m/s. Keep the controller and true-state feedback unchanged for this comparison, so any difference comes from the learned models. Inspect limiter activity, steering-rate RMS and origin stability as well as yaw tracking; previous coverage results showed why feasibility alone is insufficient.

Only after that comparison should observer-based acquisition and evaluation be introduced. Changing geometry or fleet heterogeneity now would mix a new plant effect with the sensing change. An eventual practical workflow must remove the sideslip dependency from acquisition and runtime feedback too.

6. Audit and limits

Verified checkpoint envelopes, frozen provenance, client completeness, training-loss selection, original training input/full-measurement hash pairs, model grids, every stored model metric and eight published model and validation tables. Recomputed 1,980 model-grid points. Paired validation input/yaw hashes agree across methods; every record has 1,800 scored samples and nuisance rank three.

The raw original arrays are unavailable in this workspace. Therefore the saved yaw hashes and held-out RMSE values were not independently recomputed from raw trajectories; their completeness, pairing, provenance and aggregate arithmetic were checked. No new fitting, controller synthesis or nonlinear rollouts were performed. Frozen experiment files and original results remain unchanged.

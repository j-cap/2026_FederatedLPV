  • Local OE → fixed nominal prior: a 45.7% reduction without learning from peers. Regularization alone already solves much of the limited-data estimation problem.
  • Fixed nominal prior → Complementary: 6.99% lower mean error with a fleet-learned prior. This is the relevant additional gain over a strong local baseline; it varies across fleets.
  • SameSpeed → Complementary: 4.10% lower mean error, an absolute 0.000169 deg/s. This isolates the tested change in peer operating speeds while keeping target data, peer identities, duration, input and paired noise fixed.

The fixed nominal prior accounts arithmetically for 92.3% of the absolute Complementary improvement over Local OE; SameSpeed accounts for 95.6%. These ratios compare error reductions; they are not causal decompositions or additive contributions.

SameSpeed and Complementary both use personalized models. Their difference is peer data: eight same-speed peers versus two same-speed peers and six at the other anchor speeds. No target gains its own additional-speed training record. Changing speed also changes response amplitude, parameter sensitivity and output signal-to-noise ratio; this does not isolate coverage geometry from those effects.

These experiments answer different questions under different protocols. Their numerical errors must not be pooled into one ranking. Links below preserve the original evidence and limitations.

All present peers share the true relaxation lengths (0.4/0.7 m) and known geometry (1.2/1.6 m). That is favorable for transfer. The plant uses tanh tires with force relaxation; these are gentle fixed-speed simulated maneuvers, not a validation of general nonlinear vehicle operation. Only three development fleets were reused, with no independent confirmation in this sequence. Do not tune a preferred story against these test outcomes and then call it confirmatory.

5. What we can conclude now

Under limited local speed coverage, a fleet-learned physical prior can improve yaw-only model identification, but most of the gain over unregularized local OE is already achieved by a fixed nominal prior. Complementary peer speeds provide a small additional benefit concentrated at low target speed.

This is a useful, qualified identification result. It is not yet sufficient to support a strong claim that federated learning is necessary for, or materially improves, closed-loop vehicle control. The old group-specific LPV narrative is not established by these physical-model pilots and should not be carried over unchanged.

Consolidate and discuss the scientific scope before commissioning further experiments: either accept a narrower identification/regularization contribution with explicit limits, or make a meaningful control consequence the requirement for a control-paper direction. No new experiment or manuscript rewrite is included in this review.

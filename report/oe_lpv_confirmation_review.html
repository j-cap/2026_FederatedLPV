1. What was tested

The prespecified identification claim is confirmed on all ten fresh fleets.

Federated models achieve 2.05% mean error at recipient-unobserved speeds. The improvement holds against both primary baselines in every fleet. This supports sharing complementary speed coverage under the frozen simulated acquisition protocol.

The practical problem is incomplete local speed coverage: each vehicle observes only two or three of eleven speeds. A fixed nominal 20 m/s LTI controller collects separate training and validation maneuvers at those speeds. Local four-start output-error (OE) identification estimates A and B. Vehicles then use restricted local regression, smooth local regression, global pooling, or learned-group pooling to obtain full-envelope models.

The ten reserved seeds 1311–1320 were run after the pipeline was frozen. Each fleet contains 30 vehicles and 83 observed client-speed cells. Family labels and full-envelope reference Jacobians are evaluation-only information for primary methods. No settings were selected from these confirmation results.

4. Where sharing helps—and its cost

Federated error at observed speeds is 1.09%, compared with 0.33% for Smooth Local and 0.38% for Restricted Local. Group pooling sacrifices some vehicle-specific fit where local information is available. The gain is its prediction at speeds without local observations: 2.05%, compared with 3.94% and 20.44%, respectively.

The paper should claim improved identification across the speed envelope from incomplete local coverage. These results do not support uniform superiority at every speed or client.

7. Does the result depend on the smoothness choice?

Federated wins in 70/70 fleet-by-strength comparisons across the seven prespecified settings. The best aggregate confirmation setting is 0.01, with 3.88% error versus 3.94% at the frozen 10⁶ setting. Against that best aggregate setting, the mean paired federated improvement is 47.27%.

This is a secondary sensitivity result, not a new baseline selection or an additional confirmatory test. The primary penalty remains unchanged.

8. What the learned groups represent

Every primary fleet selects three groups. All 30 group designs have rank seven, and 29 cover all eleven grid speeds. Fleet 1312's heavy-vehicle group lacks 22–26 m/s observations but has observations on both sides; its condition number is 16.0 and its client-mean unseen error is 1.32%. Missing grid points are therefore not automatically a failure when interpolation is well supported.

Family agreement varies: adjusted Rand index (ARI) ranges from 0.518 to 1.000. Four fleets reproduce the planted families exactly. In other fleets, group membership mixes family labels. Labels are not used in fitting. This supports learned compatibility under the observed coverage, rather than a claim of universally recovering physical vehicle classes.

9. Clean-state diagnostic: a real structural limitation

Exact-state local OLS is more accurate locally: its median matrix error is 0.052%, compared with 0.247% for noisy-state OE. Yet its subsequent group regression is not uniformly better. Mean clean-state Federated unseen error is 2.67%, and it beats clean-state Smooth Local and Global in nine of ten fleets.

Fleet 1315 is the exception. Its clean-state clustering creates a five-client group observing only 10–20 m/s. That group's design has rank six instead of seven, and its mean unseen error is 38.76%. The current method permits this group; minimum group size does not guarantee adequate scheduling information. The primary OE groups for the same fleet are full rank and cover all speeds.

Do not interpret the better primary aggregate as evidence that measurement noise improves identification. These two pipelines select different partitions, and finite-amplitude approximation remains in the clean-state fits. The diagnostic exposes a grouping/coverage weakness even when local target noise is small. This negative result remains in the report and the frozen method is unchanged.

10. Why family labels alone are not an upper bound

The privileged FamilyLabels diagnostic also has two severe failures: fleet 1311 has 44.19% mean unseen error, and fleet 1318 has 23.35%. The affected nominal and handling families, respectively, have no observations at 10–14 m/s. Their full-rank design condition numbers are approximately 3,225 and 3,399. Family-specific pooling can therefore extrapolate poorly despite correct physical labels.

Learned primary groups in these fleets cover the full grid and avoid those extreme errors, although they mix some family members. This is consistent with the importance of useful aggregate coverage; it does not isolate coverage as the sole causal factor. FamilyLabels is a privileged diagnostic, not a guaranteed best-achievable model.

11. Local identification and acquisition quality

All 830 cells yield valid selected OE fits and satisfy the existing accuracy diagnostic, applied bounds and settling requirements. Local OE error has median 0.247%, p95 1.206% and maximum 8.410%. All selected fits have Jacobian rank ten. 3294/3,320 individual starts converge; the four-start procedure still selects a valid fit in every cell.

The observed maxima are sideslip 2.52°, lateral acceleration 4.92 m/s² and steering 2.65°. Rate limiting reaches the prescribed 30°/s bound. Mean validation limiter duty is 6.37%. All 830 cells still fail the historical zero-limiter criterion; the prospectively frozen amendment permits limiting because applied steering is recorded. These results establish usable simulated acquisition, not smooth ordinary driving or real-world validation.

Known applied steering is used in OE. Noise is present in measured states and feedback. Affine offsets and fitting-window initial states remain local nuisance quantities even though the complete maneuver starts at β = r = 0.

No primary Federated grid model is unstable; its largest spectral radius is 0.976467. Smooth Local has 272 unstable client–speed cases and Restricted Local has 136. Global has zero. Counts include repeated use of shared models, so they are not counts of statistically independent models.

The development boundary failure does not recur in these ten fleets. This is favorable empirical evidence at the eleven evaluated speeds, not a stability guarantee between speeds or outside the envelope. It also says nothing yet about stability or performance after controller synthesis. The rank-deficient clean-state group remains stable despite large errors, showing why spectral radius and accuracy must be inspected separately.

13. What this means for the paper

Use the confirmation results as the main evidence for OE-based federated LPV identification.

The supported contribution is accurate full-envelope identification from incomplete local coverage: shared group models improve on both a strong smooth-local baseline and global pooling under a frozen acquisition and estimation protocol. Keep the observed-speed trade-off, client-level exceptions, and the secondary rank-deficient group visible.

The next manuscript pass should replace the earlier random-perturbation/OLS acquisition story with the implemented fixed-LTI closed-loop trajectories and local OE estimator. Present the ten independent fleets as confirmation and the three earlier fleets as development. The primary comparison, adjusted intervals, speed-dependent results and compact group-coverage diagnostic are natural paper figures or tables.

If a controller-performance claim remains central, it needs a separate matched evaluation with common held-out references, sensing and actuator limits. Model-error gains alone cannot establish it. No additional estimator tuning is required to report this identification result. A coverage-aware grouping constraint is a motivated follow-up, but changing it now would define a new method requiring new independent evaluation.

14. Audit and reproducibility

Verified all twenty execution checkpoint hashes and nested provenance, every local target passed to scheduling, the sampled fleet parameters/coverage, and all 3,300 reference Jacobians reconstructed from the saved simulation parameters. Recomputed 46,200 saved method/client/speed errors and their A/B, entrywise and spectral-radius diagnostics, plus 46,200 smoothness-sensitivity errors. Reproduced fleet summaries, paired gains and the prespecified intervals.

Independent pooled augmented least-squares solves reproduce the saved Global and fixed-partition group models; largest recomputed coefficient difference is 2.32e-09. This checks regression equivalence for the same learned partition, not independent clustering agreement.

No driving trajectories, OE fits or clustering searches were rerun. Raw trajectory hashes are preserved, but raw arrays were not restored for this coefficient-level audit. Saved acquisition diagnostics were checked for bounds and settling; they were not recomputed from raw time series.

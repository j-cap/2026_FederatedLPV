Tire relaxation: local identification and closed-loop comparison

Three selected vehicles, nine reused yaw-only training records, five evaluation speeds. Exploratory pilot; no federation or population-level inference.

Questions and matched comparisons

PreviousCT2 uses the existing parameters. LocalCT2 refits all four ratios on the augmented-plant data. FixedCT4 fits two relaxation lengths with the previous CT ratios fixed. JointCT4 estimates all six parameters jointly. ExactCT2 and ExactCT4 use generating ratios; both still use yaw-only observers. The exact four-state reference is not a guaranteed optimum of nonlinear tracking.

All estimators use known zero initial states, the same training yaw and steering, and training-only start selection. All controllers use the same beta/yaw/integral cost, limits, measurement noise realization and physical plant. Augmented force states have no direct LQI penalty. Their observer covariance is a fixed design allowance. Results compare complete controller/observer designs; they do not attribute gains solely to the feedback gain.

What to decide next

First assess whether JointCT4 gives stable fits and improves held-out prediction over the refitted LocalCT2 baseline. Then compare tracking, sideslip-estimation error, steering effort, feasibility and failures against ExactCT4. Similar control results mean the added physics is not yet important for this task, even if prediction improves. Weak local identification does not by itself demonstrate that a fleet can supply useful information.

Full condition tables are saved beside the checkpoints. Source hashes, numerical environment, selected parameters, all optimizer attempts, profile results, control designs, trace hashes and previews are saved.

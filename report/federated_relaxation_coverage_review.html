Personalization helps; the additional value of fleet learning is modest

At peer-observed speeds, FedPersonal reduces mean clean-yaw prediction error by 49.5% versus LocalOE, but only 7.0% versus a fixed nominal prior. Grouping worsens error by 55.1% versus FedPersonal. These are exploratory identification results, not closed-loop gains.

1. What was actually tested?

Three previously used pilot fleets, nine vehicles each. Every vehicle learns from one 12 s yaw-only record at 13, 21 or 29 m/s. Two held-out steering probes are evaluated at seven speeds. All methods use the same six-parameter, four-state physical model. No earlier broad-speed estimates initialize these fits.

Local = the vehicle's training speed on a fresh probe. Peer-observed = the two other fleet training speeds. Intermediate = 17/25 m/s. Outside fleet = 9/33 m/s. A single common parameter vector (Global) differs fundamentally from a common learned prior with individual models (FedPersonal).

All 27 local fits and 27 nominal-prior fits converged. All 2268 comparison rows are finite and pass the saved operating checks. Exact-model prediction errors are zero. Checkpoint digests, source/configuration locks, selected starts and all saved optimization histories were checked. Case and fleet-summary CSVs agree with the checkpoints.

Six previously saved preview trajectories (36 model predictions) were replayed, with no new fitting or test conditions. Their metrics and previews agree to the audit tolerances; 0/6 match the saved full numerical trace hashes exactly. This is a subset replay, not full restoration of every ignored trajectory.

4. Why grouping is not supported by this pilot

Group numbers are arbitrary within each fleet. Handling vehicles form one group in all three fleets. Nominal and heavy vehicles mostly share a group, while the third group contains one or two vehicles trained at 13 m/s. Fleets 1302 and 1303 contain a singleton group, which supplies that vehicle no peer information through its group center.

This pattern is consistent with clusters reflecting local estimation uncertainty as well as physical differences. It does not prove a causal explanation. The grouped regularized training objective is lower, but peer-speed prediction is worse than both the single learned prior and the nominal prior. The more flexible training objective is therefore insufficient evidence for better transfer.

FedGroups improves 57/108 individual peer-speed cases versus FedPersonal, yet increases their mean error by 55.1%. The larger deteriorations dominate; a case-win count alone would hide this outcome. K=3 was fixed before running, and three groups are not established as necessary.

Selected Global fits converged in all fleets. FedPersonal reached the eight-round budget in fleets 1301/1302; its last maximum log-parameter steps were 0.00110/0.00127 against a 0.001 stopping threshold. FedGroups reached the budget in fleets 1301/1303, with last steps 0.00109/0.00453. All local updates in these selected rounds converged, and all saved alternating objectives decreased. These finite-budget estimates must not be presented as converged solutions.

All local sensitivity matrices have numerical rank six under the configured relative threshold. This is not proof of practical or global parameter identifiability: friction reaches a bound in 14/27 selected local fits. The current comparison concerns estimation/generalization under finite noisy data, not demonstrated structural nonidentifiability.

True relaxation lengths are identical across all vehicles (0.4/0.7 m). The fixed nominal prior uses 0.6/0.6 m, while learned fleet centers approach the shared true lengths. This offers a plausible source of the incremental benefit, but the experiment does not isolate it from sharing the three CT ratios. Equal true relaxation lengths favor transfer and must remain explicit.

Global's poor result establishes that one identical model is unsuitable for this fleet under this protocol. It does not establish that grouping is required: personalized models around one shared center work much better.

6. Decision and next step

Keep FedPersonal as the candidate and NominalPrior as a mandatory baseline. Do not advance FedGroups to a larger study on this evidence. The pilot establishes a useful effect of regularization; the incremental advantage of learning from peers is smaller and inconsistent across the three fleets.

First complete a separately versioned convergence continuation, with unchanged data, objective and evaluation, preserving these original checkpoints. Then, if the advantage persists, compare matched peer counts and family composition with complementary-speed versus same-speed peers. That would test whether other speeds supply the missing information, rather than merely supplying more noisy parameter estimates. A targeted sharing ablation can subsequently separate common relaxation-length learning from sharing CT ratios.

No new experiment was run during this review. No controller claim follows from these results, and the earlier prediction-to-control issue remains open. Three reused pilot fleets do not support a confirmatory population claim. The manuscript remains unchanged.
